# Language certificates — which paper, for which authority

[Language requirements](language-requirements.md) says what level each profession needs.
This file says **what document proves it, who accepts it, and what it costs** — and
disentangles the two exams that are routinely confused with each other and with everything
else.

## The two professional exams that are not language certificates

These are the most-cited exams in this repo and the least well understood. Neither can be
sat at a language school, and neither produces a CEFR certificate.

| | **Fachsprachprüfung (FSP)** | **Kenntnisprüfung (KP)** |
|---|---|---|
| Tests | **Professional language** | **Professional knowledge** |
| Held by | The relevant **Kammer** (Ärztekammer, Apothekerkammer) | The state authority / a university clinic |
| Form | Oral, roughly 60 minutes | Oral and/or practical, several hours |
| Needed when | Always, for [Approbation](../jobs/healthcare/arzt-aerztin.md) in the medical professions | Only when equivalence is **not** established, or the Gutachten finds substantial gaps |
| Failure means | Retake, usually after a waiting period | Retake; limited attempts in some states |

### What the Fachsprachprüfung actually is

A structured role-play at the Kammer, typically in three parts:

1. **Patient interview** — take a history from a simulated patient in everyday German,
   then explain a diagnosis and a plan in lay terms
2. **Handover to a colleague** — the same case in professional register, with correct
   terminology and abbreviations
3. **Documentation** — write it up, plus questions on terms and their Latin/German pairs

It is examined at **C1 in the professional register on top of a general B2**, and the
combination is the point: candidates fail it who speak fluent clinical German but cannot
explain a diagnosis to a frightened layperson, and candidates fail it who are conversational
but cannot produce correct written documentation.

**Both the [Arzt](../jobs/healthcare/arzt-aerztin.md) and
[Apotheker](../jobs/healthcare/apotheker-in.md) files name the FSP as the usual failure
point for third-country applicants**, and it is worth taking that seriously: reported pass
rates in several Kammern sit well below what candidates expect, and a failure costs months
as well as the fee.

Practicalities: fees run to several hundred euro and are set per Kammer; you generally need
a **general B2 certificate to register**; each Bundesland runs its own procedure, so the
waiting time and the retake interval depend on where you apply. A few states accept
**telc Deutsch B2·C1 Medizin** in place of the Kammer exam — a real shortcut where it
applies, and worth checking before booking anything else.

Equivalents exist beyond medicine under other names — patient-safety-relevant professions
such as nursing frequently face a **berufsbezogene Sprachprüfung** at B2 set by the state
authority rather than a Kammer.

## The general certificates

| Certificate | Issued by | Levels | Mainly accepted for | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Goethe-Zertifikat** | Goethe-Institut | A1–C2 | Visas, recognition, employers, universities (C2) | The most universally recognised; also the most expensive |
| **telc Deutsch** | telc gGmbH | A1–C2 | Visas, recognition, employers | Widely available and cheaper; **telc B2·C1 Medizin / Pflege** are the profession-specific versions |
| **ÖSD** | Österreichisches Sprachdiplom | A1–C2 | Visas, recognition | Austrian, accepted in Germany; useful where Goethe dates are full |
| **TestDaF** | TestDaF-Institut | TDN 3–5 (≈B2–C1) | **University admission** | Digital, four skills, scored per skill rather than pass/fail |
| **DSH** | The individual university | DSH-1/2/3 | **That university's admission** | Cheap, but sat at the university and not portable |
| **DTZ** | BAMF / telc | A2–B1 scaled | End of an **Integrationskurs**; Niederlassungserlaubnis | Free at the end of a funded course |

**The practical rule:** for an authority — Kammer, Landesprüfungsamt, Ausländerbehörde —
use **Goethe, telc or ÖSD**. For a **university**, use **TestDaF or DSH**. Certificates
from the first group are frequently not accepted for admission, and TestDaF is frequently
not accepted by a Kammer. People lose a semester to this.

## What each authority actually demands

| Purpose | Level | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Spouse joining you** | A1 | Waived for some nationalities and for Blue Card holders' families |
| **Skilled-worker visa** | No general requirement in law | But the **job** and the **recognition** will have one |
| **§16d(3) Anerkennungspartnerschaft** | **A2** | The lowest entry bar to skilled work — see [visa routes](visa-routes.md) |
| **Ausbildung (§16a)** | **B1–B2** | Berufsschule is taught in German and is not adapted |
| **Niederlassungserlaubnis** | **B1** | Plus the Leben-in-Deutschland test |
| **Einbürgerung** | **B1** | **C1** for the accelerated route after three years |
| **Healthcare recognition** | **B2** general, **+C1 professional** in the medical professions | State-set; see the FSP above |
| **Teaching** | **C1–C2** | The highest bar in this repo |

**Nothing here expires by its own terms** — a Goethe C1 has no printed expiry. But
authorities routinely refuse certificates older than **two or three years**, and some
Kammern insist on a recent one regardless. Sit it when you need it, not years ahead.

## Funded courses — the part most people miss

You are unlikely to have to pay full price.

- **Integrationskurs** — BAMF-funded, general language to B1 plus an orientation course.
  Entitlement or eligibility depends on your residence status.
- **Berufssprachkurse (DeuFöV, §45a AufenthG)** — the important one. **Profession-specific**
  German at B1, B2 and C1, with dedicated tracks for **medicine and care, commercial work,
  and technical trades**, plus courses aimed squarely at preparing for recognition. Funded by
  the BAMF; usually free, with at most a small co-payment, and open to employed people as
  well as jobseekers.
- **Employer-funded** — care employers recruiting internationally commonly pay for B2 and
  the FSP preparation. It is a normal thing to negotiate, not a favour.

A candidate arriving at B1 for a regulated profession should be in a Berufssprachkurs
within weeks, not paying a private school for general B2.

## English certificates — TOEFL and IELTS

They have a place in Germany, and it is a narrow one.

**Useful for:** English-taught Master's admission; international schools; corporate R&D and
software roles that run in English; and airline and air-traffic work, where **aviation
English to ICAO Level 4** is a licensing requirement in its own right.

**Not useful for:** anything an authority gates. No German regulator accepts an English
certificate in place of German, and no recognition procedure is conducted in English.

The repo contains roles where English genuinely is half the job —
[Spedition](../jobs/logistics/kaufmann-frau-spedition-logistikdienstleistung.md) says so
outright, and [software](../jobs/it/softwareentwickler-in.md) is the clearest case, with
**§19c(2)** the one visa route in this repo that imposes no German requirement at all. Even
there, German decides how far you go: the Mittelstand, the works council, and every internal
process above your team run in it.
