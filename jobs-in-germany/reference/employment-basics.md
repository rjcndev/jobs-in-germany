Rules that apply to nearly every profession in this repo and were previously scattered
across the files that happened to need them: what the contract must say, how long the
probation runs, how much notice either side owes, what the working-time and holiday laws
actually guarantee, and what protection you have against dismissal.

None of it depends on your profession. All of it applies to
[Minijobs](/jobs-in-germany/reference/minijob-und-geringfuegige-beschaeftigung/) too, which is where it is most often
ignored. [Beamte](/jobs-in-germany/reference/beamte-vs-angestellte/) are outside all of this — a different statute,
different everything.

## The contract {#the-contract}

Under the **Nachweisgesetz**, the employer must give you the essential terms **in writing**,
on paper and signed — most of them within seven days of starting, the rest within a month.
Since the 2022 reform the list explicitly includes:

pay and its components · working time · place of work · holiday · probation · **the
procedure and notice periods for termination, including the three-week deadline for
contesting one** · overtime arrangements · any training entitlement · the pension
institution, where one applies.

An employer who does not provide it is committing an administrative offence. More usefully:
the document is your evidence when something is later disputed.

**Read for these three things specifically:**

- **Which tariff agreement applies**, if any. It overrides most of the defaults below in
  your favour, and it is a one-line reference in the contract.
- **Befristung** — fixed term. Without an objective reason a fixed term is capped at two
  years, with at most three extensions inside it; with a reason (cover, project, seasonal)
  it can run longer. Academic contracts are the extreme case — see the
  [WissZeitVG](/jobs-in-germany/services/wissenschaftliche-r-mitarbeiter-in/).
- **Ausschlussfristen** — clauses requiring claims to be raised within, typically, three
  months. They are common, they are usually valid, and they quietly destroy unpaid-overtime
  claims.

## Probezeit and notice {#probezeit-and-notice}

**Probezeit** is capped at **six months**. During it either side may terminate with **two
weeks'** notice and no reason. On a fixed-term contract the probation must be proportionate
to the term.

After it, **§622 BGB** sets the statutory minimum: **four weeks to the 15th or the end of a
calendar month**. The *employer's* notice then lengthens with your tenure:

| Your tenure | Employer's notice |
|---|---|
| 2 years | 1 month, to month end |
| 5 years | 2 months |
| 8 years | 3 months |
| 10 years | 4 months |
| 12 years | 5 months |
| 15 years | 6 months |
| 20 years | **7 months** |

**The lengthening is one-directional.** Your own notice stays at four weeks unless the
contract says otherwise — and a contract may not give you a longer period than the employer
has. Tariff agreements often set different periods, in either direction.

Notice must be **in writing and signed**. Email and messaging do not terminate a German
employment contract.

## Dismissal protection {#dismissal-protection}

The **Kündigungsschutzgesetz** applies when **both** are true:

1. you have been there **more than six months**, and
2. the business has **more than ten** employees (part-timers counted pro rata).

Where it applies, a dismissal needs a reason in one of three categories — conduct
(*verhaltensbedingt*, normally requiring a prior **Abmahnung**), personal capacity
(*personenbedingt*, including long-term illness), or operational
(*betriebsbedingt*, which requires a **Sozialauswahl** ranking staff by tenure, age,
dependants and disability).

> **The three-week deadline is the single most important line in this file.**
> A **Kündigungsschutzklage** must reach the Arbeitsgericht **within three weeks** of
> receiving the dismissal. Miss it and the dismissal becomes legally effective **however
> unlawful it was.** There is no general extension and no informal grace.

Stronger protection applies during pregnancy and Mutterschutz, during Elternzeit and
Pflegezeit, to severely disabled employees (the Integrationsamt must consent first), and to
Betriebsrat members.

**Never sign an Aufhebungsvertrag in the room.** A termination agreement is voluntary, and
because it is voluntary the Agentur für Arbeit will normally impose a **Sperrzeit of up to
twelve weeks** on your unemployment benefit. Employers offer them precisely to avoid the
protections above. Take it away, get advice, and note that a severance payment often does
not cover what the Sperrzeit costs.

**On the day you learn a job is ending**, register as *arbeitsuchend* with the Agentur für
Arbeit — within three days of learning of it, or at latest three months before the end.
Late registration is itself a Sperrzeit ground.

## Working time {#working-time}

The **Arbeitszeitgesetz** is a health-and-safety statute, and it is stricter than most
arrivals expect:

- **8 hours a day maximum**, extendable to **10** only if the average stays at 8 over six
  months or 24 weeks
- **11 hours of uninterrupted rest** between shifts
- **Breaks**: 30 minutes over a six-hour day, 45 minutes over nine. Unpaid, and not
  attachable to the end of the shift
- **Sundays and public holidays are prohibited working days** — with broad exceptions for
  healthcare, emergency services, hospitality, transport and continuous processes, which
  is why so much of this repo works them anyway. Exceptions require a **replacement rest
  day**
- **Working time must be recorded.** Following the ECJ and a 2022 Bundesarbeitsgericht
  decision, employers must have a system for recording **all** hours, not only overtime.
  This is your evidence in any dispute about unpaid work

Supplements for night, Sunday and holiday work are a matter for the tariff or the contract —
the statute compels a supplement only for night work, and leaves the amount open. What the
supplements are worth, and why **§3b EStG makes most of them tax-free**, is in
[shift work and supplements](/jobs-in-germany/reference/shift-work-and-supplements/).

## Holiday and sick pay {#holiday-and-sick-pay}

**Bundesurlaubsgesetz** minimum: **24 working days on a six-day week**, i.e. **20 on the
usual five-day week**. Tariff agreements and normal contracts give **25–30**, and 30 is the
public-sector standard.

Three rules that are frequently got wrong:

- **Carry-over is the exception.** Leave lapses at 31 December unless operational or
  personal reasons justify carrying it to 31 March.
- **But it does not lapse silently.** Following the ECJ and the BAG, untaken leave only
  expires if the **employer actively told you** to take it and warned you of the deadline.
  Where they did not, it accumulates.
- **Leave keeps accruing while you are sick**, and sickness during booked leave gives the
  days back — with a medical certificate.

**Sick pay**: after four weeks' employment, **Entgeltfortzahlung at 100% of pay for up to
six weeks** per illness. Then **Krankengeld** from your Krankenkasse at roughly 70% of gross,
capped — see [health insurance](/jobs-in-germany/reference/health-insurance/). The certificate reaches the employer
electronically (**eAU**); the employer may demand one from the first day, so check the
contract rather than assuming three days.

## The Arbeitszeugnis {#the-arbeitszeugnis}

**§109 GewO** entitles you to a written reference on leaving, and a **qualifiziertes
Zeugnis** covering performance and conduct if you ask for one. Ask. Recruiters expect it,
and a missing Zeugnis is read as an answer in itself.

It must be **truthful** and **benevolently worded** at the same time — an obligation that
made the document impossible to write honestly, so the profession evolved a code instead.
Grades hide in the degree words:

| Phrase | Grade |
|---|---|
| *stets zu unserer **vollsten** Zufriedenheit* | 1 |
| *stets zu unserer **vollen** Zufriedenheit* | 2 |
| *zu unserer **vollen** Zufriedenheit* | 3 |
| *zu unserer Zufriedenheit* | 4 |
| *im Großen und Ganzen zu unserer Zufriedenheit* | 5 |

The closing formula carries as much weight as the body. A full ending **thanks** you,
**regrets** your leaving and **wishes** you well. An ending that omits the regret, or omits
the thanks, is deliberate. So is *bemühte sich* (tried), *war stets bemüht* (always tried
and never managed), and a conspicuous mention of punctuality where the substance should be.

If yours reads badly, you can demand correction — and within the Ausschlussfrist, so do not
sit on it.

## The Betriebsrat {#the-betriebsrat}

A works council may be elected in any establishment with five or more employees. It is not a
union; it is a statutory body with **co-determination rights** over working time, shift
plans, overtime, holiday scheduling, monitoring systems and individual dismissals — the
employer must hear it before any dismissal, and a dismissal issued without that is void.

Where one exists, it is usually the fastest route to fixing something. Where none exists,
employees can call a meeting to elect one, and the law protects those who do.
