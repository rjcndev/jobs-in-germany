title: "Health insurance"
---

Zero of the 45 profession files mentioned this, which is why it is here. For anyone actually
moving to Germany it matters more than several of them.

**Insurance is compulsory.** Everyone resident must be covered (§193 VVG). You cannot opt
out, and you will need proof of cover to register an address, get a residence permit, or
start a job.

The system splits in two, and **the choice between them is one of the most consequential
financial decisions you will make in Germany** — because one direction is very hard to
reverse.

## GKV vs PKV {#gkv-vs-pkv}

| | **GKV** — gesetzliche Krankenversicherung | **PKV** — private Krankenversicherung |
|---|---|---|
| Premium based on | **Your income**, up to a ceiling | **Your age and health at entry** |
| Employer pays | **Half** | Half, up to the GKV equivalent |
| Family | **Non-earning spouse and children covered free** | **Each person needs their own policy** |
| Over a lifetime | Rises with income, stops at the ceiling | Cheap when young, **rises steeply with age** |
| Going back | — | **Very difficult, effectively closed after 55** |
| Who can choose | Everyone | Only certain groups — see below |

### GKV in practice {#gkv-in-practice}

- A **base rate of 14.6%** of gross income, split equally between you and your employer,
  plus a **Zusatzbeitrag** set by each individual Krankenkasse, also split.
- **Pflegeversicherung** (long-term care) on top, with a **surcharge for the childless** and
  reductions for people with several children.
- Contributions apply only up to the **Beitragsbemessungsgrenze**, a ceiling that is
  **re-set every year** — above it you pay no more, so GKV becomes proportionally cheaper
  the more you earn.
- **Familienversicherung** is the feature people underestimate: a non-earning spouse and
  your children are insured **at no additional cost**. For a single-income household with
  children this is worth a great deal and has no PKV equivalent.
- You may switch Krankenkasse freely; benefits are largely standardised by law, so the
  differences are service, Zusatzbeitrag and optional extras.

### Who may leave GKV for PKV {#who-may-leave-gkv-for-pkv}

Not everyone. Only:

- **Employees earning above the Jahresarbeitsentgeltgrenze (JAEG)** — a threshold **higher
  than the contribution ceiling** and re-set annually. Both figures move; look up the
  current ones rather than trusting any written here.
- **The self-employed and Freiberufler** — including a
  [Meister with their own business](/jobs-in-germany/reference/meister/), a
  [pharmacy owner](/jobs-in-germany/healthcare/apotheker-in/), or a practice-owning
  [physiotherapist](/jobs-in-germany/healthcare/physiotherapeut-in/).
- **[Beamte](/jobs-in-germany/reference/beamte-vs-angestellte/)** — see below.
- Students, under their own rules.

### The one-way door {#the-one-way-door}

PKV is cheap for a healthy 30-year-old and expensive for a 60-year-old, because premiums
are priced on age and health at entry, not income. Provisions exist to dampen the rise, but
the direction is fixed.

**Returning to GKV is hard, and after 55 it is effectively closed** unless your
circumstances change fundamentally. People who go private in their thirties on a good salary
and later reduce hours, have children, or become ill have limited options.

Treat PKV as a decision for the next forty years, not for the next payslip. It suits
high, stable earners without dependants; it suits a single-income family with children
poorly.

## Beamte are the exception {#beamte-are-the-exception}

Civil servants — [police](/jobs-in-germany/public-service/polizist-in/),
[teachers](/jobs-in-germany/education/lehrer-in/),
[firefighters](/jobs-in-germany/public-service/feuerwehrmann-frau/),
[customs](/jobs-in-germany/public-service/zollbeamte-r/),
[tax officials](/jobs-in-germany/public-service/steuerbeamte-r/) — receive **Beihilfe**: the state
pays a large share of their medical costs directly, commonly 50–80%, rising with family
circumstances.

They then insure only the remaining share privately, at a fraction of a full PKV premium.
**This is a major part of why Beamte net so much more than their gross suggests**, and it is
the mechanism referred to in [Beamte vs. Angestellte](/jobs-in-germany/reference/beamte-vs-angestellte/).

Several Bundesländer now also offer a **pauschale Beihilfe**, a flat subsidy that makes
staying in GKV viable for Beamte who prefer it — a genuinely useful option for anyone with
a family or pre-existing conditions.

## Arriving from abroad {#arriving-from-abroad}

- **EU/EEA/Switzerland:** the **EHIC** covers temporary stays. Once you take up residence
  and work, you join the German system.
- **A visa or residence permit requires proof of cover.** Incoming-expat policies are
  accepted for arrival and job-seeking but are **not** a substitute for proper insurance
  once employed.
- **Employment normally means automatic GKV membership** unless you earn above the JAEG and
  actively choose otherwise. For most arrivals the default is both automatic and sensible.
- **Pre-existing conditions** are irrelevant to GKV acceptance and central to PKV pricing —
  another reason the default is often the right answer.
