title: "The Meisterbrief and the ladder above Ausbildung"
---

Referenced across the trade files — Meisterpflicht, Meisterbrief, Aufstiegs-BAföG,
Meisterprämie — and explained here rather than in each.

The short version: **for Anlage A trades the Meister is the route to owning a business**, it
sits at the same level as a Bachelor's degree, and the state pays for much of it.

## The Meisterprüfung has four parts <a id="the-meisterprüfung-has-four-parts"></a>

| Teil | Content |
|---|---|
| **I** | Fachpraxis — practical work, usually including a Meisterprüfungsprojekt |
| **II** | Fachtheorie — technical theory, calculation, norms |
| **III** | Business, commercial and legal — **common across all trades** |
| **IV** | **Berufs- und Arbeitspädagogik — the AEVO** |

Teile III and IV are the same whatever your trade, can be taken separately and in advance,
and **transfer** if you change direction.

**Teil IV is the AEVO** (Ausbildereignungsverordnung) — the qualification that licenses you
to train apprentices. That is the direct link back to [ausbildung.md](/jobs-in-germany/reference/ausbildung/): every
Azubi's trainer holds it.

## Why it matters: Anlage A {#why-it-matters-anlage-a}

Trades in **Anlage A** of the Handwerksordnung may only be operated by someone entered in
the **Handwerksrolle**, which normally means the Meisterbrief. Anlage B trades carry no such
requirement. See the [skilled-trades overview](/jobs-in-germany/skilled-trades/).

**Anlage A membership is not static.** The 2004 reform deregulated dozens of trades; in
**2020 the Meisterpflicht was restored for twelve**, including Fliesenleger, Estrichleger,
Parkettleger and Raumausstatter, with existing businesses grandfathered.

## Four ways into the Handwerksrolle without a Meisterbrief {#four-ways-into-the-handwerksrolle-without-a-meisterbrief}

Worth knowing, because the files that say "Meister required" are describing the normal case,
not the only one:

1. **Altgesellenregelung (§7b HwO)** — a Geselle with **six years' experience in the trade,
   at least four of them in a leading position**, can be authorised to run a business in most
   Anlage A trades. **Excluded** are the health-related trades (e.g. Augenoptiker,
   Hörakustiker, Orthopädietechniker, Zahntechniker). This is the most underused route in the
   whole system.
2. **Ausnahmebewilligung (§8 HwO)** — discretionary, on documented experience and hardship.
3. **EU/EEA nationals (§9 HwO)** — a simplified route under the qualifications directive.
4. **Employed Betriebsleiter** — the business employs someone who holds the qualification.

## It is funded {#it-is-funded}

- **Aufstiegs-BAföG (AFBG)** covers course and examination fees and, for full-time courses,
  a contribution to living costs. Part grant, part low-interest loan — and **passing
  cancels a large share of the loan**.
- **Meisterprämie / Meisterbonus** — a state top-up on passing. Several Bundesländer pay a
  substantial lump sum and some effectively refund the fees entirely. It is **state law, so
  it varies**; check your Bundesland.

Between the two, the Meister is one of the least self-funded qualifications available in
Germany. Trade files that mention cost as a barrier are usually describing people who did
not check.

## It ranks with a Bachelor {#it-ranks-with-a-bachelor}

The **Meister sits at level 6 of the Deutscher Qualifikationsrahmen (DQR)** — the same level
as a Bachelor's degree. Two consequences people miss:

- **It grants university entrance.** A Meisterbrief qualifies you for degree study in every
  Bundesland without Abitur.
- Since the **2020 Berufsbildungsmodernisierungsgesetz**, the optional titles
  **Geprüfte/r Berufsspezialist/in** (DQR 5), **Bachelor Professional** (6) and
  **Master Professional** (7) exist alongside the traditional ones. They are contested —
  parts of the trades dislike borrowing academic language, and adoption is uneven — but they
  are legally available and increasingly seen on certificates.

## The IHK parallel ladder {#the-ihk-parallel-ladder}

Outside the Handwerk, the same structure exists without the Meisterpflicht:

| Level | Handwerk | Industrie / commercial |
|---|---|---|
| Qualified worker | Geselle/Gesellin | Facharbeiter/in, Kaufmann/-frau |
| DQR 6 | **Meister/in** | **Industriemeister/in**, **Fachwirt/in** |
| DQR 7 | Betriebswirt (HwK) | **Betriebswirt/in (IHK)**, Master Professional |

The [warehouse](/jobs-in-germany/logistics/fachkraft-fuer-lagerlogistik/),
[kitchen](/jobs-in-germany/hospitality/koch-koechin/) and
[industrial](/jobs-in-germany/industrial/) files all invoke this ladder —
Logistikmeister, Küchenmeister, Industriemeister Metall.

A third route, **staatlich geprüfte/r Techniker/in**, sits alongside at DQR 6: two years
full-time or four part-time at a Fachschule, more technically weighted than the Meister and
without the business-ownership entitlement.

## What it is worth in money {#what-it-is-worth-in-money}

Across the trade files in this repo, the Meister is consistently a step of roughly
**€700–1,200/month** as an employee — and far more than that as the gateway to ownership,
which is where Handwerk earnings actually are. See [pay.md](/jobs-in-germany/reference/pay/).
