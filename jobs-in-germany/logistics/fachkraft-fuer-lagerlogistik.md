## Two qualifications, not one {#two-qualifications-not-one}

- **Fachlagerist/in** — 2 years. Operational: receiving, storing, picking, packing.
- **Fachkraft für Lagerlogistik** — 3 years. Adds planning, process optimisation, tour
  planning, freight documentation and cost calculation.

The two-year qualification can be **topped up with the third year**. If you are choosing,
take the three-year route: the difference in ceiling is significant, because the planning
content is what leads to Schichtleitung and Lagerleitung.

## What the job involves {#what-the-job-involves}

Goods receipt and inspection, storage strategy, order picking (Kommissionierung),
packing and dispatch, inventory control, and warehouse-management-system work. Physical,
shift-based, and increasingly automated — in large distribution centres the job is
shifting from moving goods to supervising the systems that move goods.

**Staplerschein** — the forklift licence under DGUV Vorschrift 68 — is effectively
mandatory. Employers normally pay for it; the course is short. Do not pay for it yourself
before asking.

## Qualification route {#qualification-route}

- **3 years dual**, employer plus Berufsschule
- **Entry:** formally open; Hauptschulabschluss typical. **This is one of the most
  accessible qualified occupations in Germany.**
- **Paid throughout:** roughly €900–1,200/month gross
- **Afterwards:** **Logistikmeister/in (IHK)** or Fachwirt für Logistiksysteme — the
  standard route into supervision and a real pay step

## Pay {#pay}

| Stage | Gross/month |
|---|---|
| Newly qualified | €2,600 – €3,100 |
| Experienced | €3,100 – €3,700 |
| Schichtleitung | €3,600 – €4,300 |
| Logistikmeister / Lagerleitung | €4,200 – €5,500 |

Shift supplements — nights and weekends — add meaningfully, and in three-shift operations
they are a large share of take-home. Tariff coverage varies: contract logistics providers
often pay near the **Mindestlohn** (€13.90/hour from January 2026) for unqualified
warehouse work, while industrial employers under IG Metall pay substantially more for the
same tasks on their own sites.

That gap is the key economic fact of the sector: **who owns the warehouse matters more than
what you do in it.**

## Demand {#demand}

Consistently high, driven by e-commerce and contract logistics. Also the sector most
exposed to warehouse automation — which is displacing picking work while increasing demand
for people who can run automated systems. The three-year qualification sits on the right
side of that split; pure manual picking does not.

## Foreign candidates {#foreign-candidates}

1. **To work:** nothing required. Unregulated, and this is one of the easiest sectors to
   enter with limited German.
2. **For the skilled-worker visa:** IHK FOSA equivalence assessment of a foreign
   qualification. Warehouse experience without a formal qualification will not support a
   skilled-worker permit.
3. **Westbalkanregelung (§26(2) BeschV)** is widely used in this sector.
4. **Language:** **A2–B1** is often enough operationally, which makes warehouse work a
   common first job. **B2** is needed for supervision and for the Meister route. Note that
   safety instruction and hazard documentation are in German, and employers have a legal
   duty to ensure you understand them.

## Pitfalls {#pitfalls}

- **Leiharbeit (agency work) dominates entry-level warehouse hiring.** Pay and security are
  materially worse than direct employment; the Arbeitnehmerüberlassungsgesetz gives you
  equal-pay rights after a qualifying period, which agencies sometimes manage around. Ask
  who your contract is with.
- **Piece-rate and performance targets** in large distribution centres are intense and have
  been the subject of repeated disputes.
- **Physical wear** is real — back and joint injuries are the sector's occupational
  signature. Plan a route toward Meister or systems work.
- **Do not pay for your own Staplerschein** before asking the employer or the
  Bundesagentur.
