## What you actually need {#what-you-actually-need}

Three separate things, and people conflate them constantly:

1. **Driving licence** — **C1/C** for trucks, **CE** for truck and trailer, **D** for buses.
2. **Berufskraftfahrerqualifikation** — required to drive commercially, under the
   **BKrFQG**. Shown as **Schlüsselzahl 95** on the licence.
3. **Periodic training** — **35 hours every five years** to keep code 95 valid.

A licence alone does **not** let you drive commercially. This is the most common
misunderstanding among experienced foreign drivers.

## Two routes in {#two-routes-in}

**Ausbildung (3 years):** the full dual training. Licences and Grundqualifikation are
obtained within it, you are paid throughout (roughly €900–1,200/month), and you may drive
at **18** rather than 21. Ends with an IHK exam.

**Quereinstieg (career changers):** get the licence, then either the full
**Grundqualifikation** (exam-based) or the **beschleunigte Grundqualifikation** — a
140-hour course plus exam. Far faster, and the route most adults take.

**Cost matters here.** A CE licence plus Grundqualifikation bought privately runs into
several thousand euros — commonly €4,000–9,000 depending on region and starting point.
**The Bundesagentur für Arbeit frequently funds this via a Bildungsgutschein**, and many
haulage firms fund it against a commitment period. Almost nobody should be paying for this
out of pocket without first asking both.

## Pay {#pay}

| Role | Gross/month |
|---|---|
| Nahverkehr / regional distribution | €2,600 – €3,300 |
| Fernverkehr (long distance) | €3,000 – €3,800 |
| ADR / Gefahrgut, tanker, heavy haulage | €3,400 – €4,500 |
| Bus — public transport (TV-N NW, EG 5–7) | €3,215 – €4,382 |

**Spesen change the picture.** Tax-free per-diems (Verpflegungsmehraufwand) for time away
— currently €14 for a partial day and €28 for a full day — plus an overnight allowance
form a large, untaxed share of a long-distance driver's take-home. Compare offers on
*net* including Spesen, not on gross.

Public transport bus driving offers regular rosters, TV-N conditions and a pension, and on
the tariff scale it is competitive with long-distance haulage rather than behind it —
attractive to drivers leaving Fernverkehr. **There is no national TV-N.** Separate
agreements cover Bayern, Berlin and Nordrhein-Westfalen (the NRW agreement also applies in
Baden-Württemberg and Niedersachsen), and Eingruppierung varies by operator, so the figure
above is NRW-specific (verified 2026-09, table valid 01.05.2026 – 31.12.2026).

## Demand {#demand}

Among the most severe shortages in Germany. The workforce is old — a large share of active
drivers are near retirement — and replacement training has never matched the outflow.
Estimates of the annual shortfall run into the tens of thousands. Employers sponsor,
fund licences and recruit internationally.

## Foreign drivers {#foreign-drivers}

This is where it gets difficult. Read carefully.

1. **EU/EEA licences** are valid in Germany and exchangeable without retesting. Code 95 and
   periodic training carry over. This group has an easy path.
2. **Third-country licences** are the problem. Under the **FeV**, category **B** can often
   be exchanged without a test depending on the issuing country and state practice
   (Anlage 11 FeV). **Categories C/CE/D generally cannot** — you normally have to sit the
   **full German theory and practical test**, in German, after the required training.
   Decades of professional driving abroad do not exempt you.
3. **Grundqualifikation** must be obtained in the EU regardless.
4. **Medical and psychological requirements:** C/CE and D require periodic medical
   examinations, eyesight tests, and for bus categories a **psychological assessment**
   (Betriebsmedizinische und psychologische Untersuchung).
5. **Language:** **B1** is the practical floor and the theory test is a real obstacle.
   Some test formats are available in other languages — check with the local Fahrerlaubnis-
   behörde, since practice varies.
6. **Visa:** skilled-worker route requires recognised qualification; **Westbalkanregelung
   (§26(2) BeschV)** is heavily used for drivers. Non-EU drivers employed by EU hauliers
   also need a **Fahrerbescheinigung**.

## Pitfalls {#pitfalls}

- **"I have driven trucks for 20 years" does not convert.** Budget time and money for a
  full German licence acquisition if you are from a third country.
- **Never pay for a licence privately before** asking the Bundesagentur about a
  Bildungsgutschein and asking employers about funding.
- **Employer-funded licences come with tie-in clauses** (Rückzahlungsklausel) — leaving
  early means repaying. Read the terms and the sliding scale.
- **Driving and rest times** are enforced by tachograph with real fines, on the driver as
  well as the operator. The **EU Mobility Package** also bars taking the regular weekly
  rest in the cab and requires the employer to enable a return home at regular intervals.
- **Code 95 expiry** sneaks up. The 35 hours are due every five years regardless of how
  much you drive.
