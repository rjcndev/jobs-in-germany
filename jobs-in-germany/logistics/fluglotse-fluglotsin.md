## One employer, essentially {#one-employer-essentially}

Civil air navigation services in Germany are provided by the **Deutsche Flugsicherung (DFS)**, a federally owned company, with a handful of regional tower operators alongside it for smaller airports and the military service separately.

That monopoly shapes everything: one selection process, one training academy, one pay structure, and a career in which changing employer is not normally an option. It is the most closed labour market in this repo — and the trade for that is stability, funded training and pay that no other non-degree route here approaches.

## Selection is the hard part {#selection-is-the-hard-part}

The screening runs through aptitude testing — spatial reasoning, multi-tasking under load, working memory, English, and psychological assessment — in stages over months.

**The rejection rate is the profession's best-known fact.** Only a small fraction of applicants are accepted; single-digit percentages are the usual figure quoted. It is not a qualification filter, because there is no prior qualification to have. It is an **aptitude** filter, and it cannot be prepared for in the way an exam can.

Entry conditions to know before applying:

- **Abitur or Fachhochschulreife**
- **An upper age limit on the main entry route** — it is low, in the mid-twenties, and it is a genuine barrier for career changers. Check the current figure with DFS before assuming
- **Strong English and German**, tested
- **Medical fitness** to the air-traffic-controller standard, and a **security reliability check** under aviation-security law
- **Willingness to be assigned** to a tower or centre anywhere in Germany. You do not choose your unit

## Training and the licence {#training-and-the-licence}

Roughly **three years**, and **paid throughout** at a trainee salary that is comfortable by any comparison in this repo:

1. **Academy phase** — theory and simulation
2. **Unit training** — on-the-job, live traffic, under supervision, at the assigned unit
3. **Licence issue** with a rating and **unit endorsement**

Ratings determine what you control:

| Rating | Work |
|---|---|
| **Tower (ADI)** | Runway, taxiways and the immediate airspace — visual control |
| **Approach (APS)** | Sequencing arrivals and departures around an airport |
| **Area (ACS)** | En-route traffic in a control centre |

**The endorsement is unit-specific.** Like the [train driver's route certificate](/jobs-in-germany/logistics/triebfahrzeugfuehrer-in/), competence is validated for the sector you actually work, and moving means re-validating. Ratings also lapse if not exercised, and recurrent checks are continuous.

**Failure during training is common and it is not a disgrace** — unit training washes people out who passed selection. DFS generally offers alternative roles inside the organisation, but plan for the possibility.

## What the job involves {#what-the-job-involves}

Separating aircraft, in real time, with no option to pause. Radar or visual, radio and coordination with adjacent sectors, in a structured phraseology that is the safety mechanism itself.

The conditions:

- **Shift work around the clock**, including nights and holidays
- **Short position sessions with mandatory breaks** — you cannot be on position continuously, because sustained attention degrades
- **Very high workload in peaks**, near-zero in troughs
- **Absolute accountability.** Errors are investigated formally, and the professional and personal weight of that is real
- **Early retirement.** Controllers leave the operational role well before the general pension age, with a transitional arrangement bridging to it — an unusual and valuable term that the monthly figures below do not show

## Pay {#pay}

**Market estimate**, but unusually well-documented publicly because DFS pay is negotiated collectively and reported.

| Stage | Gross/month |
|---|---|
| Trainee, during the ~3 years | €1,200 – €2,000 rising through the phases |
| Newly licensed | €6,000 – €7,500 |
| Experienced, busy unit | €8,500 – €11,000 |
| Senior / supervisor roles | €11,000 – €13,000+ |

Plus shift and unit allowances, and the **transitional pension arrangement** for early retirement, which is a substantial part of the total and has no equivalent elsewhere in this repo.

Two honest notes. Pay varies with the **traffic complexity of your unit** — a major centre pays more than a quiet tower. And this is the **highest-paying route in this repo that needs no degree and no prior qualification**, which is precisely why the selection funnel is as narrow as it is.

## Demand and outlook {#demand-and-outlook}

**Recruitment is continuous, because attrition through selection and training is high** — the organisation must start many to finish few. Retirements add to it.

Longer term:

- **Remote and digital towers** are operational in Germany, centralising control of smaller airports into remote centres. This changes where the job is done rather than whether it exists, and DFS has been an early mover.
- **Single European Sky** and increasing automation raise long-horizon questions about sector numbers, though controller judgement remains the safety layer and full automation is not on a realistic timetable.
- Air traffic volumes and the airspace structure are the main drivers, and both are stable.

## Foreign-trained candidates {#foreign-trained-candidates}

**Be realistic: this is the hardest occupation in this repo for a foreign candidate to enter, and not because of recognition rules.**

1. **Licensed controllers from EU states** hold a licence issued under the same EU regulation, and it is transferable in principle — but a **German unit endorsement** and validation are required, and recruitment of already-licensed controllers from abroad is limited.
2. **Third-country licences** have no direct conversion route.
3. **Language is the hard gate, and it is double.** **English to ICAO Level 4 or above** for the international traffic, and **German at effectively native level** — VFR and domestic traffic is worked in German, and phraseology under load leaves no room for hesitation.
4. **The age limit** on the main entry route excludes most career changers.
5. **Security clearance** under aviation-security law is required, and it involves checks that are more straightforward for long-term residents.
6. **Visa:** no practical skilled-worker route into the training. EU/EEA right to work, or long-term residence, is the realistic starting position.

## Pitfalls {#pitfalls}

- **Apply early.** The age limit is the constraint that removes most interested people, and nothing about it is negotiable.
- **You cannot revise your way in.** Aptitude testing is the filter; practice helps with format, not with capacity.
- **You do not choose your unit**, and unit assignment determines your city, your traffic and part of your pay.
- **Washing out in unit training happens to capable people.** Have a plan.
- **The shift pattern is the long-term cost.** The pay and the early retirement exist because of it, not in spite of it.
