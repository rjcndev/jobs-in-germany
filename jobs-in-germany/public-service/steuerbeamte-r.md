## Zoll and Finanzamt are different employers {#zoll-and-finanzamt-are-different-employers}

A distinction that confuses almost everyone, including Germans:

| | [**Zoll**](/jobs-in-germany/public-service/zollbeamte-r/) | **Finanzamt** |
|---|---|---|
| Employer | **Federal** — Bundeszollverwaltung | **A Bundesland** — Landesfinanzverwaltung |
| Collects | Customs duties, excise (energy, tobacco, alcohol), Kfz-Steuer | Income, corporation, trade and value-added tax |
| Also does | Undeclared-work enforcement, smuggling investigation | Assessment, audit, tax investigation, enforcement |
| Pay scale | One national Bundesbesoldung | **Sixteen state Besoldungen** |

So the Zoll is the exception among the public-service careers here, and tax administration is back to the familiar **sixteen-system** pattern: you apply to one Bundesland, are trained by it, and are paid on its scale. Choose it deliberately.

## Routes in {#routes-in}

Both are **paid from the first day** as a Beamter auf Widerruf.

**Gehobener Dienst (A 9 – A 13)** — the main route:
- Entry: Abitur or Fachhochschulreife
- A **three-year dual Bachelor**, the **Diplom-Finanzwirt/in (FH)**, at the state's Fachhochschule für Finanzen, alternating theory blocks with placements in a Finanzamt
- Graduate as **Steuerinspektor/in**

**Mittlerer Dienst (A 6 – A 9)** — a two-year Vorbereitungsdienst on a mittlerer Schulabschluss, being scaled back in several Länder. **Höherer Dienst** requires a law degree and a Referendariat.

**No fitness test, no medical beyond the standard amtsärztliche Untersuchung, no shifts.** Compared with [police](/jobs-in-germany/public-service/polizist-in/) and [fire](/jobs-in-germany/public-service/feuerwehrmann-frau/), the selection is an aptitude test and an interview. For anyone drawn to Beamtenstatus but not to operational work, this is the accessible door.

## What the job involves {#what-the-job-involves}

| Area | Work |
|---|---|
| **Veranlagung** | Assessing returns — where most people start |
| **Betriebsprüfung** | Auditing businesses on site. The prestige track, and the one with real negotiation |
| **Steuerfahndung** | Tax investigation — criminal work, search powers, close to police practice |
| **Vollstreckung** | Enforcement and collection |
| **Rechtsbehelfsstelle** | Appeals against assessments |
| Umsatzsteuer-Sonderprüfung | VAT special audits, including fraud patterns |

## The exit route that makes this career unusual {#the-exit-route-that-makes-this-career-unusual}

Most Beamten careers are a closed world. This one has a door out that is worth knowing about before you go in.

**§36 StBerG** lets officials of the gehobener Dienst with sufficient years in the Finanzverwaltung sit the **Steuerberaterprüfung without a degree** — the qualification that, per the [Steuerfachangestellte file](/jobs-in-germany/commercial/steuerfachangestellte-r/), unlocks €5,500–€8,000 a month employed and considerably more in a practice.

So the career can run: paid Bachelor → Finanzamt → audit experience → Steuerberater. A significant number of German Steuerberater came exactly that way, and their Finanzamt experience is commercially valuable precisely because they know how the other side works.

Note the mirror image: inside the Finanzamt you **apply** tax law; §3 StBerG means you may not **advise** on it. The two files describe the same subject from opposite sides of the table.

## Pay {#pay}

**Landesbesoldung — state law. NRW figures, valid 01.04.2026 – 28.02.2027**, verified 2026-09. They do **not** transfer between Bundesländer:

| Stage | Grade | Gross/month |
|---|---|---|
| Finanzanwärter/in (during training) | AW A 9 | **€1,616** |
| Steuersekretär/in (mittlerer Dienst) | A 6 – A 8 | €3,128 – €4,100 |
| Steuerinspektor/in (gehobener Dienst) | A 9 – A 10 | €3,438 – €4,784 |
| Steueroberinspektor/in, Steueramtsrat/-rätin | A 11 – A 12 | €4,048 – €5,771 |
| Senior posts | A 13 | €5,221 – €6,381 |

No shift or night supplements, unlike the operational Beamten careers — so the figures are closer to the whole story here than they are for [police](/jobs-in-germany/public-service/polizist-in/) or [firefighters](/jobs-in-germany/public-service/feuerwehrmann-frau/). Beamte gross is still **not comparable** to an employee's: see [Beamte vs. Angestellte](/jobs-in-germany/reference/beamte-vs-angestellte/).

## Demand {#demand}

Strong. A retirement wave is passing through the Finanzämter, and understaffing in **Betriebsprüfung** specifically is a recurring political subject, since audit capacity determines how much tax is actually collected. Every Bundesland recruits annually into the gehobener Dienst.

## For non-German candidates {#for-non-german-candidates}

1. **Citizenship:** §7 BeamtStG — German, EU, EEA or Swiss. Practice varies by Bundesland for posts exercising state authority; check the specific state.
2. **No recognition procedure.** A foreign tax or accounting qualification is not assessed — you enter the German training from the start. As the [Steuerfachangestellte file](/jobs-in-germany/commercial/steuerfachangestellte-r/) notes, foreign accounting knowledge transfers poorly anyway, because German tax law *is* the subject.
3. **Language: C1.** The training is a law degree in substance, assessments are binding legal acts, and appeals turn on written reasoning.
4. **Choose the Bundesland first** — it is your employer, your training and your pay.

## Pitfalls {#pitfalls}

- **Zoll ≠ Finanzamt.** Different employers, different applications, different pay systems.
- **The training is genuinely academic.** Tax law, not bookkeeping; people underestimate it and fail.
- **Promotion depends on Beförderungsstellen being available**, not only on performance — a common frustration across the Beamten careers.
- **Veranlagung is where you start**, and it is not the audit or investigation work that attracted most applicants. Expect several years first.
