Professions entered as a **Beamter/Beamtin** rather than an employee. Read
[Beamte vs. Angestellte](/jobs-in-germany/reference/beamte-vs-angestellte/) first — Besoldung instead
of tariff, Ruhegehalt instead of a pension, no right to strike, and an
**EU-citizenship requirement** that is a hard stop for many readers of this repo.

[Lehrer/in](/jobs-in-germany/education/lehrer-in/) is filed under education but belongs to the same
system.

## How you get in: the Anwärterdienst <a id="how-you-get-in-the-anwärterdienst"></a>

You do not apply for a Beamten job and start work. You apply for a **Laufbahn** and are
appointed as a **Beamter auf Widerruf** for a training period — the **Vorbereitungsdienst**
— during which you are paid **Anwärterbezüge**, well below the eventual salary.

| Laufbahn | Grades | Entry | Training |
|---|---|---|---|
| mittlerer Dienst | A 6 – A 9 | Mittlerer Schulabschluss | 2 years |
| **gehobener Dienst** | **A 9 – A 13** | **Abitur / Fachhochschulreife** | **3-year Bachelor at a state Hochschule, as a paid Anwärter** |
| höherer Dienst | A 13 – A 16 | Master / Staatsexamen | Referendariat |

The gehobener Dienst route is now standard in policing and much of administration: **you are
paid to take a degree**, and you are a civil servant from the first day of it.

## The gates everyone meets {#the-gates-everyone-meets}

- **Citizenship** — §7 BeamtStG restricts appointment to German, EU, EEA or Swiss nationals,
  and for duties involving state authority several Länder require **German citizenship
  specifically**. Police is the sharpest case; see the profile.
- **Age limits** at appointment, set per Bundesland, commonly in the 30s to low 40s.
- **Amtsärztliche Untersuchung** — a medical assessment of your likely fitness to serve
  until retirement. It refuses people, and it is a frequent and under-anticipated reason for
  rejection.
- **Sicherheitsüberprüfung** and a clean record.
- **Beamter auf Probe** — usually three years — before **auf Lebenszeit**.

## Besoldung is state law {#besoldung-is-state-law}

Since the 2006 Föderalismusreform each Bundesland sets its own pay. Figures in these files
are labelled with the state they come from and **do not transfer**. See
[reference/pay.md](/jobs-in-germany/reference/pay/#reliability-of-these-figures).
