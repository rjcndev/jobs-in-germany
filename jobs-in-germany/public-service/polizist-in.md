> by being paid to take a degree — if you clear the citizenship, medical and fitness gates.

## Eighteen separate employers {#eighteen-separate-employers}

There is no German police service. There are **sixteen Landespolizeien**, each under its own
state Polizeigesetz with its own recruitment, training, ranks and **Besoldung**, plus:

- **Bundespolizei** — borders, railways, airports, federal protection
- **Bundeskriminalamt (BKA)** — federal criminal investigation

You apply to one. Moving between them later is possible but is a transfer between employers,
not an internal move, and pay differs because **Besoldung is state law**.

This is the same sixteen-system fragmentation as [Lehrer](/jobs-in-germany/education/lehrer-in/) and
[Erzieher](/jobs-in-germany/education/erzieher-in/) — choose the Bundesland deliberately, and early.

## Citizenship — read before anything else {#citizenship--read-before-anything-else}

**§7 BeamtStG** limits appointment to German, EU, EEA and Swiss nationals. But policing
involves **hoheitliche Befugnisse** — the exercise of state authority — and the law permits
restricting such posts to Germans. In practice:

- **Bundespolizei and BKA require German citizenship.**
- **Länder vary.** Several admit EU nationals; several require German citizenship. Some have
  changed position in recent years.

**Check the specific force before investing anything in an application.** For many
non-German readers of this repo, naturalisation is the practical precondition, and that is a
years-long path in itself, not a formality.

## The routes in {#the-routes-in}

**Gehobener Dienst** is now the standard and in several states the only entry:

- Apply to one force; selection runs over months
- Appointed **Polizeikommissaranwärter/in** — a Beamter auf Widerruf from day one
- **Three-year Bachelor** at the state's police Hochschule, combining academic study with
  practical placements, **paid throughout** as Anwärterbezüge
- Graduate as **Polizeikommissar/in** (A 9), then Beamter auf Probe before tenure

**Mittlerer Dienst** (two years, A 7–A 9) is being wound down in most Länder; check whether
your target state still runs it. **Höherer Dienst** requires a Master at the Deutsche
Hochschule der Polizei in Münster and is reached by promotion, not direct entry.

Some states also employ **Wachpolizei / Angestellte** in auxiliary roles under TV-L — not
sworn officers, and not the same career.

## The selection gates {#the-selection-gates}

More applicants fail here than at any later stage:

- **Sporttest** — a physical fitness assessment, state-specific and genuinely demanding
- **Polizeidiensttauglichkeit** — a medical examination under PDV 300 covering eyesight
  (including correctable limits), hearing, BMI ranges, and a range of chronic conditions.
  **This is the most common reason for rejection.**
- **Written tests and assessment centre** — German language, logic, situational judgement
- **Sicherheitsüberprüfung**, a clean record, and checks extending to debts
- **Driving licence**, and often a minimum height in some states
- **Age limits** at appointment, set per Bundesland
- **Tattoo policies** exist and vary by state; most have relaxed, but visible tattoos still
  cause problems in some forces

## What the job involves {#what-the-job-involves}

**Schutzpolizei** — uniformed patrol, incident response, traffic, public order.
**Kriminalpolizei** — investigation, reached after years in uniform in most states.
**Bereitschaftspolizei** — deployable public-order units, where most new officers start.
Plus specialist paths: traffic, water police, aviation, cybercrime, SEK/MEK, dog handling,
forensics.

Shift work is continuous — nights, weekends, public holidays. Deployment to demonstrations,
football matches and major events is routine and often means long days away.

**Assaults on officers are a recognised and growing occupational risk**, and the
psychological load — repeated exposure to violence, death and trauma — is the profession's
under-discussed long-term problem. Ask what supervision and post-incident support a force
actually provides.

As a Beamter you have **no right to strike**.

## Pay {#pay}

**Besoldung — state law, NRW figures, valid 01.04.2026 – 28.02.2027**, verified 2026-09.
They do **not** transfer to other Bundesländer:

| Stage | Grade | Gross/month |
|---|---|---|
| Kommissaranwärter/in (during the degree) | AW A 9 | **€1,616** |
| Polizeikommissar/in, on graduation | A 9 | €3,438 – €4,295 |
| Polizeioberkommissar/in | A 10 | €3,694 – €4,784 |
| Polizeihauptkommissar/in | A 11 – A 12 | €4,048 – €5,771 |
| Senior posts | A 13 | €5,221 – €6,381 |

Plus **Erschwerniszulagen** for night, weekend and holiday duty, which are substantial in
shift service, and in some states a **Polizeizulage** — its existence, size and
pensionability differ by Bundesland and have been repeatedly changed.

Remember that Beamte gross is **not comparable** to an employee's: no pension or
unemployment contributions, Beihilfe instead of a full health premium, and Ruhegehalt at the
end. See [Beamte vs. Angestellte](/jobs-in-germany/reference/beamte-vs-angestellte/).

## Demand {#demand}

High. A retirement wave is passing through most forces and every Bundesland has been
recruiting heavily for years, with several expanding headcount. Selection standards have not
generally been lowered — the constraint is suitable applicants, not vacancies.

## For non-German candidates {#for-non-german-candidates}

1. **Citizenship first.** See above. This decides whether the rest is worth reading.
2. **No recognition procedure exists** — you do not bring a foreign police qualification and
   have it assessed. You enter the German training from the start, whatever your experience.
3. **Language: C1 at minimum, realistically native-level.** Statements, reports, court
   evidence and de-escalation under stress all happen in German, and written work is
   evidential. The selection tests examine German explicitly.
4. **Multilingualism is valued** once you are in, and several forces actively recruit
   officers with migration backgrounds — but through the ordinary route, not a separate one.

## Pitfalls {#pitfalls}

- **Assuming EU citizenship is enough.** For several forces it is not.
- **The medical.** People invest a year in applications and are refused on eyesight or a
  chronic condition. Check the state's PDV 300 criteria early.
- **Choosing the Bundesland casually** — it sets your pay, your training and your career.
- **Expecting Kripo work soon.** Investigation usually follows years in uniform.
- **The psychological load** is the career's real long-term cost, and it is not visible at
  recruitment.
