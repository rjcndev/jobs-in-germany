## The title trap {#the-title-trap}

You can be hired to do engineering work with a foreign degree and no formalities. You **may not call yourself "Ingenieur"** — on a business card, in an email signature, on LinkedIn — unless you meet your Bundesland's Ingenieurgesetz requirements. Each of the 16 states has its own law, and several require notification to or registration with the **Ingenieurkammer**. Misuse is an administrative offence, and it is occasionally enforced.

Employers know this and generally title foreign hires "Engineer", "Entwickler" or "Konstrukteur" until the paperwork is done.

Certain activities *are* genuinely restricted: **Bauvorlageberechtigung** (the right to submit building applications) and the role of **Prüfingenieur** require Kammer membership and further qualification. If your work touches structural approval, this matters a lot.

## Pay {#pay}

Much of industry is covered by **IG Metall** or **IG BCE** collective agreements, which matter more than the individual negotiation. Approximate **annual gross**, 2026:

| Stage | Annual gross |
|---|---|
| Graduate entry | €52,000 – €62,000 |
| 3–5 years | €65,000 – €80,000 |
| Senior / project lead | €80,000 – €100,000 |
| Management | €100,000+ |

Tariff-bound employers add a 13th-month payment, holiday pay, and a 35-hour week in the metal and electrical industries — worth substantially more than the headline figure suggests. Bauingenieurwesen in consultancies pays below the industrial average; automotive and semiconductor pay above it.

Public-sector engineers (Bauämter, municipal utilities) sit in **TVöD EG 11–13**, paying less than industry but with strong security and predictable hours.

## Foreign-trained candidates {#foreign-trained-candidates}

1. **To work:** nothing required. Get hired, get a visa.
2. **To use the title:** apply to the **Ingenieurkammer of your Bundesland**. They check your degree against the state's Ingenieurgesetz, usually with an **anabin/ZAB** assessment of the institution and qualification.
3. **anabin comparability** matters independently for the visa — the immigration office wants your degree rated comparable to a German one.
4. **Visa:** the **EU Blue Card** is the normal route and engineering salaries clear the threshold easily. Engineering is a recognised shortage field, which lowers the bar further.
5. **Language:** varies sharply. R&D at large corporates often runs in English; Mittelstand, construction and anything involving German norms, suppliers or authorities runs in German. **B2+** widens the market enormously.

## Pitfalls {#pitfalls}

- **Using the title before you are entitled to.** Harmless-seeming, formally an offence.
- **DIN, VDE and Eurocode norms** are the actual working language of German engineering. Foreign engineers underestimate how much of the job is norm compliance.
- **Bauingenieure especially**: without Bauvorlageberechtigung your career ceiling in construction is real. Plan the Kammer route early.
- **Tariff vs. non-tariff** employers differ by far more than base salary. Ask which agreement applies before comparing offers.
