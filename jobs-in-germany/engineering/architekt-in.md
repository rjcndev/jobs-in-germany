> Designs buildings and runs the process that gets them built and approved. Germany protects the **title** under sixteen separate state laws and reserves one **activity** — submitting a building application — to the people who hold it. A degree alone gets you neither: entry to the Architektenliste takes roughly two further years of documented practice.

## Why this file introduces more patterns than any other here {#why-this-file-introduces-more-patterns-than-any-other-here}

Four things are true of architecture that are true of almost nothing else in this repo, and together they make it the clearest worked example of how German professional regulation actually behaves.

1. **The title is protected by state law, not federal law** — the second such case after [Ingenieur/in](/jobs-in-germany/engineering/ingenieur-in/). Sixteen Architektengesetze, sixteen chambers, sixteen sets of entry conditions. Two instances is enough to treat this as a pattern rather than a curiosity: see [choosing a Bundesland](/jobs-in-germany/reference/bundeslaender/).
2. **There is a genuine reserved activity** — Bauvorlageberechtigung. Unlike Ingenieur, where the title is protected but the work is free, part of an architect's work is legally closed to non-members.
3. **A degree is not enough.** Kammer entry requires documented practice afterwards, which makes this one of the few professions here where the qualification and the licence are separated by years.
4. **Automatic EU recognition** — architecture is one of only **seven** professions with it, and the **only non-medical one**.

## The seven, and why it matters {#the-seven-and-why-it-matters}

Directive 2005/36/EC harmonises training for seven professions, so a qualification listed in its annex is recognised **automatically** in every member state — no equivalence assessment, no gap analysis, no Kenntnisprüfung. The seven are:

doctor · dentist · nurse responsible for general care · midwife · veterinary surgeon · pharmacist · **architect**

This repo already contains [Arzt](/jobs-in-germany/healthcare/arzt-aerztin/), [Apotheker](/jobs-in-germany/healthcare/apotheker-in/), [Pflegefachfrau/-mann](/jobs-in-germany/healthcare/pflegefachfrau-pflegefachmann/) and [Zahnarzt](/jobs-in-germany/healthcare/zahnarzt-zahnaerztin/), and adds [Hebamme](/jobs-in-germany/healthcare/hebamme/) — so the contrast is visible at first hand. Compare an EU-trained architect, who is listed on production of the right diploma, with an EU-trained [Physiotherapeut](/jobs-in-germany/healthcare/physiotherapeut-in/), who goes through a full individual assessment because physiotherapy is not on the list.

**For third-country qualifications none of this applies.** Automatic recognition is an internal-market mechanism, not a quality judgement.

## What the job involves {#what-the-job-involves}

German practice organises a project into the nine **Leistungsphasen** of the HOAI, and the structure shapes careers as much as fees — most architects specialise toward one end:

| LPH | | |
|---|---|---|
| 1–2 | Grundlagenermittlung, Vorplanung | Brief, feasibility |
| 3–4 | Entwurfs- and Genehmigungsplanung | The design, and **the building application** |
| 5 | **Ausführungsplanung** | Full construction drawings — the bulk of the hours |
| 6–7 | Vorbereitung and Mitwirkung bei der Vergabe | Tendering, bills of quantities |
| 8 | **Objektüberwachung** | Site supervision, defects, cost control |
| 9 | Objektbetreuung | Post-completion |

A common split is between **Entwurf** people (1–4), who design, and **Ausführung/Bauleitung** people (5–8), who make it real. The second group is more in demand and frequently better paid; the first is what most students imagine the job to be. Worth knowing before choosing a Büro.

Governing norms: the **Landesbauordnung** of the state you build in, the **GEG** for energy, DIN 276 for cost groups, DIN 277 for areas, VOB/B for construction contracts, and increasingly **BIM**, which large public clients now mandate.

## Specialisations {#specialisations}

The Kammer lists them **separately**, and they are separate registrations — not badges on one qualification:

| Fachrichtung | Scope | Bauvorlage? |
|---|---|---|
| **Architektur** | Buildings | **Yes** |
| **Innenarchitektur** | Interiors, fit-out, conversions | **Limited** — for interior alterations, state-dependent |
| **Landschaftsarchitektur** | Open space, planting, external works | Generally no |
| **Stadtplanung** | Urban design, Bauleitplanung | Generally no |

## Confusable titles {#confusable-titles}

| Title | Reality |
|---|---|
| **Bauingenieur/in** | A different qualification. Structural and civil engineering. May hold a **Bauvorlageberechtigung** in its own right in most states — sometimes a "kleine" one limited by building class |
| **Bautechniker/in** | A Techniker, not a degree. May not use either protected title |
| **Bauzeichner/in** | The [3-year Ausbildung](/jobs-in-germany/engineering/bauzeichner-in/) that produces the drawings. Frequently mistaken abroad for a junior architect |
| **Architekt im Praktikum / Absolvent** | Holds the degree, **not** the title. May not sign anything |
| **Softwarearchitekt/in** | Unaffected — the protection lives in building law, and IT usage is not read as claiming it |

## Qualification route {#qualification-route}

1. **Bachelor** in Architektur, usually 3–4 years, generally with an admission portfolio.
2. **Master**, 2 years. Most Bundesländer require a **total of at least four years of study** for Architektenliste entry, and in practice the Master is the normal path; a few states admit a four-year Bachelor with a longer practice period instead.
3. **Berufspraxis — usually two years**, documented, under an architect, and required to cover a spread of Leistungsphasen rather than two years of rendering. Several chambers also require a set number of **Fortbildung hours** during it.
4. **Eintragung in die Architektenliste** at the Kammer of the state you work in. Membership is then compulsory, as is **professional indemnity insurance** (Berufshaftpflicht).
5. **Then** you may call yourself Architekt/in and submit building applications.

Above it: Kammer roles, **Prüfsachverständiger**, energy-efficiency expert registration, Sachverständiger for defects and valuation, or partnership and practice ownership.

**Moving Bundesland means transferring your Kammer entry** — routine, but not automatic, and the same trap as the [Versorgungswerk](/jobs-in-germany/reference/versorgungswerke/) transfer that travels with it.

## Pay {#pay}

**Market estimate throughout.** There is a collective agreement for staff in architecture and engineering offices, but it binds only where an employer has joined, and most have not — so architecture is one of the least tariff-covered qualified professions in this repo.

| Stage | Gross/month |
|---|---|
| Absolvent, pre-listing ("Architekt im Praktikum") | €3,000 – €3,600 |
| Listed Architekt/in, a few years in | €3,700 – €4,600 |
| Projektleitung / Bauleitung, experienced | €4,600 – €6,000 |
| Büroleitung, associate, large-practice senior | €6,000 – €8,000 |
| Public sector (Bauamt, Landesbaubehörde) | TVöD/TV-L **EG 11–13**, and **verbeamtet** in some states |

Three things the table cannot show:

- **The public sector pays better than small practices** for the same work, with tariff certainty, a 39–40 hour week that is real, and sometimes [Verbeamtung](/jobs-in-germany/reference/beamte-vs-angestellte/). It is the most underrated employer in this field.
- **Unpaid overtime is endemic** in private Büros, and competition entries (**Wettbewerbe**) are largely unpaid work that practices treat as marketing.
- **Self-employment is the high end and the risky end.** Fee income is now negotiated (below), and personal liability for defects runs for years.

For a profession requiring five years of study plus two of practice, entry pay sits below [software](/jobs-in-germany/it/softwareentwickler-in/) and around [engineering](/jobs-in-germany/engineering/ingenieur-in/) — a gap the profession itself complains about loudly.

## The HOAI, and what the ECJ did to it {#the-hoai-and-what-the-ecj-did-to-it}

The **Honorarordnung für Architekten und Ingenieure** set fees as a function of construction cost, building complexity and Leistungsphasen — and for decades its **minimum and maximum rates were binding**. Undercutting was illegal.

In **2019 the European Court of Justice struck that down** (C-377/17): binding minimum and maximum rates breached the Services Directive. The **HOAI 2021** implemented the judgment — the fee tables survive, but as **orientation, not obligation**.

What changed in practice:

- **Fees are now negotiated**, and in a weak market they are negotiated downward. Small practices lost the floor that protected them from being undercut.
- Fee agreements must be **in writing** to be enforceable on the HOAI's terms.
- Public procurement can price on fee, which it previously could not.

No other profession in this repo has had the economics of its output rewritten by a court this recently. If you are weighing self-employment here, this is the single most important structural fact about it — and the reason older practitioners' advice about income may be out of date.

## Demand and outlook {#demand-and-outlook}

**Honestly: mixed, and currently the weakest part of this file.**

German residential construction went through a sharp contraction from 2023 as interest rates, material costs and regulatory cost collided — housing starts fell well below the political targets, projects were shelved, and architecture practices cut staff and hours. Anyone told that construction in Germany is uniformly short of people is being told about the trades, not about design offices.

Where the work is:

- **Bestand, not Neubau** — energy retrofit under the **GEG**, conversion, extension and reuse. This is now the larger share of the profession's work and is politically funded.
- **Public building** — schools, hospitals, infrastructure — which is counter-cyclical, and where the public-sector employers above are hiring.
- **Bauleitung and Ausführungsplanung** roles, consistently harder to fill than design roles.
- **BIM competence**, which large clients increasingly require.

Weak: small-practice residential new-build, and anything dependent on speculative housing.

## Foreign-trained candidates {#foreign-trained-candidates}

1. **To work as a designer in a practice — no listing required.** You may do the work, hold the job and build a career unlisted. What you may not do is **use the title** or **sign a building application**. Many foreign architects work here for years without noticing which of the two they have.
2. **EU/EEA/Swiss qualification on the Directive's annex → automatic recognition.** Present it to the Kammer of your Bundesland. Practice-period requirements may still apply where your qualification did not include them.
3. **Third-country qualification** → the **Architektenkammer** assesses equivalence individually. Expect the degree to be checked against **anabin/ZAB** first, expect a documented **practice period** in Germany, and expect building law and German construction practice to be the identified gap.
4. **Language: C1 realistically.** Building law, planning-authority correspondence, client meetings, and site instruction that becomes evidence when something goes wrong. See [language certificates](/jobs-in-germany/reference/language-certificates/).
5. **Visa:** **§18b**, with the **Blue Card (§18g)** reachable at experienced level but frequently **not at entry pay** — check the current threshold, and expect the shortage list to matter.
6. **Compulsory extras on listing:** Kammer membership fees, professional indemnity insurance, and membership of the **[Versorgungswerk](/jobs-in-germany/reference/versorgungswerke/)** — with the statutory-pension exemption to apply for within three months.

## Pitfalls {#pitfalls}

- **The title is not the job, and the job is not the title.** Decide early whether you need listing at all. If you intend to sign off buildings or practise independently, you do, and the two-year clock starts at graduation.
- **Choose the Bundesland deliberately.** Entry conditions, required practice, CPD hours and the scope of Innenarchitekten's Bauvorlage all differ.
- **Bauvorlageberechtigung is shared with Bauingenieure** in most states. Being an architect is not the only way to acquire it, and in some states it is not the easiest.
- **The unpaid-overtime culture is real** and concentrated in well-known design practices, which trade prestige against pay. Your [employment rights](/jobs-in-germany/reference/employment-basics/) apply there exactly as everywhere else.
- **Liability is personal and long.** Defect liability runs for years after completion; indemnity insurance is not optional, and it is not cheap.
- **Post-HOAI fee competition** means an older practitioner's assumptions about income may simply no longer hold.
