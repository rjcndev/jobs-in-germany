## The reserved role above it {#the-reserved-role-above-it}

This is the part that makes the trade unusual, and it is a further instance of the repo's
[activity-gating pattern](/jobs-in-germany/#the-fourth-pattern-the-gate-is-on-the-activity-not-the-title).

The **Versammlungsstättenverordnung** — the model regulation for places of assembly, enacted
as state building law in each Bundesland — requires that stage operations in venues above
defined thresholds be supervised by a **Verantwortliche Person für Veranstaltungstechnik**.
That role requires the **Meister für Veranstaltungstechnik (IHK)** or an equivalent
qualification.

So while nothing stops you working in the trade with no qualification at all, **someone in
the building must hold the Meister**, and they carry personal responsibility for whether the
show is technically safe to run. It is one of the clearest cases in this repo of a
qualification that gates a **function** rather than a job title.

The Meister has Fachrichtungen — broadly **Bühne/Studio**, **Halle** and **Beleuchtung** —
and it is the single most valuable credential in the sector.

## What the job involves {#what-the-job-involves}

| Area | Work |
|---|---|
| **Rigging** | Suspending truss, hoists, speakers and scenery **over people**. Load assumptions, chain-hoist inspection, secondary safety, ground support |
| **Bühnentechnik** | Flying systems, stage machinery, revolves, scenery movement |
| **Beleuchtung** | Lighting design implementation, moving lights, control desks, DMX networks |
| **Beschallung** | PA systems, mixing, RF coordination for radio microphones |
| **Video** | LED walls, projection, media servers, streaming |
| **Energieversorgung** | Temporary power distribution, generators, RCD protection — electrical work that brings the **DGUV V3 / Elektrofachkraft** rules with it |
| **Auf- und Abbau** | Load-in and load-out, which is most of the physical work and most of the night shifts |

The governing rules are **DGUV Vorschrift 17/18** for production and performance venues, the
state **Versammlungsstättenverordnung**, and the inspection regimes for hoists, truss and
flying systems. Rigging additionally runs on recognised **Sachkunde** qualifications for
those who plan and check suspended loads.

**The safety case is straightforward and absolute:** everything hanging is above an audience,
and almost all of it is installed under time pressure by tired people at night.

## Where the work is {#where-the-work-is}

| Employer | Character |
|---|---|
| **Public theatres and opera houses** | **TVöD or TV-L**, verifiable pay, a pension arrangement, and the most stable employment in the sector |
| **Arenas, congress centres, trade-fair venues** | In-house technical teams; steadier than touring |
| **Event service and rental companies** | The volume employer. Project-driven, long hours, seasonal peaks |
| **Touring** | Contract by contract, frequently freelance, and a life rather than a job |
| **Broadcast and studio** | Steadier, technically narrower |
| **Freelance** | Widespread, day-rate based, and the default in parts of the sector |

The theatre route is consistently underrated by people entering through the touring side: the
same skills, tariff pay, and a roster that a family can survive.

## Pay {#pay}

**Market estimate**, except the public-sector row.

| Stage | Gross/month |
|---|---|
| Entry, event service company | €2,600 – €3,100 |
| Experienced | €3,100 – €3,800 |
| Specialist — rigging, audio systems, video engineering | €3,600 – €4,500 |
| **Meister für Veranstaltungstechnik** | €4,000 – €5,200 |
| **Public theatre (TVöD/TV-L EG 6–9a)** | **€3,240 – €4,980** |
| Freelance | Day rates; highly variable and dependent on reputation |

TVöD row is the scale verified for this repo, valid **01.05.2026 – 31.03.2027**.

**Overtime, night and weekend work are the norm**, and the supplements are partly tax-free —
see [shift work and supplements](/jobs-in-germany/reference/shift-work-and-supplements/). Event work
also has specific working-time exemptions, which is exactly why knowing the
[rules](/jobs-in-germany/reference/employment-basics/) matters here: the exemptions have conditions,
including compensatory rest.

## Demand and outlook {#demand-and-outlook}

**Severe shortage, with a specific and recent cause.**

The events sector was shut down almost completely for roughly two years from 2020 —
uniquely among the occupations in this repo, the work did not slow, it stopped. Freelancers
had no income and limited access to support, employed technicians were on Kurzarbeit or let
go, and **a large share of the workforce left the sector permanently** for logistics, IT and
industrial maintenance, where the skills transfer easily.

The events came back. The people did not. The result is a persistent shortage of experienced
technicians, particularly in rigging and audio, which has pushed up day rates and made the
sector — belatedly — compete on conditions as well.

Structural direction: more LED and video, more complex rigging, more power demand, more
automation of stage movement, and continuing growth in corporate and conference work
alongside music and theatre. The skills are also unusually portable into
[industrial maintenance](/jobs-in-germany/industrial/) and film production.

## Foreign candidates {#foreign-candidates}

1. **To work: no recognition required.** Unregulated, and the sector hires on demonstrated
   competence — a touring CV travels well.
2. **For the visa: yes.** §18a needs a recognised equivalent, assessed by **IHK FOSA**.
3. **The Meister and the Sachkunde qualifications are German** and will not transfer. If you
   intend to take responsibility for suspended loads or to be the venue's competent person,
   budget for qualifying here.
4. **Electrical work brings the Elektrofachkraft rule with it** — see
   [occupational certificates](/jobs-in-germany/reference/occupational-certificates/).
5. **Language: B1–B2.** Safety briefings, load-in coordination and the inspection paperwork.
   The international touring world runs substantially in English, which is why people
   underestimate this; the German venue, its Brandschutz officer and its documentation do
   not.
6. **Visa:** §18a, or **§16a** to train here. **No Blue Card.**

## Pitfalls {#pitfalls}

- **"Freelance" is the sector's default and is frequently wrong.** If a company directs your
  work, sets your hours and provides the equipment, that is employment. See
  [Fliesenleger](/jobs-in-germany/skilled-trades/fliesenleger-in/) for the same pattern elsewhere.
- **The working-time exemptions have conditions.** Compensatory rest is not optional, and a
  sector norm is not a legal permission.
- **Never accept responsibility for rigging you are not qualified to sign off.** The
  liability is personal and the failure mode is catastrophic.
- **The public theatres are the underrated employer**, and they train people too.
- **The career has a physical half and a technical half.** Move toward the second — Meister,
  system design, project management — before the load-ins stop being possible.
