## Sixteen school systems, not one {#sixteen-school-systems-not-one}

Education is **exclusively Länder competence** (Kulturhoheit). There is no federal school
law, no federal teaching qualification and no federal curriculum. Each Bundesland has its
own school law, teacher training regulations, subject combinations, exam structure and
Besoldung.

The KMK coordinates mutual recognition between states, but **a qualification from one state
is not automatically usable in another** — transfers happen constantly and are negotiated
case by case, often with conditions. This is worse fragmentation than
[Erzieher/in](/jobs-in-germany/education/erzieher-in/), because here the *pay system* is state law too.

Choose the Bundesland deliberately and early. It determines your training, your salary,
your subject combination and whether you are made a Beamter at all.

| Gymnasium / Sekundarstufe II | Upper secondary, Abitur |
| **Berufsbildende Schulen / Berufskolleg** | Vocational schools — the deepest shortage |
| **Sonderpädagogik / Förderschule** | Special needs — also acutely short |

You qualify for **one** Lehramt. Moving between them means retraining.

## The two-subject rule — the trap for foreign teachers {#the-two-subject-rule--the-trap-for-foreign-teachers}

Almost every German Lehramt requires **two teaching subjects** (Fächerkombination), studied
to depth, plus educational science. Some combinations are restricted; some are in demand.

Foreign teachers overwhelmingly qualify in **one** subject. This is the most common reason
foreign teaching qualifications fail equivalence assessment — not language, not pedagogy,
but a structural mismatch in what the qualification contains. It is frequently fixable by
studying a second subject, which costs years.

## Route in {#route-in}

1. **Degree** — Bachelor plus **Master of Education** in most states; Bavaria and Saxony
   retain a **Staatsexamen** structure. Either way it counts as the first phase.
2. **Referendariat / Vorbereitungsdienst** — 12 to 24 months depending on state, teaching
   under supervision, paid as an **Anwärter/in**. In NRW 2026 that is **€1,760/month**
   (A 12) or **€1,793** (A 13).
3. **Zweites Staatsexamen** — the second phase exam.
4. **Einstellung** — hiring runs through state allocation procedures, often with a ranked
   list and regional assignment rather than a free job market.

**Quer- und Seiteneinstieg** — lateral entry — exists in every state for shortage subjects
(MINT, Informatik, Musik, Kunst, Sonderpädagogik, vocational fields). Terms vary wildly:
some routes lead to full Lehramtsbefähigung and Verbeamtung, others leave you permanently
angestellt. Ask precisely which, in writing, before accepting.

## Pay {#pay}

**As a Beamter** — Besoldung, and therefore **state law**. NRW 2026, verified 2026-09,
table valid **01.04.2026 – 28.02.2027**:

| Grade | Typical role | Gross/month |
|---|---|---|
| **A 12** | Grundschule / Sek I in states that have not equalised | €4,505 – €5,771 |
| **A 13** | Gymnasium, Berufskolleg, Sonderpädagogik — and increasingly all Lehrämter | €5,221 – €6,381 |
| A 14 | Senior posts, Fachleitung | €5,531 – €7,035 |
| A 15 | Schulleitung | €6,356 – €7,906 |

</div>


**"A13 für alle"** — the long campaign to pay primary teachers the same as secondary — has
succeeded in most Bundesländer over recent years. Whether it applies to you is a
state-by-state question and one of the first things to check.

**As an employee** — TV-L, all states except Hessen, valid **01.04.2026 – 28.02.2027**:


<div class="jig-table" markdown="1">

| Grade | Gross/month |
|---|---|
| E 11 | €4,178 – €6,051 |
| E 12 | €4,311 – €6,627 |
| E 13 | €4,759 – €6,765 |

The gross figures look comparable. **They are not comparable.** A Beamter on A 13 nets
substantially more than an employee on E 13 — no pension contribution, no unemployment
insurance, Beihilfe instead of full health premiums — and retires on Ruhegehalt rather than
the statutory pension. See
[beamte-vs-angestellte](/jobs-in-germany/reference/beamte-vs-angestellte/).

## Workload {#workload}

Teaching load is measured in **Pflichtstunden** (Deputat), not office hours: roughly
**25–28** lessons per week at Grundschule, **23–25** at Gymnasium, varying by state.
Preparation, marking, parent contact, conferences and documentation sit on top, within a
nominal 40–41 hour week. Correction-heavy subject pairs — German, languages — are
materially more work than the timetable shows, which is worth weighing when choosing
subjects.

## Demand {#demand}

Severe and structural, but **uneven**. Acute shortage in Grundschule, Sonderpädagogik,
MINT subjects and vocational schools, and in rural and eastern regions. Closer to balance,
occasionally oversupplied, in Gymnasium humanities in attractive cities. "Teacher shortage"
is true in aggregate and false for some specific combinations — check your Lehramt and
subjects, not the headline.

## Foreign-trained candidates {#foreign-trained-candidates}

1. **Apply to the Zeugnisanerkennungsstelle** of your target Bundesland. No national
   procedure exists.
2. **Both phases must be matched.** German qualification has two: academic study *and* a
   supervised Referendariat with its own exam. A foreign degree that included little
   supervised practice will usually be found incomplete, and the remedy is an
   **Anpassungslehrgang** or **Anpassungsqualifizierung**.
3. **The two-subject requirement** is the most common failure point — see above.
4. **Language: C1 minimum, and C2 is commonly required** for Lehramt recognition. The
   highest language bar in this repo. The reasoning is that you are modelling the language
   of instruction.
5. **Visa:** §16d AufenthG for recognition, then §18b.
6. **Verbeamtung generally requires EU/EEA/Swiss citizenship** (§7 BeamtStG). A recognised,
   fully qualified third-country teacher will typically be hired as an **Angestellte/r on
   TV-L** — lower net pay, statutory pension, no tenure — and stay there unless they
   naturalise. This is the single most consequential fact in this file, and it has nothing
   to do with competence.

## Pitfalls {#pitfalls}

- **Choosing the Bundesland late.** It sets training, subjects, pay and Verbeamtung.
  Changing later is a negotiation, not a transfer.
- **The citizenship gate on Verbeamtung** — permanent, large, and invisible in job ads.
- **Age limits** for Verbeamtung (commonly 42–47 at appointment) catch career changers,
  and the Referendariat itself consumes one to two of those years.
- **The amtsärztliche Untersuchung** can refuse appointment on health grounds even after
  you have completed everything.
- **Seiteneinstieg terms vary enormously.** Some routes dead-end as permanent Angestellte.
- **Subject combination is a lifelong workload decision**, not just an academic one.
