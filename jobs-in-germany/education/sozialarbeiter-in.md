> Anerkennung** — a separate act by the Bundesland — is what lets you use the title and hold
> the posts that carry statutory powers. It pairs with [Erzieher/in](/jobs-in-germany/education/erzieher-in/), which
> has the same two-stage structure one qualification level down.

## The two-stage structure {#the-two-stage-structure}

This is the point of the file, and it is the thing most foreign-trained applicants and most
career-changers get wrong.

| Stage | What it is |
|---|---|
| **1. The degree** | Bachelor of Arts in Soziale Arbeit, at a Hochschule für angewandte Wissenschaften. Seven semesters is typical, including a **Praxissemester** |
| **2. The staatliche Anerkennung** | A separate administrative act by the Bundesland, confirming practical competence |

**How stage 2 is obtained depends on the Bundesland**, and there are broadly two models:

- **Integrated** — the degree contains enough supervised practice that the Anerkennung is
  granted with it, automatically or on application.
- **Separate** — a **Berufsanerkennungsjahr** after graduation: a year of supervised
  practice, paid, before the Anerkennung is granted.

Which model applies, how long the practice must be, and what documentation it needs are all
**state law**. This is the third profession in this repo regulated sixteen times over, after
[Lehrer](/jobs-in-germany/education/lehrer-in/) and [Erzieher](/jobs-in-germany/education/erzieher-in/) — see
[choosing a Bundesland](/jobs-in-germany/reference/bundeslaender/), which is where that pattern is
collected.

**Why bother?** Because without it:

- you may not use the protected title
- public-sector posts grade you lower, or will not appoint you
- and a set of statutory functions is closed to you — **Jugendamt** case work under SGB VIII,
  **Bewährungshilfe**, guardianship and care proceedings, and a range of roles where the
  post exercises public authority

## What the job involves {#what-the-job-involves}

Social work in Germany is a wide field held together by law rather than by setting. The
statutes are the job: **SGB VIII** (children and young people), **SGB XII** (social
assistance), **SGB II** (basic security and employment), **SGB IX** (participation and
disability), plus family and residence law.

Common fields:

| Field | Work |
|---|---|
| **Allgemeiner Sozialer Dienst (ASD)** at the Jugendamt | Child protection assessments, family support, court applications. The most legally exposed post in the profession |
| **Erziehungshilfen** | Residential and family-based support, foster care |
| **Schulsozialarbeit** | In schools, alongside [teachers](/jobs-in-germany/education/lehrer-in/) |
| **Suchthilfe, Wohnungslosenhilfe, Migrationsberatung** | Counselling and case management, largely at charitable providers |
| **Eingliederungshilfe, Behindertenhilfe** | Participation planning under SGB IX |
| **Bewährungshilfe, Straffälligenhilfe** | Probation — **frequently a civil-service post**; see [Beamte vs. Angestellte](/jobs-in-germany/reference/beamte-vs-angestellte/) |
| **Betriebliche Sozialarbeit, Kliniksozialdienst** | In companies and hospitals — discharge planning is a large, quiet field |

Employers split roughly three ways: **municipalities** (TVöD), **free charitable providers**
— Caritas, Diakonie, AWO, Paritätischer — on AVR agreements, and a smaller private and
corporate sector.

## Confusable titles {#confusable-titles}

| Title | Reality |
|---|---|
| **[Erzieher/in](/jobs-in-germany/education/erzieher-in/)** | A **Fachschule** qualification, also with staatliche Anerkennung, one level below. Works in Kita, Hort and residential care. Not a shorter social-work degree |
| **Kindheitspädagoge/in** | A newer Bachelor aimed at early years. Overlaps with Erzieher's field at degree level |
| **Sozialarbeiter without the Anerkennung** | Holds the degree, may do much of the work for a private provider, **may not use the title** and is locked out of the statutory posts |
| **Sozialassistent/in** | A two-year school qualification, an entry tier — not a social worker |
| **Sozialpädagoge vs. Sozialarbeiter** | Historically different emphases, now largely one degree. Treat as interchangeable in job ads |

## Pay {#pay}

Municipal employers use **TVöD SuE**. The scale is the one verified for this repo, valid
**01.05.2026 – 31.03.2027** — but the specific S-groups for social work were **not
separately verified**, so treat the figures below as **indicative positions on a verified
scale**, not as exact steps.

| Stage | Group | Gross/month |
|---|---|---|
| Berufsanerkennungsjahr | Trainee rate | Low — a practice-year allowance |
| Entry, with Anerkennung | **S 11b – S 12** | €3,900 – €4,300 |
| Experienced | S 11b – S 12, higher Stufen | €4,800 – €5,500 |
| **ASD / Jugendamt case work** | **S 14** — a higher group precisely because of the child-protection responsibility | €4,300 – €5,700 |
| Team or service leadership | S 15 – S 18 | €5,000 – €6,963 |
| Charitable provider (AVR) | Comparable, provider-dependent | Usually close to TVöD |
| Private provider, no agreement | **Market estimate** | €3,200 – €4,000 |

Two things the table hides. **S 14 exists because ASD work carries the risk**: the
**Garantenstellung** in child protection means a social worker can face personal
consequences for a failure to act. And **the non-tariff private sector pays substantially
less** for work that is often harder — establish which agreement applies before comparing
offers.

## Demand and outlook {#demand-and-outlook}

**Strong, broad, and structurally underpinned by law** — the statutory entitlements these
posts deliver do not shrink with the economy, and several have expanded: the legal right to
after-school care, participation planning under SGB IX, and school social work.

- **Jugendamt ASD posts are chronically hard to fill**, because the caseloads and the
  liability are known.
- **Schulsozialarbeit has grown fastest** and is the most sought-after field, so it is the
  hardest to enter.
- Municipal budgets are the pressure point: social work is a statutory duty, but how
  generously it is staffed is a local political choice, and austerity shows up as caseload
  rather than as redundancy.

Honest counterweight: **burnout and moral injury are the profession's real occupational
hazard.** Supervision — in the German sense of funded professional reflection — is standard
in good employers and a reasonable thing to ask about in an interview.

## Foreign-trained candidates {#foreign-trained-candidates}

1. **To work: it depends what you want to do.** A charitable provider may employ you on a
   foreign social-work degree in a support role. The **title** and the **statutory posts**
   need the German staatliche Anerkennung.
2. **The degree is checked first** — comparability through **anabin/ZAB**, since this is an
   academic qualification. That is *not* the same as the Anerkennung; see
   [recognition authorities](/jobs-in-germany/reference/recognition-authorities/).
3. **Then the Bundesland decides on the Anerkennung**, usually requiring German-specific
   legal content and frequently a **supervised practice period** here. Social law is the
   identified gap almost every time, and reasonably so: SGB VIII has no foreign equivalent.
4. **Language: C1.** Case documentation is evidential, court reports are read by judges, and
   the counselling itself is the work. Below C1 the job is not doable.
5. **Visa:** **§18b**; the Blue Card is borderline — reachable at experienced or ASD grading,
   generally **not at entry**. Check the current threshold.
6. **Civil-service posts** such as Bewährungshilfe may additionally require **EU
   citizenship**, exactly as in [teaching](/jobs-in-germany/education/lehrer-in/).

## Pitfalls {#pitfalls}

- **The degree without the Anerkennung is a half-qualification.** Confirm before you enrol
  whether the programme includes it, and in which Bundesland.
- **Check the Bundesland early** — the Anerkennungsjahr is a paid year of your life in some
  states and not required in others.
- **Private-provider pay is a different world from TVöD.** The agreement name in the contract
  is the single most informative line in it.
- **ASD is not an entry-level post**, whatever the vacancy says. Go in with supervision
  arranged and your own understanding of the Garantenstellung.
- **This is a job with statutory powers over families.** That carries weight both ways, and
  the file would be dishonest not to say so.
