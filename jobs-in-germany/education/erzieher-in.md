## Why this profession is different {#why-this-profession-is-different}

Every other regulated profession in this repo rests on a **federal** law — MTBG, PflBG, BÄO, MPhG. Erzieher/in does not. It is governed by each Bundesland's own Fachschulordnung and social-professions rules, administered by state ministries.

Consequences, all of them practical:

- Training content, length and entry requirements **differ by state**
- Foreign recognition procedures differ by state, in substance, not just in which office you post the forms to
- A recognition granted in one state is usually accepted elsewhere, but **not automatically** — expect a further procedure when moving

If you are choosing where in Germany to settle, this profession is one where the choice should be made *before* starting the paperwork, not after.

## What the job involves {#what-the-job-involves}

Depending on setting: supporting early development and play, language development, observation and documentation of developmental progress, working with parents, after-school supervision, or residential and youth social work. The German framework emphasises **Bildungsauftrag** — Kitas are legally educational institutions, not childcare — and each state has its own Bildungsplan.

Settings: Kindertagesstätten, Krippen (under-3s), Horte, Jugendhilfe, Heimerziehung, integrative and special-needs settings.

## Qualification route {#qualification-route}

Two routes, and the difference is financial:

**Classic (schulisch):** 2 years full-time at a Fachschule für Sozialpädagogik, then a one-year supervised **Anerkennungsjahr** in practice. The two school years are typically **unpaid**, and private Fachschulen may charge fees. The Anerkennungsjahr pays roughly €1,700–1,950/month.

**PiA — praxisintegrierte Ausbildung:** 3 years, employed throughout, **paid from day one** at roughly €1,350–1,550/month under TVAöD. Introduced and heavily expanded to fight the shortage, and now the route most people should want.

**The hidden prerequisite:** most states require a relevant prior qualification before entry — commonly a 2-year **Sozialassistent/in** or **Kinderpfleger/in**. Realistic total training time is therefore **five years**, not three. This surprises nearly everyone.

**Degree alternative:** **Kindheitspädagoge/in (B.A.)**, 3–4 years at a Hochschule, leading to state recognition as well. Better paid at the top end, less common in ordinary Kitas.

## Pay {#pay}

Public and church employers pay under **TVöD SuE** (Sozial- und Erziehungsdienst). A qualified Erzieher/in normally enters at **S8a**; Kita leadership runs S9–S18 scaled by facility size. Gross monthly, from the table valid **01.05.2026 – 31.03.2027**, verified against the published TVöD SuE scale 2026-09:

| Stage | Gross/month |
|---|---|
| Newly qualified (S8a Stufe 1–2) | €3,509 – €3,738 |
| Experienced (S8a Stufe 4–6) | €4,207 – €4,669 |
| Kita-Leitung (S9 to S18, by facility size) | €3,649 – €6,963 |

Plus the **SuE-Zulage** (a monthly allowance), and two additional **Regenerationstage** per year won in collective bargaining. Church providers use AVR agreements at broadly comparable levels; private and parent-run providers often pay less.

## Demand {#demand}

Acute and legally driven. Two statutory entitlements create demand the system cannot meet: the **Rechtsanspruch** on a Kita place from age one, and the **Ganztagsanspruch** for primary-school children, which began phasing in with the 2026/27 school year under the Ganztagsförderungsgesetz and extends year by year through the primary grades. Neither entitlement came with enough trained staff.

## Foreign-trained candidates {#foreign-trained-candidates}

1. **Apply to the competent authority of your chosen Bundesland** — typically the Kultusministerium, Landesjugendamt, or a designated recognition office. **There is no national procedure.** Start at anerkennung-in-deutschland.de to identify the right body.
2. **Gleichwertigkeitsprüfung** against that state's training. Foreign qualifications in early-years education are often found partly equivalent, with gaps in German-specific law (SGB VIII), the state Bildungsplan, and safeguarding.
3. **Closing gaps:** an Anpassungslehrgang or a Kenntnisprüfung, depending on state.
4. **Language: B2 minimum, and several states require C1.** The requirement is stricter than in most professions for a substantive reason — supporting children's language development is a core task, and authorities take the view that it needs native-adjacent fluency.
5. **Visa:** §16d AufenthG for recognition purposes, then the skilled-worker permit. Salary is below the EU Blue Card threshold, so the Blue Card is not the route.
6. **Interim work:** many people work as **Ergänzungskraft** or Sozialassistent/in while recognition runs. Check what the contract converts to on recognition.

## Pitfalls {#pitfalls}

- **State fragmentation is the defining problem.** Research the specific Bundesland before anything else, and be wary of general advice — including summaries like this one.
- **The unpaid classic route** traps people who did not realise PiA existed. Ask every Fachschule whether they offer it.
- **The five-year reality** of training when a prerequisite qualification is needed.
- **Title confusion:** Erzieher/in, Kinderpfleger/in, Sozialassistent/in and Kindheitspädagoge/in are four different qualifications at three different levels. Ads use them loosely; contracts and pay grades do not.
- **Erweitertes Führungszeugnis** (enhanced criminal record check) is mandatory, and foreign applicants need an equivalent from their home country.
