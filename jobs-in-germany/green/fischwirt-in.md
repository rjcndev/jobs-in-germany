> The smallest occupation in this repo, and split into two halves moving in opposite
> directions: **aquaculture**, which is short of qualified people, and **coastal fishing**,
> which is being ended by quota. Also the only green profession here that requires **maritime
> certification** on top of the qualification.

## Two Fachrichtungen {#two-fachrichtungen}

### Fischhaltung und Fischzucht — aquaculture {#fischhaltung-und-fischzucht--aquaculture}

Trout and carp farming are the German mainstays, with **recirculating aquaculture systems
(RAS)** growing for higher-value species. The work:

- Stock management, feeding, grading, health monitoring and treatment
- **Water quality** — oxygen, ammonia, temperature, flow — which is the technical core, and
  in a RAS is a continuously monitored engineering problem
- Pond management, harvesting, processing, smoking and direct marketing
- **Water law and permits**, which govern abstraction, discharge and stocking
- Predator management — cormorants and otters are a real and politically contested economic
  problem for pond farms

**Traditional Teichwirtschaft is also conservation work.** Carp pond landscapes are protected
habitats of high nature value, and agri-environment payments support their maintenance —
which is why parts of this Fachrichtung look more like the
[Schäferei](/jobs-in-germany/green/tierwirt-in/) model than like food production.

### Kleine Hochsee- und Küstenfischerei — coastal fishing <a id="kleine-hochsee--und-küstenfischerei--coastal-fishing"></a>

Working small and medium vessels on the North Sea and Baltic: gear handling, navigation,
catch handling and on-board processing, engine watch, and the logbook and landing
documentation that EU fisheries control requires.

**This half of the occupation is in structural decline, and the file would be dishonest not
to lead with it.** Quotas under the Common Fisheries Policy have been cut severely — Baltic
cod and herring in particular have been reduced to levels that make targeted fishing
unviable — decommissioning schemes have removed vessels, the fleet is old and the workforce
is ageing. There are also offshore wind and marine-protection area constraints on where the
remaining fleet can work.

People still fish, and small-scale coastal fishing with direct marketing survives in places.
Entering it as a career in 2026 is a decision to take with the quota tables open.

## Maritime certification — a separate gate {#maritime-certification--a-separate-gate}

Sea-going work needs more than the Ausbildung, and none of it is implied by it:

- **Seediensttauglichkeit** — a medical fitness certificate, renewed periodically
- **Basic safety training** to the international standard — sea survival, firefighting,
  first aid
- **Befähigungszeugnisse** for operating and navigating vessels by size and area, and
  engine-watch qualifications where applicable

These are the same kind of
[activity gates](/jobs-in-germany/reference/occupational-certificates/) the repo records elsewhere, and
foreign equivalents need checking individually — maritime certificates follow international
conventions, so some transfer better than most German qualifications do.

## Pay {#pay}

**Market estimate**, and the fishing half is often not a wage at all.

| | Gross/month |
|---|---|
| Aquaculture, qualified | €2,400 – €3,000 |
| Aquaculture, experienced / RAS technical | €3,000 – €3,800 |
| **Fischwirtschaftsmeister / farm management** | €3,600 – €4,600 |
| Coastal fishing, employed crew | €2,400 – €3,400, **often on a share of the catch rather than a salary** |
| Vessel owner | A business result; in recent quota years, frequently negative |

Share-based crew payment is worth understanding before signing: it moves the risk of a bad
season onto the crew, and the statutory minimum wage still applies underneath it.

## Demand and outlook {#demand-and-outlook}

- **Aquaculture: a genuine shortage of qualified people**, and a sector with political
  support — domestic fish production is favoured over imports, and RAS technology is
  expanding. The technical end of this Fachrichtung is the growth area in the whole
  occupation.
- **Fisheries: declining**, for the reasons above.
- **Adjacent employment** absorbs a lot of this qualification: state fisheries authorities,
  angling associations as **Gewässerwart**, hatcheries, research institutes, and fish health
  services.

## Qualification and above {#qualification-and-above}

- **3 years dual**, examined by the Landwirtschaftskammer or a state fisheries authority —
  and in the coastal states these authorities have names of their own, so confirm which
  applies
- **Above it:** **Fischwirtschaftsmeister/in**, or a degree in fisheries science, aquaculture
  or marine biology for research, authority and management roles

## Foreign candidates {#foreign-candidates}

1. **To work: no recognition required.**
2. **For the visa: yes** — via the Landwirtschaftskammer or the state fisheries authority.
   This is the green sector's most fragmented competent-authority question, because the
   coastal states organise it differently again.
3. **Maritime certificates are separate** and partly internationally portable — check them
   individually rather than assuming either way.
4. **Language: B1–B2**; more for authority and advisory roles.
5. **Visa:** §18a, **§16a**, or **§26(2) BeschV**.

## Pitfalls {#pitfalls}

- **Choose the Fachrichtung with the outlook in front of you.** They are not close to
  equivalent right now.
- **Share-of-catch pay is not a salary**, and the minimum wage still applies.
- **Aquaculture is an engineering job** as much as an animal one, particularly in RAS.
- **Water rights and permits are the constraint** on starting a fish farm, more than capital.
- **The sea-going medical is disqualifying for some conditions**, and it is worth checking
  before training rather than after.
