> The contractor side of agriculture, and the part nobody outside the sector sees. Farms
> increasingly do not own the machinery that works their land — a **Lohnunternehmen** does,
> and sends operators. It pays better than farm employment, it is more technical, and it is
> one of the least-known qualified occupations in this repo.

## Why contracting exists {#why-contracting-exists}

A self-propelled forage harvester or a large combine costs as much as several houses and is
used for a few weeks a year. Farms below a certain size cannot justify owning one, and above
a certain complexity cannot justify the downtime risk. So the work is bought in:

- **Lohnunternehmen** — agricultural contracting businesses, owning the machinery and
  employing the operators
- **Maschinenringe** — cooperative machinery rings that broker equipment and labour between
  farms

Both employ Fachkräfte Agrarservice, and both are growing as machinery costs rise faster than
farm incomes.

## What the job involves {#what-the-job-involves}

- **Operating high-value machinery** — combines, forage harvesters, self-propelled sprayers,
  slurry tankers with injection equipment, balers, large tractors and implements
- **Precision agriculture** — GPS guidance, section control, variable-rate application,
  yield and application mapping, and the data handover to the customer afterwards
- **Road transport** between farms, which is a large part of the working day and brings
  driving-licence requirements with it
- **Maintenance and repair** — contractors keep their own workshops, and a real part of this
  qualification is diagnosing and fixing machinery in a field at ten at night
- **Plant protection and fertiliser application** on the customer's behalf, with the
  documentation obligations that carries
- **Biogas logistics** — substrate harvest, transport and silage management for biogas
  plants, a substantial contracting market
- **Customer work** — you are on someone else's farm, working to their expectations, and
  the job is billed by area or by hour

## How it differs from Landwirt {#how-it-differs-from-landwirt}

| | **[Landwirt/in](/jobs-in-germany/green/landwirt-in/)** | **Fachkraft Agrarservice** |
|---|---|---|
| Breadth | Agronomy, livestock, farm business | **Machinery, application technique, logistics** |
| Employer | A farm | A **contractor**, working across many farms |
| Animals | Often central | **None** |
| Pay | Lower | **Higher** |
| Seasonality | Year-round with peaks | **Campaign work** — extreme peaks, quieter winters |

If you want agriculture without livestock and with machinery at the centre, this is the
qualification, and it is routinely overlooked by people who only know the Landwirt route.

## The campaign {#the-campaign}

Contracting runs on campaigns: silage, harvest, slurry, drilling. During them the days are
very long, the weather sets the schedule, and a breakdown at the wrong hour costs the
customer their crop.

German working-time law makes specific allowance for agricultural peak periods, but it is
allowance, not exemption — the compensatory rest and the record-keeping still apply. See
[employment basics](/jobs-in-germany/reference/employment-basics/). Winters are correspondingly
quieter, and many contractors fill them with winter road service, workshop overhaul and
transport work.

## Licences and certificates {#licences-and-certificates}

This occupation carries more of them than most in the green sector:

- **Führerschein T** for agricultural vehicles, and frequently **C/CE** for road transport —
  with **Code 95** where the work counts as commercial haulage. See
  [Berufskraftfahrer/in](/jobs-in-germany/logistics/berufskraftfahrer-in/)
- **Pflanzenschutz-Sachkunde**, with a refresher every three years
- **Sprayer inspection** and application-equipment competence
- Plant, loader and lifting tickets

They are short, employer-funded and directly convertible into pay — the pattern collected in
[occupational certificates](/jobs-in-germany/reference/occupational-certificates/).

## Pay {#pay}

**Market estimate.**

| Stage | Gross/month |
|---|---|
| Qualified, entry | €2,900 – €3,500 |
| Experienced operator, main machines | €3,400 – €4,200 |
| With CE and transport work, or team responsibility | €3,900 – €4,800 |
| **Agrarservicemeister / Betriebsleitung** | €4,300 – €5,400 |

**Campaign overtime is where the money is**, and it should be paid or banked explicitly —
ask which, and ask what happens to a positive balance at year end.

## Demand and outlook {#demand-and-outlook}

**Strong and structurally supported.** Outsourcing of field operations has increased for
decades and continues to, because machinery is expensive, precision requirements are rising
and farms are consolidating. Contractors are also the natural home for the most advanced
equipment, which makes this the part of agriculture with the best technology exposure.

Additional drivers: **slurry injection requirements** under fertiliser law, which need
specialised equipment most farms will never buy; **biogas** substrate logistics; and the
growth of data-driven application services.

The shortage of qualified operators is genuine, and contractors compete for them with pay and
with machinery.

## Foreign candidates {#foreign-candidates}

1. **To work: no recognition required.**
2. **For the visa: yes** — **Landwirtschaftskammer** or state authority.
3. **Driving licences are the practical obstacle.** A third-country C/CE licence generally
   cannot be exchanged, and Code 95 must be obtained here. Budget for it.
4. **Language: B1–B2.** Customer contact, machinery safety and application documentation.
5. **Visa:** §18a, **§16a**, or **§26(2) BeschV**.
6. **Machinery experience transfers well**, and demonstrable operating skill on current
   equipment is worth more in this occupation than in most.

## Pitfalls {#pitfalls}

- **Check which licences the job actually requires** before accepting — T is not C/CE.
- **Campaign hours are real.** Establish how overtime is handled in writing.
- **You are working on other people's farms**, which is a customer-service job as much as a
  machinery one.
- **The workshop half matters.** Contractors value people who can fix what they operate, and
  pay for it.
- **Winter work varies enormously** between employers. Ask what you will be doing in January.
