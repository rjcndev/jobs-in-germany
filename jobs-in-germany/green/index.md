Fourteen recognised occupations covering farming, forestry, horticulture, viticulture, livestock and fisheries. They sit outside both systems the rest of this repo describes.

## A third chamber {#a-third-chamber}

The [skilled trades](/jobs-in-germany/skilled-trades/) answer to the **Handwerkskammer** and the [industrial](/jobs-in-germany/industrial/) occupations to the **IHK**. The Grüne Berufe answer to the **Landwirtschaftskammer** — training supervision, examinations, and equivalence assessment of foreign qualifications.

**And only in some Bundesländer.** Where no Landwirtschaftskammer exists, a state ministry or Regierungspräsidium is the competent body instead. So this sector combines a third chamber system *with* the sixteen-state fragmentation seen in [Erzieher](/jobs-in-germany/education/erzieher-in/) and [Lehrer](/jobs-in-germany/education/lehrer-in/).

Identify the competent body for your target Bundesland before anything else.

## What else is distinctive {#what-else-is-distinctive}

- **Qualification gates.** Applying pesticides commercially requires the **Pflanzenschutz-Sachkunde** certificate; chainsaw work requires documented training. Neither is implied by the job title, and neither transfers automatically from abroad.
- **Seasonal migrant labour.** Harvest work runs largely on the **70-day short-term employment rule**, social-insurance-free and overwhelmingly staffed from Romania and Poland. It is a structural feature of German agriculture with a documented enforcement record, and it is not the same thing as the qualified occupations profiled here.
- **Weather and physical risk** are everyday working conditions, not footnotes.
- **Public-sector employment is common** in forestry, which makes pay tariff-verifiable — unusually for a manual trade.

## Professions in this category {#professions-in-this-category}

<div class="jig-cards"></div>

- [Fachkraft Agrarservice (Agricultural Contracting Technician)](/jobs-in-germany/green/fachkraft-agrarservice/) <span>The contractor side of agriculture, and the part nobody outside the sector sees.</span>
- [Fischwirt/in (Fisheries and Aquaculture Worker)](/jobs-in-germany/green/fischwirt-in/) <span>The smallest occupation in this repo, and split into two halves moving in opposite directions: aquaculture, which is short of qualified people, and coastal fishing, which is being ended by quota.</span>
- [Forstwirt/in (Forest Worker)](/jobs-in-germany/green/forstwirt-in/) <span>Fells, plants and tends forest.</span>
- [Gärtner/in (Horticulturist / Landscape Gardener)](/jobs-in-germany/green/gaertner-in/) <span>Seven Fachrichtungen under one job title, and they are not variations on a theme — the largest, Garten- und Landschaftsbau, is a construction trade in all but name and is booming on urban greening and climate adaptation, while the production Fachrichtungen are under real pressure from imports and energy costs.</span>
- [Hauswirtschafter/in (Home Economics / Institutional Housekeeping)](/jobs-in-germany/green/hauswirtschafter-in/) <span>Formally one of the Grüne Berufe, and almost nobody who holds it works on a farm.</span>
- [Landwirt/in (Farmer / Agricultural Worker)](/jobs-in-germany/green/landwirt-in/) <span>The anchor file for the Grüne Berufe, and the one place in this repo where a whole labour model has to be described alongside the profession: the 70-day rule, which makes German harvest work social-insurance-free, is staffed overwhelmingly from Romania and Poland, and has a documented enforcement and exploitation record.</span>
- [Pferdewirt/in (Equine Professional)](/jobs-in-germany/green/pferdewirt-in/) <span>This is the file where the repo's honesty convention matters most.</span>
- [Tierwirt/in (Livestock Farmer)](/jobs-in-germany/green/tierwirt-in/) <span>Five Fachrichtungen under one qualification, and they range from industrial-scale dairy and poultry units to Schäferei — a subsidised, culturally protected occupation with a few hundred full-time practitioners, and one of the genuine edge cases in this repo.</span>
- [Winzer/in (Winegrower)](/jobs-in-germany/green/winzer-in/) <span>Viticulture and cellar work in one qualification, concentrated in a handful of Bundesländer, and run overwhelmingly on family succession.</span>
