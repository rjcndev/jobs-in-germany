---
title: "Industrial trades (Industrieberufe)"
---

Skilled trades trained and employed **inside industry** rather than in the Handwerk. Same
kind of work, sometimes the same job title — and a different chamber, tariff, pay level and
set of self-employment rules.

## Handwerk vs. Industrie {#handwerk-vs-industrie}

The repo's [skilled-trades](/jobs-in-germany/skilled-trades/) files are Handwerk. These are not. The
difference is not cosmetic:

| | **Handwerk** | **Industrie** |
|---|---|---|
| Chamber | Handwerkskammer (HWK) | **Industrie- und Handelskammer (IHK)** |
| Legal basis | **Handwerksordnung (HwO)** | Berufsbildungsgesetz (BBiG) |
| Self-employment | Anlage A trades need the **Meisterbrief** | No Meisterpflicht — but see below |
| Typical tariff | Regional Handwerk agreements | **IG Metall / IG BCE**, 35-hour week, 13th month |
| Employer | Small and medium Betriebe | Manufacturers, plants, large industrial sites |
| Pay | Lower | **Materially higher at the same skill level** |

**The trap this creates.** An industrial electrical qualification does **not** give you the
right to open an electrical contracting business. That requires entry in the Handwerksrolle
for the Anlage A trade, which normally means the
[Handwerk route and a Meisterbrief](/jobs-in-germany/skilled-trades/elektroniker-in-energie-und-gebaeudetechnik/).
The two systems train similar people and grant different rights.

## Pay is better here, and not verifiable {#pay-is-better-here-and-not-verifiable}

Industrial trades sit under **IG Metall** or **IG BCE** agreements, which pay above Handwerk
for comparable work and add a 13th-month payment, holiday pay and a 35-hour week in the
metal and electrical industries.

But as [reference/pay.md](/jobs-in-germany/reference/pay/#reliability-of-these-figures) records,
these agreements are **regionally fragmented** — multiple Tarifgebiete negotiating
separately, with **ERA** grading that varies by employer. There is no single scale to check
against, so every figure in these files is a **market estimate**, not a verified one.
Always ask which agreement applies and whether the employer is in the employers'
association.
