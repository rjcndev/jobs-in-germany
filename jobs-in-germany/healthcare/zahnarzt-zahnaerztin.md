> Approbation, like a doctor — but with two things medicine does not have: **no specialist training is required to practise generally**, and a **large private economy sitting on top of a deliberately basic statutory entitlement.** The second is what makes dentistry financially unlike the rest of healthcare in this repo.

## Qualification route {#qualification-route}

1. **Zahnmedizin, 10 semesters** (5 years), plus the examination period. Admission is by a **very high NC** plus aptitude tests; the old waiting-time quota was abolished in 2020, so there is no longer a route in by patience.
2. The curriculum was **reformed by the ZApprO in 2021** — more clinical contact earlier, more integration with medicine, and a three-part state examination.
3. **Approbation** from the state authority.
4. **Two years as an employed dentist — the Vorbereitungszeit.** Required before you may hold your own statutory-insurance licence. In practice this is the Assistenzzeit and it is where the job is actually learned.
5. **Optional:** Fachzahnarzt in **Kieferorthopädie** (3 years), **Oralchirurgie** (4 years), Parodontologie in some chambers, or Öffentliches Gesundheitswesen.

**The contrast with medicine is the point.** An [Arzt](/jobs-in-germany/healthcare/arzt-aerztin/) needs five to six years of Facharzt training before independent practice. A dentist needs **two years of employment** and is then a fully qualified general dentist. Dentistry's specialisations are narrow and optional; medicine's are compulsory and broad.

**Mund-Kiefer-Gesichtschirurgie is the exception in the other direction** — it requires **both** a dental and a medical degree plus specialist training. It is the longest qualification route in this repo by a wide margin.

## What the job involves {#what-the-job-involves}

Diagnosis and restorative work, endodontics, periodontal treatment, extractions and minor surgery, prosthetics, prevention and professional cleaning, paediatric dentistry, and — increasingly — digital workflow: intraoral scanning, CAD/CAM, and chairside milling.

You work with dental assistants (**Zahnmedizinische Fachangestellte** — the dental counterpart of the [MFA](/jobs-in-germany/healthcare/medizinische-fachangestellte-r/)), dental hygienists and a **Zahntechniker** laboratory, which is a separate Handwerk trade with its own Meister.

Most dentists work in **private practice** — their own, a Berufsausübungsgemeinschaft, or as an employee. Hospital and university posts exist but are a small minority.

## The money, and why it is different {#the-money-and-why-it-is-different}

Statutory health insurance covers a **deliberately basic standard**: the treatment necessary, in its plainest adequate form. Nearly everything better is paid by the patient.

| System | Covers |
|---|---|
| **BEMA** | The statutory scale. Basic fillings, standard treatment, and a **Festzuschuss** — a fixed subsidy — toward dentures and crowns |
| **GOZ** | The private scale. Applies to privately insured patients, and to every upgrade a statutory patient chooses |

So implants, high-grade restorations, aligners, whitening and better prosthetics are private revenue from statutorily insured patients. No other profession in this repo has a comparable structural private income stream on top of public entitlement — and it is the main reason dental practice economics differ from medical ones.

The honest flip side: a large part of the job involves **advising patients on treatment they will pay for personally**, which puts clinical judgement and commercial interest in the same room. The profession's own ethics debate is about exactly that, and patients know it.

## Pay {#pay}

**Market estimate throughout** — there is no binding tariff for dentists in private practice.

| Stage | Gross/month |
|---|---|
| Vorbereitungsassistent/in (the two years) | €3,000 – €4,000 |
| Employed dentist, qualified | €4,500 – €6,500 |
| Employed, experienced or with a specialisation | €6,000 – €9,000 |
| Hospital / university (TV-Ärzte or TV-L) | Comparable to the medical scale |
| **Practice owner** | Not comparable — see below |

**Practice ownership** is where the profession's reputation for income comes from. Owners' surplus before tax is statistically among the highest of any occupation in Germany. It is also a business: buying or building a practice runs to several hundred thousand euro, carries staff and equipment costs of roughly two-thirds of revenue, and the income is a return on risk and management as much as on clinical work. Treat it as entrepreneurship, not as a salary grade.

Note also **[Versorgungswerk](/jobs-in-germany/reference/versorgungswerke/)** membership rather than the statutory pension, and **Kammer** membership — both compulsory, both from day one.

## Demand and outlook {#demand-and-outlook}

**Comfortable, and unusually unconstrained.** Bedarfsplanung exists for dentists too, but it is far less restrictive than the cap on [psychotherapy](/jobs-in-germany/healthcare/psychotherapeut-in/) — most regions remain open, so a qualified dentist can generally obtain a statutory licence without buying scarcity.

Trends worth knowing:

- **Rural undersupply is real and growing** as older practice owners retire without successors. This is the strongest opportunity in the field, and it is not in the cities.
- **Investor-owned dental MVZ** have expanded rapidly, backed by private equity and consolidating practices into chains. It is the sector's live political argument, and legislation restricting it has repeatedly been proposed. It also means "employed dentist" increasingly means employed by a group, not by a colleague.
- **Prevention has worked.** German caries rates in children have fallen dramatically over decades, shifting the work toward periodontology, prosthetics, implantology and an ageing population's needs.

## Foreign-trained candidates {#foreign-trained-candidates}

1. **Approbation is mandatory.**
2. **EU/EEA/Swiss: automatic recognition.** Dentist is one of the **seven professions** on the Directive's list — see [Architekt](/jobs-in-germany/engineering/architekt-in/) for the full seven. Present the listed diploma; no equivalence assessment.
3. **Third-country:** individual equivalence assessment by the state authority, with a **Kenntnisprüfung** where gaps are found. A **Berufserlaubnis** can allow supervised work in the meantime, exactly as in medicine.
4. **Language: B2 general plus the C1 professional exam.** The **[Fachsprachprüfung](/jobs-in-germany/reference/language-certificates/)** applies here as it does to doctors and pharmacists, and it is examined by the Zahnärztekammer.
5. **Visa:** **§18b**; **Blue Card comfortably reachable**.
6. **The two-year Vorbereitungszeit applies to you too** before an own statutory licence, and recognised foreign practice time does not automatically substitute for it.

## Pitfalls {#pitfalls}

- **Approbation is not a licence to treat statutory patients.** The Zulassung and the two years come after it.
- **The NC is the real barrier to studying here**, and there is no longer a waiting route. Studying abroad in the EU and returning under automatic recognition is a well-trodden path — check the specific degree appears in the Directive's annex before committing.
- **Practice purchase is a business decision** with a valuation, a handover period and patient-retention risk. Get it assessed by someone who does this professionally.
- **MKG-Chirurgie means two full degrees.** Know that before choosing it as a goal.
- **The private-pay conversation is part of the job.** If that sits badly with you, hospital, university and public-service dentistry exist and pay less.
