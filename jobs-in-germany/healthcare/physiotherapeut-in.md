> Treats movement and musculoskeletal disorders through manual therapy and exercise. Regulated and licensed, but paid markedly less than the diagnostic professions.

## What the job involves {#what-the-job-involves}

Assessment and treatment of patients referred by a doctor: manual therapy, exercise therapy, gait and posture work, post-operative rehabilitation, neurological rehabilitation, respiratory physiotherapy. Settings range from private practices and rehabilitation clinics to hospitals, sports clubs and home visits.

**No direct access.** Treatment under statutory insurance requires a doctor's prescription (Heilmittelverordnung), which also dictates the number and duration of sessions. **Direktzugang** has been debated for over a decade without being enacted.

## Qualification route {#qualification-route}

- **3 years** at a Physiotherapieschule, theory plus clinical placements, ending in a state exam
- **Entry:** mittlerer Schulabschluss
- **Fees are the sore point.** Unlike nursing and MT, physiotherapy training was *not* swept up by the recent reforms. Many schools are private and still charge tuition — commonly €300–500/month. Public and hospital-attached schools are free but heavily oversubscribed.
- **Bachelor route** (often dual, 3.5–4 years) exists and leads to the same licence.

A reform toward full academisation (Vollakademisierung) of the therapy professions has been announced repeatedly. Verify its current state before relying on anything here.

## Pay {#pay}

The weak point of the profession, and the reason for chronic attrition. Gross monthly, 2026. Only the public-sector row is tariff-verified (TVöD VKA, valid 01.05.2026 – 31.03.2027); the private-practice figures are market estimates:

| Setting | Gross/month |
|---|---|
| Private practice, newly qualified | €2,600 – €3,000 |
| Private practice, experienced | €3,000 – €3,600 |
| Hospital / public sector (TVöD EG 7–9a) | €3,295 – €4,980 |
| Self-employed practice owner | Highly variable; depends on private-pay share |

Public-sector employment pays better than private practice, which is the reverse of what many applicants assume. Earnings improve substantially with certified specialisations — **Manuelle Therapie, Bobath, Lymphdrainage, KG-Gerät** — because these unlock higher billing positions. Employers often part-fund them; get that in writing.

## Foreign-trained candidates {#foreign-trained-candidates}

1. **Apply for Anerkennung** to the Bundesland authority (Landesprüfungsamt or equivalent).
2. **Gleichwertigkeitsprüfung**, then an **Anpassungslehrgang** or **Kenntnisprüfung** to close gaps. Foreign physiotherapy degrees are frequently found *broader* than German training in assessment and *narrower* in the specific German manual-therapy canon.
3. **Language:** **B2** is standard.
4. **Visa:** §16d AufenthG, or the skilled-worker route post-licence. Be aware that physiotherapy salaries sit **below the EU Blue Card threshold**, so the Blue Card is generally not available — you use the ordinary skilled-worker permit instead.

## Pitfalls {#pitfalls}

- **Salary expectations.** Physiotherapists from the UK, Australia or the Nordics routinely find German pay lower than they anticipated relative to cost of living.
- **Tuition fees** at private schools catch people who assumed German vocational training is always free. It is not, in this profession.
- **The certificate treadmill:** without Manuelle Therapie and similar certifications your earning ceiling is low, and the courses cost thousands and take years.
- **Prescription-bound practice** frustrates clinicians used to autonomous assessment.
