> The cleanest example in this repo of a profession changing its entry route wholesale: **in 2020 the Ausbildung was abolished outright** and replaced by a dual Bachelor, under EU pressure. Also the only profession here whose presence at a procedure is legally mandatory — a doctor may not conduct a birth without one.

## The 2020 academisation {#the-2020-academisation}

Until 2020, Hebamme was a **three-year school-based Ausbildung** entered from a Realschulabschluss. It no longer exists. The **Hebammenreformgesetz** replaced it, with effect from 01.01.2020 and a transition that closed at the end of 2022, with a **dual Bachelor of Science in Hebammenwissenschaft**.

**Why it happened matters more than the fact.** The EU's professional-qualifications directive was amended to require twelve years of general schooling before midwifery training. The German route began after ten. The choice was to raise the school-entry bar or to academise the profession, and Germany academised it — making this the one case in this repo where **EU law visibly rewrote a German qualification route**.

The point generalises. [Physiotherapie](/jobs-in-germany/healthcare/physiotherapeut-in/) and the therapy professions have been arguing for the same move for years; midwifery is what it looks like when it actually happens, including the disruption: schools closed, universities scrambled to open places, and the number of training places dipped before recovering.

**Practical consequences today**

- The qualification is a **degree**, which changes the visa route from §18a to **§18b** and puts the **Blue Card** within reach where pay allows.
- It is **dual and paid** — you are employed by a clinic throughout, so it is a degree that does not cost you fee-free time. See [Ausbildung](/jobs-in-germany/reference/ausbildung/) for the contrast with the ordinary dual system.
- Midwives trained before 2020 keep their qualification. **Nothing was revoked**, and the older cohort is the majority of the workforce.

## What the job involves {#what-the-job-involves}

- **Vorsorge** — antenatal care, which midwives may provide independently; many pregnancies are followed by a midwife and a gynaecologist in parallel
- **Geburtshilfe** — conducting physiological births independently; assisting where a doctor leads
- **Wochenbettbetreuung** — postnatal home visits, statutorily covered by health insurance, and the largest field of self-employed practice
- **Stillberatung**, newborn checks, and **Rückbildung** and antenatal courses
- Documentation, and the escalation decision: recognising when a birth stops being physiological

Settings: hospital delivery ward, **Geburtshaus**, home birth, or a self-employed practice doing antenatal and postnatal care without attending births.

## The Hinzuziehungspflicht — a reserved role in reverse {#the-hinzuziehungspflicht--a-reserved-role-in-reverse}

**§4 HebG.** A doctor must **call in a midwife** to a birth. Midwives are entitled to conduct a physiological birth on their own responsibility; doctors are not entitled to conduct one without a midwife present.

There is nothing else like it in this repo. The usual pattern is a profession excluded from an activity; here a profession is made **structurally indispensable to another one's**. It is the legal reason a delivery ward cannot run on doctors alone, and the reason midwife shortages close wards outright.

## Qualification route {#qualification-route}

- **Dual Bachelor**, 6–8 semesters (most commonly 7), at a university with a clinic partner
- **Entry:** Abitur or Fachhochschulreife, plus — in most programmes — a training contract with the clinic **before** you can enrol. Securing the clinic place is the real bottleneck, not the university place
- **Paid throughout** by the clinic partner, at training rates comparable to other healthcare trainees
- **Finishes with a state examination** alongside the degree. The **Erlaubnis zum Führen der Berufsbezeichnung** comes from the state authority, not the university
- **Above it:** Master's routes, clinical leadership, Praxisanleitung, academic midwifery and research — an entirely new ladder that the old route did not have

## Pay {#pay}

**Employed in a hospital: tariff, and verifiable.** Grading is typically **TVöD-K EG 9a**, though houses differ and some grade to the P-table; figures are the **TVöD VKA** scale already verified for this repo, valid **01.05.2026 – 31.03.2027**.

| Stage | Grade | Gross/month |
|---|---|---|
| Entry | EG 9a | €3,659 – €3,878 |
| Experienced | EG 9a | €4,098 – €4,587 |
| Senior / Leitung Kreißsaal | EG 9b – 10 | €3,780 – €5,753 |

Delivery wards run around the clock, so **shift, night, Sunday and holiday supplements are a substantial and largely tax-free addition** — see [shift work and supplements](/jobs-in-germany/reference/shift-work-and-supplements/). The table above understates real take-home more than most rows in this repo.

**Self-employed is a different economy.** Fees for antenatal and postnatal care are set nationally in the **Hebammenhilfevertrag (§134a SGB V)** and billed to the patient's health insurer. Postnatal home-visit practice is viable and common. **Attending births self-employed is the problem**, for one reason:

> **Professional indemnity insurance.** Premiums for self-employed intrapartum care rose to five figures annually, which is unpayable from the fee income of the small number of births one midwife can attend. Large numbers of self-employed midwives responded by dropping birth attendance and keeping only antenatal and postnatal work.

A **Sicherstellungszuschlag** paid through the health insurers compensates part of the premium for those who still attend births and meet a minimum caseload. It has slowed the decline rather than reversed it, and independent home birth and Geburtshaus provision remains patchy and regionally uneven.

## Demand and outlook {#demand-and-outlook}

**Strong and structural.** Germany does not have enough midwives; delivery wards have closed for staffing rather than demand, and the shortage is worst in rural regions and in postnatal care, where new parents routinely cannot find a midwife at all.

The 2020 reform arguably tightened supply in the short run by raising the entry bar to Abitur and losing the school-based cohorts. Its longer-term effect is contested inside the profession.

Counterweights worth knowing: the work is shift-based, emotionally heavy, and carries serious liability; and hospital midwifery is chronically understaffed in a way that makes one-to-one care — the thing most midwives entered the profession to do — often impossible.

## Foreign-trained candidates {#foreign-trained-candidates}

1. **Recognition is mandatory.** You may not practise without the state Erlaubnis.
2. **EU/EEA/Swiss:** midwifery is one of the **seven professions with automatic recognition** under Directive 2005/36/EC — see [Architekt](/jobs-in-germany/engineering/architekt-in/), where the list is set out. A listed qualification is recognised on production of the diploma.
3. **Third-country:** individual equivalence assessment by the state authority, with an **Anpassungslehrgang** or **Kenntnisprüfung** where gaps are found. The competent authority varies by Bundesland — see [recognition authorities](/jobs-in-germany/reference/recognition-authorities/).
4. **Language: B2 legally, C1 realistically.** Consent, escalation, handover and documentation in a setting where minutes matter.
5. **Visa:** **§18b** since the qualification is now a degree; §16d to pursue recognition.
6. **Professional indemnity insurance** is a condition of practising self-employed, and its cost is the single largest fact about that option.

## Pitfalls {#pitfalls}

- **The route no longer exists in the form most guides describe.** Anything describing a three-year Ausbildung is pre-2020 and wrong.
- **Getting the clinic contract is the admission bottleneck**, and it is applied for separately, earlier, and in German.
- **Employed and self-employed are different professions economically.** Decide which you are aiming at before planning around a fee schedule or a tariff table.
- **Insurance, not regulation, is what limits independent birth attendance.** No law stops you; the premium does.
