## The 2020 generalist reform {#the-2020-generalist-reform}

The **Pflegeberufegesetz** merged three separate nursing qualifications into one generalist
training from 1 January 2020. Trainees now do a common two-year foundation, then either
continue generalist (Pflegefachfrau/-mann, recognised EU-wide) or specialise in the third
year as Gesundheits- und Kinderkrankenpfleger/in or Altenpfleger/in.

The generalist title is the one that travels. The two specialist titles are valid in
Germany but do **not** carry automatic EU recognition.

## What the job involves {#what-the-job-involves}

Patient assessment and care planning, administering medication, wound care, monitoring
vitals, assisting procedures, extensive documentation, and coordinating with doctors,
therapists and families. Three-shift rotation (Früh/Spät/Nacht) is the norm in hospitals
and inpatient elderly care.

German nurses have historically had a narrower scope of practice than UK or US nurses —
less autonomous prescribing and assessment. That is slowly widening through
**Vorbehaltsaufgaben** (tasks legally reserved to qualified nurses) introduced by the PflBG.

## Qualification route {#qualification-route}

- **3 years**, dual: employed by a care provider, schooled at a Pflegeschule
- **Entry:** mittlerer Schulabschluss, or Hauptschulabschluss plus a completed Ausbildung
- **Paid throughout:** roughly €1,340–1,500/month gross depending on year, under TVAöD-Pflege
- **No school fees** — abolished by the PflBG
- **Ends with** a state exam and the licence

A parallel **Bachelor route** (primärqualifizierender Pflegestudiengang, 3–4 years) exists
and leads to the same licence plus a degree. Uptake is low but growing.

## Pay {#pay}

Public-sector employers pay under **TVöD-P**. A newly qualified nurse starts around **P7**;
specialists and team leads sit higher. Gross monthly, from the table valid
**01.05.2026 – 31.03.2027**, verified against the published TVöD-P scale 2026-09.
Note that **P7 has no Stufe 1** — the ladder starts at Stufe 2:

| Stage | Gross/month |
|---|---|
| Newly qualified (P7 Stufe 2–3) | €3,510 – €3,701 |
| Experienced (P7 Stufe 4–6) | €3,998 – €4,305 |
| Specialist — ITS, anaesthesia, OR (P9–P10) | €3,992 – €4,961 |

Plus shift supplements, night rates (significant), weekend and public-holiday pay, and in
many states a **Pflegezulage**. Actual take-home for a full-time rotating nurse is well
above base.

Church-run providers (Caritas, Diakonie) use their own comparable agreements (AVR).
Private chains often pay less. **Leiharbeit** (agency nursing) pays more per hour with
worse conditions and is a live political controversy.

## Employers and demand {#employers-and-demand}

Hospitals, nursing homes (Pflegeheime), outpatient care services (ambulante Pflegedienste),
rehabilitation clinics, hospices. Demand is acute and structural — nursing is the flagship
shortage occupation in Germany and the main target of international recruitment.

## Foreign-trained candidates {#foreign-trained-candidates}

1. **Apply for Anerkennung** to the competent authority of the Bundesland — typically the
   Bezirksregierung or Landesamt für Gesundheit und Soziales. State-bound, as always.
2. **EU/EEA qualifications** are automatically recognised under Directive 2005/36/EC if
   they match the general nurse standard — a much shorter path.
3. **Third-country qualifications** go through a Gleichwertigkeitsprüfung, then either an
   **Anpassungslehrgang** (6–12 months supervised practice, ending in a Abschlussgespräch)
   or a **Kenntnisprüfung** (practical exam). Employers usually prefer the Anpassungslehrgang
   because they can employ you as a Pflegehelfer/in while you do it.
4. **Language:** **B2** is the standard requirement; a few states and many employers ask for
   B2 plus a care-specific language test.
5. **Visa:** §16d AufenthG for recognition purposes, or — since March 2024 — an
   **Anerkennungspartnerschaft** (§16d(3)), which lets you enter with A2 German and a
   contract, and pursue recognition after arrival. The **beschleunigtes Fachkräfteverfahren**
   run by the employer can compress processing times considerably.

## Pitfalls {#pitfalls}

- **Working as a Pflegehelfer/in while awaiting recognition is normal** — but confirm the
  contract upgrades automatically on licence, and at what pay grade. Some employers stall.
- **Scope shock:** experienced foreign nurses frequently find German practice more
  hierarchical and more documentation-heavy than they expect.
- **Anpassungslehrgang quality varies wildly** between providers. Ask what the last cohort's
  pass rate was.
- **The three old titles still appear everywhere** in ads and in people's certificates.
