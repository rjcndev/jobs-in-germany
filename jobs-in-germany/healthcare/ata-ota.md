> **Federally regulated since 2022**, and before that not regulated at all. The clearest recent example in this repo of **regulation arriving**: a qualification that existed for two decades as an industry recommendation became a protected title with a state licence, a national curriculum and — for the first time — a recognition procedure for people trained abroad.

## What changed in 2022 {#what-changed-in-2022}

| | **Before** | **Since 01.01.2022** |
|---|---|---|
| Legal basis | A **recommendation** of the Deutsche Krankenhausgesellschaft | **Federal statute** — ATA-OTA-Gesetz and its Ausbildungsverordnung |
| Curriculum | Varied by hospital | National, with defined hours and placements |
| Title | Unprotected | **Protected** |
| Qualification | A certificate from the training hospital | A **state Erlaubnis** |
| Foreign qualifications | **No recognition procedure existed** | Equivalence procedure with Anpassungslehrgang or Kenntnisprüfung |
| Funding | Hospital-by-hospital | Financed through the hospital training levy, like nursing |

**Why this is worth a file.** The repo's framing treats regulation as settled — a profession either is or is not regulated. ATA/OTA shows the boundary moving, recently, and in the direction of more regulation, the same way the [2020 re-regulation of twelve trades](/jobs-in-germany/skilled-trades/raumausstatter-in/) did in the Handwerk. Nothing here is permanent.

**Practical consequences.** Qualifications obtained under the old DKG recommendation were **grandfathered** — holders could apply for the new title. And a foreign-trained surgical assistant who previously had nothing to apply for now has a procedure, which is the single biggest change for readers of this repo.

## The two professions {#the-two-professions}

They share a law, a length and a structure, and the work is quite different.

| | **OTA** | **ATA** |
|---|---|---|
| Works in | Operating theatre | Anaesthesia, recovery, and often ICU/emergency support |
| Core role | **Instrumentieren** — managing the sterile field and handing instruments; and **Springer**, the unsterile role supplying the table | Preparing and monitoring anaesthesia, assisting airway management, drug preparation, monitoring in recovery |
| Also | Positioning, counting, sterile-goods management, documentation, endoscopy and day-surgery units | Patient handover, emergency support, transport of ventilated patients |
| Alongside | Surgeons, anaesthetists | Anaesthetists, ICU nurses |

Both are heavily protocol-driven, both carry real patient-safety weight — the instrument and swab count is a legal obligation, not a formality — and both mean long standing, on-call duty and an environment where the tempo is set by other people.

## Confusable titles — and one career decision {#confusable-titles--and-one-career-decision}

| Title | Reality |
|---|---|
| **OP-Pflegekraft with Fachweiterbildung Operationsdienst** | A [qualified nurse](/jobs-in-germany/healthcare/pflegefachfrau-pflegefachmann/) plus a **2-year specialist Weiterbildung**. Does the same theatre work |
| **OTA** | A 3-year entry qualification that leads **only** to theatre work |
| **Anästhesiepflege** | A nurse with the anaesthesia/intensive-care Fachweiterbildung — the nursing counterpart of ATA |
| **Sterilisationsassistent/in (Fachkraft für Medizinprodukteaufbereitung)** | Reprocesses instruments. A short qualification, not this |

> **The decision:** a nurse with the Fachweiterbildung can work in theatre **and** anywhere else in the hospital. An OTA cannot work as a nurse on a ward. The OTA route is shorter and starts earning sooner; the nursing route is slower and keeps every door open.

For anyone unsure whether they want theatre for a whole career — and theatre is physically and temperamentally demanding — that is not a small difference. The repo's general rule applies: check what a qualification closes, not only what it opens.

## Qualification route {#qualification-route}

- **3 years dual**, hospital plus a state-approved school. Placements are prescribed and include areas outside the specialism
- **Entry:** mittlerer Schulabschluss, or a Hauptschulabschluss with a completed vocational qualification
- **Paid throughout**, at hospital training rates comparable to nursing
- **Staatliche Prüfung** — written, oral and practical — then the state **Erlaubnis**
- **Above it:** Praxisanleitung, team and theatre coordination, specialisation by surgical discipline, medical-device and sterile-goods management, and industry roles with implant and device manufacturers, which recruit from this group and pay well

## Pay {#pay}

Hospital employment means tariff pay, and it is verifiable. Grading is typically **TVöD-K EG 9a**, with houses differing.

| Stage | Grade | Gross/month |
|---|---|---|
| Entry | EG 9a | €3,659 – €3,878 |
| Experienced | EG 9a | €4,098 – €4,587 |
| Coordination / Praxisanleitung | EG 9b – 10 | €3,780 – €5,753 |

TVöD VKA scale, valid **01.05.2026 – 31.03.2027**, as verified elsewhere in this repo.

**On-call is the real money.** Theatres run emergency cover around the clock, so Bereitschaftsdienst, Rufbereitschaft, night, Sunday and holiday supplements form a substantial and largely **tax-free** addition — see [shift work and supplements](/jobs-in-germany/reference/shift-work-and-supplements/). Ask how on-call is structured before comparing two offers; it can move take-home by several hundred euro a month.

## Demand and outlook {#demand-and-outlook}

**Severe shortage, with a direct and visible consequence:** German hospitals cancel and postpone operations because theatres cannot be staffed, not because surgeons are unavailable. Theatre capacity is a staffing problem.

Both titles are on shortage lists, recruitment is active, and the 2022 regulation was in part a response to it — a standardised, recognisable qualification makes both domestic training and international recruitment easier.

Outlook is stable: surgical volume is not falling, day surgery is expanding, and none of the work is plausibly automated. The counterweights are physical wear, on-call load, and a narrow qualification.

## Foreign-trained candidates {#foreign-trained-candidates}

1. **Recognition is now mandatory — and now possible.** Before 2022 neither was true.
2. **No automatic EU recognition**; every application is assessed individually.
3. **Competent authority is the Bundesland.** Expect equivalence assessment against the new national curriculum, with an **Anpassungslehrgang** or **Kenntnisprüfung** for gaps. See [recognition authorities](/jobs-in-germany/reference/recognition-authorities/).
4. **If you are a qualified nurse with theatre experience**, consider applying for [nursing recognition](/jobs-in-germany/healthcare/pflegefachfrau-pflegefachmann/) instead and adding the Fachweiterbildung here. It is usually the stronger position, and nursing has automatic EU recognition, which this does not.
5. **Language: B2 legally, B2–C1 realistically.** Theatre communication is terse, fast and safety-critical, and the count is called aloud.
6. **Visa:** §18a, or §16d to pursue recognition; §16a to train here. **No Blue Card.**

## Pitfalls {#pitfalls}

- **Anything written before 2022 describes a different legal position.** Check the date on any guidance you find.
- **The qualification is narrow.** Confirm you want theatre or anaesthesia specifically.
- **Grandfathered colleagues exist** with the old DKG certificate; their qualification is valid and not inferior.
- **On-call load varies enormously by house** and is the main quality-of-life variable.
- **ATA and OTA are not interchangeable.** They are separate titles with separate examinations, and hospitals advertise for one or the other.
