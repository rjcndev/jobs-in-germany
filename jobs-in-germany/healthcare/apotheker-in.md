## Reserved ownership, not just reserved work {#reserved-ownership-not-just-reserved-work}

Every other regulated profile here restricts an *activity*. German pharmacy law goes further and restricts **who may own the business**.

Under the **Apothekengesetz (ApoG)**:

- A pharmacy may be owned and run only by a licensed pharmacist, **personally and responsibly** — the *Fremdbesitzverbot* and *Inhaberprinzip*. No corporate chains, no outside investors.
- Since 2004 one pharmacist may hold **at most four**: one Hauptapotheke plus up to three Filialapotheken, all within defined proximity.
- Operating requires a separate **Betriebserlaubnis** from the state authority, on top of your Approbation.
- Day-to-day operation is governed in detail by the **Apothekenbetriebsordnung (ApBetrO)**.

The **European Court of Justice upheld this model in 2009** (C-171/07, brought against the Saarland pharmacists' chamber over DocMorris), accepting that member states may reserve pharmacy ownership to pharmacists on public-health grounds.

Practical consequence: there is no corporate ladder. The career fork is **employed pharmacist or owner**, and owning means running a small business with your licence and personal liability attached to it.

## Where pharmacists work {#where-pharmacists-work}

- **Offizin** — public pharmacy. The large majority.
- **Krankenhausapotheke** — hospital pharmacy: clinical pharmacy, sterile compounding, cytostatics, medication safety. Better hours, tariff pay, more clinical.
- **Industrie** — pharmaceutical manufacturing, QA/QC, regulatory affairs, drug safety. The best paid, and the furthest from patients. **Qualified Person** roles under the AMG are a distinct, well-paid specialism.
- **Behörden** — Amtsapotheker in public health administration, inspection, Bundeswehr.

**Fachapotheker** specialisation (Allgemeinpharmazie, Klinische Pharmazie, Offizinpharmazie, Arzneimittelinformation, Toxikologie, and others) runs about three years under the Apothekerkammer, alongside work.

## Qualification route {#qualification-route}

- **8 semesters (4 years) of Pharmazie** at university, NC-restricted, under the **Approbationsordnung für Apotheker (AAppO)**
- **Three Staatsexamen**: after the Grundstudium, after the Hauptstudium, and after the practical year
- **Praktisches Jahr** — 12 months of practical training, **at least six of them in a public pharmacy**; the rest may be hospital, industry or administration
- **Approbation** on passing the third exam

Note what is absent: unlike almost every other profession in this repo, **there is no vocational route in**. The adjacent Ausbildung qualifications — **PTA** (pharmaceutical technical assistant) and **PKA** (commercial assistant) — are separate occupations that do not ladder up to Apotheker. Becoming a pharmacist means the degree.

## Pay {#pay}

**Employed pharmacists in public pharmacies** are covered by the **ADEXA/ADA Gehaltstarifvertrag**. Verified 2026-09, valid **01.01.2026 – 31.12.2026**, full-time at **39 h/week**:

| Berufsjahr | Gross/month |
|---|---|
| 1st year | €4,166 |
| 2nd – 5th year | €4,236 |
| 6th – 10th year | €4,528 |
| 11th year onward | €4,922 |

Plus a **tarifliche Sonderzahlung** of a full month's pay with the November salary. Note the sector stress written directly into the agreement: **since August 2024 that payment may be cut to as little as 50%** in the ADA area where the pharmacy's projected result is at least 10% below the prior year, certified by its tax adviser.

This table covers the federal area **except the Nordrhein and Sachsen chamber districts**, which negotiate separately.

**Other settings:**

| Setting | Gross/month |
|---|---|
| Hospital pharmacy (TVöD, typically EG 13–14) | €4,901 – €7,552 |
| Industry | Higher; commonly €70,000–€110,000+ annual |
| Pharmacy owner | Highly variable — see below |

The hospital row is tariff-verified (TVöD VKA, valid 01.05.2026 – 31.03.2027); grading varies by role. The industry row is a market estimate.

Notice the shape: an employed Offizin pharmacist reaches roughly **€4,922 after eleven years** — a flat ladder, and below what a hospital pharmacist at EG 14 or an industry role reaches. Ownership and industry are where the money is.

## The sector is under real financial stress {#the-sector-is-under-real-financial-stress}

This matters more for pharmacy than for most professions here, and a profile that omitted it would mislead.

Pharmacies are remunerated for prescription medicines largely through a **fixed fee per package** rather than a margin — a fee that has been essentially **frozen since 2013** while costs rose. The results are visible: the number of pharmacies in Germany has fallen for two decades and is now **below 17,000**, down from roughly 21,600 in 2008, with closures concentrated in rural areas. Owners staged coordinated protest closures in 2023–24.

Reform has been repeatedly drafted and repeatedly contested — including proposals to allow branches led by PTA rather than a present pharmacist, strongly opposed by the profession. **Check the current legislative status** rather than relying on any written summary, including this one. The fee figure and pharmacy count above should also be re-checked; both move.

None of this makes the profession a bad one — pharmacists are in demand and employed pay is solid and tariff-protected. It does mean that **buying a rural pharmacy is a materially riskier proposition than it was a decade ago.**

## Foreign-trained candidates {#foreign-trained-candidates}

1. **EU/EEA/Switzerland:** pharmacy qualifications are automatically recognised under Directive 2005/36/EC. Apply for Approbation; subject to language, you get it.
2. **Third countries:** the state authority runs a **Gleichwertigkeitsprüfung**; where training is not equivalent you sit a **Kenntnisprüfung** covering German pharmacy practice and law. A **Berufserlaubnis** (§11 BApO) is the temporary, restricted permission that bridges the gap — the same structure as the medical [Berufserlaubnis](/jobs-in-germany/healthcare/arzt-aerztin/).
3. **Fachsprachprüfung:** a pharmaceutical-German exam at the Apothekerkammer, required in effect everywhere. Formally **B2 general plus C1 professional**; plan for C1.
4. **German pharmacy law is the substance of the exam** — AMG, ApBetrO, the reimbursement system, Rabattverträge. Foreign clinical knowledge transfers; the legal and reimbursement framework does not.
5. **Visa:** §16d AufenthG while pursuing recognition, then §18b. The **EU Blue Card** is reachable — borderline at Offizin entry pay, comfortable in industry.

## Pitfalls {#pitfalls}

- **Owning is the only way up in Offizin**, and ownership now carries genuine business risk. Decide deliberately rather than drifting toward it.
- **The four-pharmacy cap** means there is no scaling beyond a small group. Growth ambitions have a hard legal ceiling.
- **PTA and PKA are not stepping stones.** If you want to be a pharmacist, you need the degree from the start.
- **The Fachsprachprüfung**, not clinical knowledge, is the usual obstacle for third-country applicants — as with [doctors](/jobs-in-germany/healthcare/arzt-aerztin/).
- **Nordrhein and Sachsen have their own pay agreements**; do not assume the national table applies.
