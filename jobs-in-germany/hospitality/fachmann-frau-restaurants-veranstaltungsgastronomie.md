## Qualified service is the minority {#qualified-service-is-the-minority}

In most professions in this repo, the qualification is the normal way in. Here it is not. The great majority of restaurant service in Germany is done by **unqualified staff** — students, [Minijobbers](/jobs-in-germany/reference/minijob-und-geringfuegige-beschaeftigung/), seasonal workers and career changers — while the three-year Ausbildung is held by a minority concentrated in hotels, fine dining and event catering.

| | **Qualified** | **Unqualified service** |
|---|---|---|
| Training | 3-year IHK Ausbildung | On the job |
| Typical setting | Hotels, fine dining, Bankett and events | Cafés, casual restaurants, bars, pubs |
| Pay | Modest premium, better progression | At or near the statutory minimum |
| Path up | Restaurantleitung, F&B, Sommelier | Limited without qualifying |

Two practical consequences. If you want to *work* in service, you need nothing — this is one of the most accessible jobs in Germany. If you want the **skilled-worker visa**, you need a recognised qualification, and casual experience will not substitute. See [visa routes](/jobs-in-germany/reference/visa-routes/).

The shorter **Fachkraft für Gastronomie** (2 years, also 2022) can be topped up to the three-year qualification. See the [sector README](/jobs-in-germany/hospitality/) for the full reform table.

## What the job involves {#what-the-job-involves}

Guest service and advice, order taking and till systems, wine and drinks service, table and room setup, and — the part the new title makes explicit — **Veranstaltungsgastronomie**: banqueting, conferences and event catering, including planning, staffing and billing a function. That event side is where the qualification earns its keep and where the better jobs are.

## Conditions, honestly {#conditions-honestly}

The [sector README](/jobs-in-germany/hospitality/) covers the framework — statutory minimum wage, tax-free tips, the Arbeitszeitgesetz and how routinely it is breached. Specific to service:

- **Evenings, weekends and public holidays** are the working week, not exceptions
- **Teildienst** — split shifts of, say, 11:00–14:00 and 17:00–23:00 — is legal and common, and removes the evening without paying for it
- **Tips are a large share of real income here**, larger than in any other profession in this repo, and **tax-free without limit** under §3 Nr. 51 EStG. How tips are pooled and distributed varies enormously between employers — ask before accepting
- Turnover is the highest of any German sector and staffing never recovered from the pandemic

## Qualification route {#qualification-route}

- **3 years dual**, restaurant or hotel plus Berufsschule, **IHK** exam
- **Entry:** formally open
- **Paid throughout:** roughly €1,000–1,250/month gross
- **Ladder:** Stationsleitung, **Restaurantleitung / Oberkellner**, F&B management, **Hotelfachwirt (IHK)**, or **Sommelier** — a recognised and genuinely well-regarded specialisation that changes earning power in fine dining

## Pay {#pay}

**Market estimates**, not tariff-verified. Regional **DEHOGA** agreements exist but coverage is patchy:

| Stage | Gross/month |
|---|---|
| Unqualified service | At or near the statutory minimum |
| Qualified, newly trained | €2,400 – €3,000 |
| Experienced, Stationsleitung | €2,900 – €3,500 |
| Restaurantleitung / Oberkellner | €3,300 – €4,300 |
| F&B management, large property | €4,500 – €6,500 |
| Sommelier, fine dining | €3,500 – €5,000 |

**Add tips.** In good restaurant service they can be a substantial addition and are untaxed, so compare net including them rather than gross.

## Demand {#demand}

Severe and sustained. Hospitality lost a large share of its workforce during the pandemic and did not get it back; service roles are among the hardest for German employers to fill. Multilingual staff are actively sought in cities.

## Foreign candidates {#foreign-candidates}

1. **To work:** nothing required. Unregulated, and one of the most accessible entry jobs in Germany.
2. **For the skilled-worker visa:** an **IHK FOSA** equivalence assessment of a foreign hospitality qualification. Experience alone will not do — the most common misunderstanding in this sector.
3. **No EU Blue Card** at service pay levels.
4. **Westbalkanregelung (§26(2) BeschV)** is heavily used here.
5. **Ausbildung visa (§16a)** to train in Germany, at B1–B2 German for Berufsschule.
6. **Language: B1 workable, B2 to progress.** Casual service in a city can run on English; fine dining, wine advice and event coordination with German clients cannot.

## Pitfalls {#pitfalls}

- **Being hired unqualified and staying unqualified.** Fine for work, a dead end for the visa and for progression.
- **Split shifts** — ask explicitly before signing.
- **Tip pooling rules** vary and are often unwritten. Get them stated.
- **Unpaid trial shifts** are common and generally unlawful where you are doing real work.
- **Seasonal contracts** in resort regions can pay well with accommodation; check the deduction and whether the befristung is genuine.
