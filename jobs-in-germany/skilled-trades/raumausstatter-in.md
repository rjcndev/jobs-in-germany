> Written as a **case study of the 2020 re-regulation**, not as a generic trade profile. As a trade it would largely repeat [Elektroniker EGT](/jobs-in-germany/skilled-trades/elektroniker-in-energie-und-gebaeudetechnik/) and [SHK](/jobs-in-germany/skilled-trades/anlagenmechaniker-in-shk/) — three years, Handwerk, Anlage A, Meister for self-employment. What earns it a file is that it is one of **twelve trades whose Meisterpflicht Germany abolished in 2004 and restored in 2020** — and the workforce is now split between owners who need the Meisterbrief and owners who do not.

## Regulation moves in both directions {#regulation-moves-in-both-directions}

The repo's framing presents **Anlage A** as settled: some trades need a Meisterbrief to open a business, others do not. It is not settled, and this trade is the proof.

| Year | What happened |
|---|---|
| **2004** | A major reform of the Handwerksordnung **deregulated 53 trades**, moving them from Anlage A to **Anlage B1**. The aim was more business formation and lower barriers to entry |
| **2004–2019** | Business numbers in the deregulated trades rose sharply — mostly **solo self-employment**, much of it precarious. **Training collapsed**: firms without a Meister cannot train, and apprentice numbers in those trades fell heavily |
| **2020** | The **Fourth Act amending the Handwerksordnung** restored the Meisterpflicht for **twelve** of them, with effect from February 2020 |

### The twelve {#the-twelve}

Fliesen-, Platten- und Mosaikleger · Betonstein- und Terrazzohersteller · Estrichleger · Behälter- und Apparatebauer · Parkettleger · **Raumausstatter** · Rollladen- und Sonnenschutztechniker · Drechsler und Holzspielzeugmacher · Böttcher · Glasveredler · Schilder- und Lichtreklamehersteller · Orgel- und Harmoniumbauer

The stated grounds were **Gefahrgeneigtheit** — risk to health or safety — and the preservation of **cultural heritage trades**, plus, unmistakably, the collapse in training.

### Bestandsschutz — two classes of owner in one trade {#bestandsschutz--two-classes-of-owner-in-one-trade}

Businesses **already registered** before the change were grandfathered. They continue without a Meisterbrief, indefinitely.

So the trade now contains:

- **Pre-2020 owners** operating lawfully with no Meisterbrief
- **Post-2020 owners** who needed one to start
- and employees, for whom nothing changed at all — **employment in an Anlage A trade has never required a Meister**

If you are comparing yourself to an established competitor and wondering how they opened without the qualification you are being asked for, that is the answer.

### What it means if you are starting now {#what-it-means-if-you-are-starting-now}

To open a Raumausstatter business you need the Meisterbrief, **or** one of the routes around it set out in [the Meister file](/jobs-in-germany/reference/meister/) — including the **§7b Altgesellenregelung** for a Geselle with six years' experience, four of them in a leading position, and the **Ausnahmebewilligung** for hardship cases.

**And one route specific to this corner of the trade:** **Bodenleger/in** is a separate, two-year Ausbildung that remains in **Anlage B1** — no Meisterpflicht. Floor-laying work can still be carried out self-employed under that trade. The re-regulation therefore has a neighbouring door that is still open, which is worth knowing and worth taking legal advice on before relying on.

## What the job involves {#what-the-job-involves}

- **Bodenbeläge** — carpet, vinyl, linoleum, design flooring, sub-floor preparation
- **Wandbekleidung** — wallpaper, fabric, decorative wall systems
- **Polsterei** — upholstering and re-upholstering furniture, including restoration
- **Dekoration** — curtains, blinds, shading systems, measuring and fitting
- **Sonnenschutz** — awnings and internal shading, overlapping with the Rollladen- und Sonnenschutztechniker trade, which was re-regulated alongside it
- Light **Sonderanfertigungen**: vehicle, caravan and boat interiors, seating for venues

Small firms, domestic and commercial customers, and a lot of work in occupied homes — so it is a customer-facing trade, with the language requirement that implies.

## Pay {#pay}

**Market estimate.** The Ausbau trades have regional agreements with limited coverage; most small firms are not bound.

| Stage | Gross/month |
|---|---|
| Geselle, entry | €2,500 – €3,000 |
| Experienced | €2,900 – €3,500 |
| Specialist (Polsterei, restoration, high-end interiors) | €3,300 – €4,000 |
| **Meister**, employed | €3,700 – €4,600 |
| Meister with own business | Not comparable |

This sits at the lower end of the trades in this repo, comparable to [Tischler](/jobs-in-germany/skilled-trades/tischler-in/), and below the Bauhauptgewerbe trades — which have a sector minimum wage and a social fund that this trade does not.

## Demand and outlook {#demand-and-outlook}

**Renovation-driven and reasonably stable.** The work is overwhelmingly in existing buildings, so it is less exposed to the new-build contraction than the structural trades. Ageing housing stock, rental turnover and commercial fit-out keep it going.

Two specific supports: **upholstery restoration** has benefited from the shift away from disposable furniture, and **shading systems** are a growing part of the work as summers get hotter and cooling load becomes a design problem.

Against that: it competes with DIY and with cheap imported furniture at the low end, and the firms are small and locally dependent.

**The re-regulation's own effect** is worth stating honestly, because it is the point of this file: restoring the Meisterpflicht was intended to raise training numbers and quality. It also raised the barrier to opening a business for people who cannot fund a Meisterbrief — which, in a trade with these wages, is a real barrier. Both things are true, and the debate about whether the 2004 reform or the 2020 reversal did more harm is unresolved.

## Foreign candidates {#foreign-candidates}

1. **To work: no recognition required**, exactly as before 2020.
2. **To open a business: the Meisterbrief now applies to you** — the grandfathering only protects businesses that already existed.
3. **For the visa: yes** — **Handwerkskammer** equivalence for §18a.
4. **Language: B2.** This is a consultative trade practised in customers' homes; measuring, advising and agreeing changes is most of the job.
5. **Visa:** §18a, **§16a**, or **§26(2) BeschV**.

## Pitfalls {#pitfalls}

- **Anything written before 2020 has the regulation backwards.** Check the date on any guidance about opening a business in this trade.
- **Bestandsschutz is not transferable** by buying a grandfathered business — take advice before assuming an acquisition carries the exemption.
- **§7b is the realistic route** for most people who want their own business here, and it requires documented leading experience, so keep the records.
- **Confirm which trade a vacancy is actually in.** Raumausstatter, Bodenleger, Parkettleger and Polsterer overlap in practice and are separate qualifications with different rules.
- **Low pay, high customer contact.** The profitable end is specialist and consultative, not volume flooring.
