## The Meister rule {#the-meister-rule}

This trade sits in **Anlage A of the Handwerksordnung**, the list of trades considered hazardous or consumer-critical. Consequences:

- **As an employee:** no recognition needed. A competent electrician with foreign training can be hired immediately.
- **To run your own business:** you must be entered in the **Handwerksrolle** at the Handwerkskammer, which normally requires the **Meisterbrief** — or an exemption (Ausnahmebewilligung), or employing a qualified Betriebsleiter.

This is the single most consequential fact for anyone planning to be self-employed.

## What the job involves {#what-the-job-involves}

Installing wiring, distribution boards and building systems; commissioning and fault- finding; increasingly **photovoltaics, battery storage, heat-pump electrics and EV charging infrastructure**, which is where demand has exploded. Work is on-site, physical, and governed tightly by **VDE norms** — especially VDE 0100.

## Qualification route {#qualification-route}

- **3.5 years dual**: employer plus Berufsschule
- **Entry:** Hauptschulabschluss in practice; formally open
- **Paid throughout:** roughly €900–1,300/month gross depending on year and region
- **Ends with** the **Gesellenprüfung** (IHK/HWK journeyman's exam)
- **Then optionally** the **Meisterprüfung** — 1–2 years, part- or full-time, covering trade practice, theory, business administration and training qualification. **Aufstiegs-BAföG** part-funds it and several states refund fees on passing (Meisterprämie/Meisterbonus).

## Pay {#pay}

| Stage | Gross/month |
|---|---|
| Geselle, newly qualified | €2,800 – €3,300 |
| Geselle, experienced | €3,300 – €4,000 |
| Meister (employed) | €4,000 – €5,000 |
| Meister with own business | Highly variable; a well-run Betrieb outearns most graduates |

Regional spread is wide — southern Germany pays best. Overtime is abundant. Note that the trades' real advantage is the **business ownership ceiling**, not the employed salary.

## Demand {#demand}

Very high, and structurally so. The **Energiewende** — solar, storage, heat pumps, grid work, EV charging — has created more demand than the trade can train for. This is one of the most persistently unfilled occupations in Germany.

## Foreign-trained candidates {#foreign-trained-candidates}

1. **To be employed:** no recognition legally required. In practice, a **Gleichwertigkeitsfeststellung** from the Handwerkskammer (under the **BQFG**) helps with pay grading and with the visa.
2. **To be self-employed:** you need Handwerksrolle entry. Foreign qualifications can be assessed for equivalence to the Meisterbrief, and there is an exemption route (**Ausnahmebewilligung**, §8 HwO) based on documented experience — it is discretionary and not quick. **EU/EEA nationals** have an easier path under §9 HwO and the qualifications directive.
3. **Language:** **B1–B2**. Site safety, VDE norms and customer contact all run in German; this is not a trade where English gets you through.
4. **Visa:** skilled-worker route with a recognised qualification, or an Ausbildung visa (§16a) to train here. Electricians appear on shortage lists, which helps.

## Pitfalls {#pitfalls}

- **Assuming employment requires recognition** — it does not, and waiting for paperwork wastes months of earning.
- **Assuming self-employment does not** — it does, and people discover this after registering a business.
- **Foreign electrical training frequently does not cover VDE**, which is the actual standard your work is judged against. Expect a real learning curve regardless of experience.
- **"Elektroniker" vs "Elektriker"**: the modern formal title is Elektroniker with a Fachrichtung. Ads use both.
