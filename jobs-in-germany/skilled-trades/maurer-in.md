## The Stufenausbildung — construction trains differently {#the-stufenausbildung--construction-trains-differently}

The Bau trades share a **staged** Ausbildung that the rest of the Handwerk does not:

| Stage | Result |
|---|---|
| **Year 1** | Common construction basics, shared across all Bau trades, much of it in an inter-company training centre (**ÜBA**) rather than on site |
| **Year 2** | **Hochbaufacharbeiter/in** — a recognised qualification in its own right. You can stop here |
| **Year 3** | The full trade: **Maurer/in** |

Two consequences worth knowing. The **intermediate qualification is real**, so leaving after two years is not leaving with nothing — but it is a lower Lohngruppe and it is not the trade. And the **ÜBA year is funded by the SOKA-BAU levy**, which is why construction training is broad and standardised where other trades depend on whatever their employer happens to build.

**Construction apprenticeships are the best paid in Germany.** Training pay in the Bauhauptgewerbe runs well above the [statutory minimum](/jobs-in-germany/reference/ausbildung/) and above most industrial rates — a deliberate recruitment instrument in a sector that struggles to attract people.

## What the job involves {#what-the-job-involves}

- **Mauerwerk** — brick, block and natural stone, load-bearing and non-load-bearing
- **Beton- und Stahlbetonarbeiten** — formwork, reinforcement, pouring and finishing. In practice a large share of modern Maurer work is concrete, not brick
- **Estrich, Putz, Abdichtung** — screed, render and waterproofing where not subcontracted
- **Dämmung** — insulation systems, now a large and growing share of the work
- **Sanierung und Umbau** — the renovation of existing structures, including historic fabric, which is where the craft content is highest
- Setting out, levelling, reading structural drawings, and increasingly digital setting-out

Sibling trades diverge from here: **Beton- und Stahlbetonbauer** specialise in reinforced concrete structures, prefabricated elements and infrastructure; **Feuerungs- und Schornsteinbauer** in industrial linings and flues.

## The unqualified route, and why it is in this file {#the-unqualified-route-and-why-it-is-in-this-file}

Most German trades have one way in. Construction has two, and the second is **Bauhelfer** — site labour, no qualification, legally normal and extremely common.

It is also where the sector's problems concentrate: subcontracting chains, posted workers on paper-thin arrangements, hours unrecorded, and entitlements that exist in law and not in practice. The [Bauhauptgewerbe reference](/jobs-in-germany/reference/bauhauptgewerbe/) sets out what applies regardless — the **AEntG minimum wage**, **SOKA-BAU** holiday, German working-time rules, **BG BAU** accident cover from the first hour — and what to check before signing.

**The honest framing:** Bauhelfer work is a way into the sector and a way to be exploited in it, and which one it turns out to be depends almost entirely on the employer. Moving from Bauhelfer to a qualification — via an Umschulung, or the **Externenprüfung** route for people with long site experience — is the single best thing someone in that position can do, and it is fundable through a [Bildungsgutschein](/jobs-in-germany/reference/weiterbildung-funding/).

## Pay {#pay}

**Market estimate**, positioned against the Bau tariff structure. The sector's **AEntG minimum wage** — two Lohngruppen, both above the statutory minimum — is the floor, and it is a perishable figure that this repo does not quote; check it at [soka-bau.de](https://www.soka-bau.de).

| Stage | Gross/month |
|---|---|
| Bauhelfer (Lohngruppe 1) | At or near the Bau minimum |
| Hochbaufacharbeiter (after 2 years) | €2,800 – €3,300 |
| Maurer/in, qualified | €3,100 – €3,800 |
| Experienced, Vorarbeiter | €3,700 – €4,400 |
| **Polier** | €4,200 – €5,200 |
| **Meister**, employed | €4,400 – €5,600 |

What the table leaves out, and it is a lot: **Auslösung** and travel allowances on away sites, partly tax-free; **Bauzuschläge**; a **13th month** under the sector agreement; and the **SOKA-BAU holiday and supplementary pension**, which are real value that never appears on a payslip as salary. Construction pay is understated by a straight monthly comparison more than any other trade in this repo.

Against that: **the working-time account**, which banks summer overtime rather than paying it, and winter months at [Saison-Kurzarbeitergeld](/jobs-in-germany/reference/bauhauptgewerbe/) rather than full pay.

## Demand and outlook {#demand-and-outlook}

**Split, and the split is the whole story.**

- **Residential new-build is weak.** The contraction from 2023 — interest rates, material costs, regulatory cost — cut housing starts sharply and has not recovered to the political targets. Firms exposed to speculative housing have shed staff.
- **Infrastructure and public building are strong**, funded and long-running: bridges, rail, water, schools, hospitals.
- **Sanierung and energy retrofit are the growth area**, driven by the GEG and by an ageing building stock. This is increasingly where a Maurer's career is.
- **Demographics are the sector's real constraint.** The workforce is old, entrant numbers have fallen for decades, and the shortage of qualified Bau tradespeople is structural rather than cyclical — which is why pay and training rates have risen even in a weak market.

## Foreign candidates {#foreign-candidates}

1. **To work: no recognition required.** Anlage A binds only self-employment.
2. **For the visa: yes.** §18a needs a recognised equivalent, assessed by the **Handwerkskammer** — see [recognition authorities](/jobs-in-germany/reference/recognition-authorities/).
3. **To run your own business: the Meisterbrief**, or one of the routes around it in [the Meister file](/jobs-in-germany/reference/meister/) — including the **§7b Altgesellenregelung** for a Geselle with six years' experience.
4. **Language: B1–B2.** Site instruction, safety briefings and coordination. The Berufsschule, if you train here, is unadapted German.
5. **Visa:** §18a with recognition, **§16a** to train here, or **§26(2) BeschV** for Western Balkans nationals — a route heavily used in construction.
6. **Know whether you are employed or posted.** If a foreign firm is sending you to a German site, read the posted-worker section of [the Bauhauptgewerbe reference](/jobs-in-germany/reference/bauhauptgewerbe/) before you travel.

## Pitfalls {#pitfalls}

- **Claim your SOKA-BAU balance** if you leave the industry or the country. Large sums go unclaimed every year.
- **A "seasonal" contract that ends each autumn** is a warning sign — the sector has funded instruments precisely so that does not have to happen.
- **Check which agreement the contract names.** Bauhaupt, Ausbau and the specialist trades are different tariff worlds.
- **The physical toll is the career's limiting factor.** Plan a route to Polier, Meister, Bauleitung or Bauzeichnen before your back makes the decision for you.
- **Wet-cement burns and silica dust** are the quiet injuries of this trade, alongside the dramatic ones. BG BAU's rules are not paperwork.
