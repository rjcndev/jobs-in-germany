## Zimmerer is not Tischler {#zimmerer-is-not-tischler}

The distinction is obvious in German and invisible in English, where both are "carpenter",
and it sends people to the wrong Ausbildung.


<div class="jig-table" markdown="1">

| | **Zimmerer** | **[Tischler/Schreiner](/jobs-in-germany/skilled-trades/tischler-in/)** |
|---|---|---|
| Works on | **Structure** — roofs, frames, load-bearing timber | **Fit-out** — furniture, doors, windows, interiors |
| Where | On site, outdoors, at height | In a workshop, then installing |
| Tariff world | **Bauhauptgewerbe** — BRTV-Bau, SOKA-BAU | Ausbau / joinery agreements |
| Scale | Buildings | Rooms and objects |
| Both | Anlage A; Meister for self-employment | Anlage A; Meister for self-employment |

</div>


If the work holds the building up, it is Zimmerer. If it goes inside the building, it is
Tischler.

Because Zimmerer are **Bauhauptgewerbe**, everything in
[The Bauhauptgewerbe](/jobs-in-germany/reference/bauhauptgewerbe/) applies: SOKA-BAU holiday that
travels between employers, the AEntG minimum wage, the working-time account, and a funded
winter.

## What the job involves {#what-the-job-involves}

- **Dachstühle** — designing, cutting and raising roof structures, still the trade's core
- **Holzrahmen- und Holztafelbau** — timber-frame walls and floors, increasingly prefabricated
- **Brettsperrholz (CLT)** — cross-laminated panels for multi-storey structures, craned into
  place. The technology behind the Holzbau boom
- **Abbund** — the cutting of structural timber, now largely **CNC** in an Abbundzentrum
  from a 3D model. A large part of modern Zimmerer work is digital
- **Sanierung** — repairing historic **Fachwerk** and roof structures, the highest-craft end
  of the trade
- **Bauwerksabdichtung, Dämmung, Fassaden** in timber, and often the roof covering where the
  firm does both
- **Holzschutz** — constructive and chemical, and the building-physics knowledge behind it

The mix has shifted hard toward prefabrication: cut precisely in a hall, assemble fast on
site. That is easier on the body and harder on the drawing.

## The Walz {#the-walz}

Zimmerer maintain the **Wanderjahre** — the journeyman years — more than any other German
trade. A Geselle goes **auf die Walz**: three years and a day travelling, working for board
and lodging, forbidden to come within a set distance of home, in the black corduroy **Kluft**
that is still a recognised sight in German towns.

It is voluntary, a minority pursuit, and genuinely practised. For a foreign reader it is
worth knowing for two reasons: you will meet people who have done it, and it signals
something real about the trade's culture — mobility, craft pride, and a status among the
Handwerk trades that the pay alone does not explain.

## Pay {#pay}

**Market estimate**, positioned against the Bau tariff. The AEntG **Bau-Mindestlohn** is the
floor; it is perishable and is not quoted here.

| Experienced | €3,600 – €4,400 |

Plus the Bauhauptgewerbe additions: away-site **Auslösung**, the 13th month, and SOKA-BAU's
holiday and supplementary pension. Minus the winter months on
Saison-Kurzarbeitergeld.

**Construction apprenticeship pay is the highest in Germany**, and Zimmerer sits inside that
— see [Ausbildung](/jobs-in-germany/reference/ausbildung/).

## Demand and outlook {#demand-and-outlook}

**The strongest structural story of any trade in this repo.**

Timber construction is expanding because concrete and steel are carbon-intensive and timber
stores carbon. That is not a fashion; it is policy. Building codes have been changed — the
**Landesbauordnungen** progressively raised the building classes in which timber structures
are permitted, opening multi-storey and public buildings to a material previously restricted
to houses. Public procurement increasingly favours it.

The consequences for the trade:

- **Multi-storey timber** is now built in German cities, and it needs Zimmerer who can work
  from engineered models
- **Prefabrication** moves work into halls, extends the season and reduces weather exposure
- **The skills premium has moved** toward CAD, Abbund programming and building physics
  alongside the traditional craft
- **Renovation and Aufstockung** — adding storeys to existing buildings in lightweight
  timber — is a large and growing niche in cities that cannot expand outward

The general construction weakness applies here too, and firms are small. But of the six
Bauhaupt trades in this repo, this is the one whose long-run direction is clearly upward.

## Foreign candidates {#foreign-candidates}

1. **To work: no recognition required.**
2. **For the visa: yes** — **Handwerkskammer** equivalence for §18a.
3. **To run a business: the Meisterbrief**, or the alternatives in
   [the Meister file](/jobs-in-germany/reference/meister/).
4. **Language: B1–B2.** Site coordination, crane signalling, structural drawings and safety
   briefings.
5. **Visa:** §18a, **§16a**, or **§26(2) BeschV**.
6. **Timber-frame practice differs by country.** German building physics — vapour control,
   airtightness, Holzschutz — is where equivalence assessments usually find the gap, and it
   is worth studying before an interview as well as before an assessment.

## Pitfalls {#pitfalls}

- **You are in the Bauhauptgewerbe.** SOKA-BAU, the working-time account and the winter all
  apply; claim the holiday balance if you leave.
- **Working at height without proper protection** is the trade's main killer, exactly as in
  [roofing](/jobs-in-germany/skilled-trades/dachdecker-in/).
- **The digital half is not optional any more.** A Zimmerer who cannot read and work from a
  3D model is limited to the site end of the trade.
- **Small firms, variable quality.** A good Holzbau firm is an excellent employer; a poor one
  is a back injury and a winter of Kurzarbeit.
- **The Walz is a serious commitment**, not a gap year, and it has rules.
