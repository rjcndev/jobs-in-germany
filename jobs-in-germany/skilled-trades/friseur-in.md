## The qualification does not command a premium {#the-qualification-does-not-command-a-premium}

Most trade files in this repo can say that finishing the Ausbildung lifts you clearly above unskilled pay. **This one cannot.** A qualified Friseur-Geselle frequently earns at or very near the **statutory Mindestlohn** — the same floor that applies to work requiring no qualification at all.

The history is instructive. Before the statutory minimum wage arrived in 2015, hairdressing wages in parts of eastern Germany were reported in the region of **€3–4 per hour**, and the trade was one of the examples that drove the legislation. A sector minimum wage was declared under the **AEntG** in the interim; the statutory minimum has since overtaken it and now sets the effective floor.

That is worth stating plainly, because the rest of this repo implies a qualification premium that here does not exist. **Choose this trade for the work, or for a route to owning a salon — not on the expectation that the certificate will pay for itself.**

## What the job involves {#what-the-job-involves}

Consultation, cutting, colouring and bleaching, perming and straightening, styling, hair and scalp treatment, and retail of products. Bridal and occasion styling, and **Haarersatz** (wigs and hairpieces, including medical supply) are specialisms that pay better.

The work is continuous customer contact — **the consultation is the job**, not an adjunct to it.

## Two health issues that end careers {#two-health-issues-that-end-careers}

- **Hand eczema.** Occupational skin disease is the defining health risk of this trade: constant wet work plus bleaches, dyes and shampoos. It is a recognised Berufskrankheit and a common reason people leave. Glove discipline and skin protection from day one are not optional.
- **Standing, posture and repetitive strain** across a full shift.

Both are early-career risks, not late-career ones.

## Qualification route {#qualification-route}

- **3 years dual**, salon plus Berufsschule, **Handwerkskammer** exam
- **Entry:** formally open
- **Paid throughout:** roughly €750–1,050/month gross — among the lowest training wages in this repo, though the statutory [Mindestausbildungsvergütung](/jobs-in-germany/reference/ausbildung/) sets a floor
- **Ends with** the **Gesellenprüfung**
- **Then:** the **Friseurmeister/in** for salon ownership, funded by [Aufstiegs-BAföG](/jobs-in-germany/reference/meister/) with a state Meisterprämie in several Bundesländer

## Pay {#pay}

**Market estimates**, not tariff-verified. Note how close entry sits to the statutory floor:

| Stage | Gross/month |
|---|---|
| Geselle, newly qualified | €2,300 – €2,800 |
| Geselle, experienced | €2,600 – €3,200 |
| Meister (employed) | €3,000 – €4,000 |
| Salon owner | Highly variable; premium positioning and location decide it |

Two things lift real earnings above the table:

- **Tips**, which are meaningful here and **tax-free without limit** under §3 Nr. 51 EStG — the same rule as [hospitality](/jobs-in-germany/hospitality/)
- **Commission** on services and product sales, common in chains and larger salons

Ask how commission is structured before accepting an offer; it is often the difference between the bands above.

## Stuhlmiete — read this before going self-employed {#stuhlmiete--read-this-before-going-self-employed}

**Chair rental** is widespread: you rent a station in someone else's salon and work for your own account. Two problems follow.

1. **It is still self-employment in an Anlage A trade**, so it requires entry in the **Handwerksrolle** — normally the Meisterbrief, or the [§7b Altgesellenregelung, §8 Ausnahmebewilligung or a Betriebsleiter](/jobs-in-germany/reference/meister/).
2. Where the salon sets your hours, prices and appointments, the arrangement is frequently challenged as **Scheinselbständigkeit** — bogus self-employment — which makes the salon liable for back social contributions and leaves you without employee protections in the meantime.

Get the arrangement checked before signing, not after.

## Demand {#demand}

Chronic shortage, driven by attrition rather than growth. Trainee numbers have fallen sharply, and qualified staff leave for retail, care and logistics work that pays better for less skill. Salons compete for Gesellen and struggle to find Meister to take businesses over.

## Foreign candidates {#foreign-candidates}

1. **To be employed:** no recognition legally required.
2. **To be self-employed — including Stuhlmiete:** Handwerksrolle entry. See above.
3. **Equivalence** sits with the **Handwerkskammer**.
4. **Language: B2**, and this is the striking part — **a higher language bar than most trades in this repo, at the lowest pay.** Consultation, understanding what a customer actually wants, and managing expectations about colour are the core of the work, and they are unforgiving of approximation. See [language requirements](/jobs-in-germany/reference/language-requirements/).
5. **Visa:** skilled-worker route with recognition, or **§16a** to train here.

## Pitfalls {#pitfalls}

- **Expecting the qualification to pay a premium.** It largely does not.
- **Skin protection from the first week.** Eczema ends careers early.
- **Stuhlmiete without Handwerksrolle entry**, or under terms that make it bogus self-employment.
- **Commission structures** vary enormously and are rarely explained upfront.
- **The language bar is real** despite the pay level.
