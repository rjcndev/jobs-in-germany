> Builds the fall protection that every other trade on the site depends on, and is exposed while building it. One of the highest accident rates in German working life, and the clearest case in this repo of a trade where **safety law is not a constraint on the job — it is the job.**

## What the trade actually is {#what-the-trade-actually-is}

Not "putting up scaffolding". A scaffold is a **temporary load-bearing structure** that other people will trust with their lives, and erecting one involves:

- reading the **Aufbau- und Verwendungsanleitung** for the system and the **Gerüstbauplan** for anything outside standard configurations
- calculating and providing **Verankerung** — ties into the building, which is where most scaffold collapses originate
- ground bearing, bracing, load class selection, and edge protection
- **Sonderkonstruktionen** — free-standing towers, bridging, suspended and hanging scaffold, weather protection roofs, stair towers, industrial and event structures
- **Prüfung und Freigabe** — inspecting, labelling and formally releasing the scaffold for use, with a record. An unreleased scaffold may not be used
- dismantling, which is statistically the more dangerous half

**The Freigabe is a legal act.** The scaffold is handed over with its permitted load class and configuration recorded, and altering it afterwards — as other trades routinely try to — is not permitted without the scaffolder.

## Safety, and the numbers behind it {#safety-and-the-numbers-behind-it}

Scaffolding sits at or near the top of German occupational accident and fatality statistics. The reasons are structural rather than cultural:

- You work at height **before** the fall protection exists, and after it has been removed
- The work is **manual handling at height** — components are carried, passed and placed by hand, all day
- **Weather** is constant exposure: ice, wind, wet steel
- **Time pressure** comes from every other trade waiting for you

What follows legally: **TRBS 2121** and the DGUV rules govern erection, use and dismantling; personal fall-arrest (**PSAgA**) with documented training and a rescue plan where collective protection is not yet in place; a **befähigte Person** must carry out the inspections; and **BG BAU** inspects this trade harder than most.

**None of this transfers from abroad.** Expect to sit the German training regardless of experience — see [occupational certificates](/jobs-in-germany/reference/occupational-certificates/).

## Tariff world {#tariff-world}

Gerüstbau has its **own** framework agreement and its **own AEntG-declared minimum wage**, separate from BRTV-Bau, while sitting inside the **SOKA-BAU** Sozialkassen system for holiday and supplementary pension. Everything in [The Bauhauptgewerbe](/jobs-in-germany/reference/bauhauptgewerbe/) applies — the travelling holiday entitlement, the funded winter, the posted-worker rules — but **check which agreement the contract names**, because the wage tables are not the Bau ones.

## Pay {#pay}

**Market estimate**, positioned against the sector minimum. That minimum is above the statutory one, is renegotiated on its own cycle, and is not quoted here.

| Stage | Gross/month |
|---|---|
| Helfer, unqualified | At or near the Gerüstbau minimum |
| Geselle, entry | €3,000 – €3,600 |
| Experienced | €3,500 – €4,200 |
| **Kolonnenführer** | €4,000 – €4,900 |
| **Meister / Aufsichtsperson** | €4,400 – €5,600 |

Plus away-site **Auslösung** and travel allowances, which are substantial in a trade that follows work across a region, and partly tax-free. Plus SOKA-BAU holiday and supplementary pension.

Scaffolding pays comparably to the other Bauhaupt trades for work that is harder and more dangerous — which is the honest reason it is short of people.

## Demand and outlook {#demand-and-outlook}

**Strong and structurally insulated.** Scaffolding is needed for facade work, roofing, insulation, PV installation, chimney work, painting, industrial maintenance and demolition. It is upstream of nearly every construction activity, so it is less exposed to any single segment's cycle than the trades it serves.

The **energy-retrofit programme is a direct driver**: every insulated facade and every rooftop PV array needs scaffold first.

Constraints: firms are small and regional, the work is seasonal at the margins, and — the sector's own problem — turnover is high, because the physical demands filter people out fast. The shortage of qualified scaffolders is severe and long-standing.

## Foreign candidates {#foreign-candidates}

1. **To work: no recognition required.** Anlage A binds only self-employment.
2. **But erecting scaffold requires documented German training** regardless of qualification. This is the trade's real entry condition.
3. **For the visa: yes** — **Handwerkskammer** equivalence for §18a.
4. **Language: B1–B2, and it matters more here than the pay suggests.** Erection is a team operation with verbal coordination at height; the Freigabe and the inspection record are documents you sign.
5. **Visa:** §18a, **§16a**, or **§26(2) BeschV**.
6. **Posted work is common in this trade**, with the risks described in [The Bauhauptgewerbe](/jobs-in-germany/reference/bauhauptgewerbe/). Establish who your employer is.

## Pitfalls {#pitfalls}

- **Helfer work without training is both illegal and the main way people get hurt.** If you are being put on a scaffold to erect it without documented instruction, that is an offence by the employer, not a shortcut.
- **Dismantling is the dangerous phase**, and is where fatigue and time pressure meet.
- **Never alter a released scaffold** for another trade's convenience, and expect to be asked.
- **Check the agreement** — Gerüstbau, not Bau, and both are SOKA-BAU.
- **Plan the exit.** Kolonnenführer, Meister, Arbeitsvorbereitung and scaffold design are the routes out of carrying components at forty-five.
