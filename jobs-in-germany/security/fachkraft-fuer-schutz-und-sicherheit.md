## What it adds over a 40-hour course {#what-it-adds-over-a-40-hour-course}

Legally, very little: the [§34a tiers](/jobs-in-germany/security/) still govern what you may do, and the
Ausbildung covers the Sachkunde content. What it actually buys:

- **It is a recognised Ausbildungsberuf**, so it supports a **§18a skilled-worker visa** —
  which the Unterrichtung does not. For a non-EU candidate that is the whole difference.
- **Progression.** Objektleitung, Einsatzleitung, planning and key-account roles go to
  qualified staff.
- **Breadth**: security technology and alarm systems, fire protection, legal foundations,
  risk assessment, planning a guarding concept rather than executing one.

## What the job involves {#what-the-job-involves}

Beyond guarding: assessing a site and designing the guarding concept, specifying and
operating security technology, coordinating teams, liaising with police and fire services,
managing incidents and the documentation that follows, and increasingly access-control and
CCTV systems administration under **DSGVO** constraints — surveillance is heavily
regulated in Germany and getting it wrong is a data-protection matter.

## Qualification route {#qualification-route}

- **3 years dual**, security firm plus Berufsschule, **IHK** exam
- **Entry:** mittlerer Schulabschluss typical; formally open
- **Paid throughout:** roughly €1,000–1,250/month gross
- **Ladder:** **Geprüfte Schutz- und Sicherheitskraft (IHK)**, **Meister für Schutz und
  Sicherheit**, or Fachwirt routes into security management

## Pay {#pay}

**Market estimates** — see the [sector README](/jobs-in-germany/security/) on the AEntG branch minimum:

| Stage | Gross/month |
|---|---|
| Newly qualified | €2,700 – €3,400 |
| Experienced | €3,100 – €3,900 |
| Objektleitung / Einsatzleitung | €3,500 – €4,500 |
| Meister für Schutz und Sicherheit | €4,000 – €5,200 |
| Security management, corporate | €4,500 – €6,500 |

Corporate **Werkschutz** — in-house security at industrial sites — pays above contracted
guarding and often sits under the site's own tariff, which is a large difference.

## Demand {#demand}

Strong and growing: critical-infrastructure protection, data-centre and logistics sites,
events, and public-sector contracts. The contracted end of the sector competes hard on price,
which suppresses pay; the in-house and specialist end does not.

## Foreign candidates {#foreign-candidates}

1. **To work:** §34a compliance as for any security role. No professional recognition needed.
2. **For the visa:** **this qualification is recognisable** — IHK FOSA equivalence, then
   §18a. That is the key difference from the unqualified route.
3. **§34a Sachkundeprüfung** is still a German legal exam.
4. **Reliability check** requires a clean record, including from your home country.
5. **Language: B2.** Reports, legal limits, DSGVO obligations and coordination with
   authorities.

## Pitfalls {#pitfalls}

- **Contracted guarding vs in-house Werkschutz** is the biggest pay difference in the sector.
- **The qualification unlocks progression, not powers** — the §34a tiers still apply.
- **DSGVO exposure** is real for anyone running CCTV or access data.
- **Price-driven contracts** mean some employers cut corners on training and equipment; ask
  what the firm funds.
