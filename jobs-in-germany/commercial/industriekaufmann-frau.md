> The commercial backbone of German manufacturing: purchasing, production planning, sales, controlling and HR inside industrial firms. Better paid than general office work for the same reason everything in German industry is — the tariff.

<div class="jig-facts"></div>

|---|---|
| **Official title** | Industriekaufmann/-frau |
| **Protected title** | **No** |
| **Category** | commercial |

| **Regulated** | No |

## What the job involves {#what-the-job-involves}

Rotation through a manufacturer's commercial functions: **Einkauf** (supplier negotiation, order management), **Produktionsplanung** (scheduling, materials), **Vertrieb** (quotations, customer management), **Controlling** (cost accounting, reporting), **Personal** (HR administration), and **Finanzbuchhaltung**.

The defining feature is proximity to physical production. You are costing and scheduling real goods, which is why the qualification leads naturally into supply chain, controlling and operations management rather than generic admin.

**SAP** is the standard environment in most industrial employers. SAP experience is the single most portable skill the job gives you.

## Qualification route {#qualification-route}

- **3 years dual**, in a manufacturing company plus Berufsschule
- **Entry:** mittlerer Schulabschluss or Abitur; Abitur is common in larger firms
- **Paid throughout:** roughly **€1,100–1,300/month rising to €1,300–1,500** in the final year at IG Metall employers — among the best-paid Ausbildungen in Germany
- **Ends with** the IHK Abschlussprüfung, including a practical project report
- **Afterwards:** Industriefachwirt, Betriebswirt (IHK), Controlling specialisms, or a part-time Bachelor. Larger firms often part-fund these.

## Pay {#pay}

Mostly covered by **IG Metall** or **IG BCE** collective agreements, which matter more than individual negotiation:

| Stage | Gross/month |
|---|---|
| Newly qualified (tariff-bound) | €3,200 – €4,000 |
| Experienced | €4,000 – €5,000 |
| Specialist — controlling, strategic purchasing | €4,800 – €6,000 |
| Team or department lead | €6,000+ |

Tariff employers add a **13th-month payment**, holiday pay, and a **35-hour week** in the metal and electrical industries. Compare total package, not base salary — the difference against a non-tariff employer is often 20–30% once these are counted.

Note the contrast with [Kaufmann/-frau für Büromanagement](/jobs-in-germany/commercial/kaufmann-frau-fuer-bueromanagement/): similar commercial content, materially better pay, because of the sector and its agreements.

## Demand and outlook {#demand-and-outlook}

Solid but not a shortage occupation in the way nursing or driving are. Worth being clear about the sectoral risk: German industry — automotive above all — is in a structural transition, and commercial roles at suppliers exposed to combustion-engine production carry real medium-term uncertainty. Purchasing, supply chain and controlling skills transfer well; a career tied to one shrinking supplier does not.

## Foreign candidates {#foreign-candidates}

1. **To work:** nothing required. Unregulated.
2. **For the visa:** **IHK FOSA** equivalence assessment. A foreign business degree may be assessed as comparable via **anabin**, which can also open the **EU Blue Card** route — unusually for this repo, senior commercial salaries in industry can clear the threshold.
3. **Language: B2–C1.** Internal German is standard at most Mittelstand employers; large corporates run some functions in English, and **purchasing and sales often need both**, since suppliers and customers are international.

## Pitfalls {#pitfalls}

- **Tariff vs. non-tariff** is the biggest variable in the offer. Always ask which agreement applies and whether the firm is a member of the employers' association.
- **Mittelstand firms are excellent employers and invisible abroad.** The best openings are rarely at companies you have heard of.
- **Sector concentration risk** — see above. Diversify your skills toward functions rather than toward one product line.
- **A foreign BBA is not a German Ausbildung** in recognition terms, and vice versa; the two systems assess different things.
