> Genuinely unlike anything else in this repo. A notary is **appointed by the state to a public office**, in a number fixed by assessed need and tied to a specific location, holds a **legal monopoly** over authenticating the transactions that matter most in German life, and charges fees set by statute with **no room to compete on price at all**.

## What only a notary may do {#what-only-a-notary-may-do}

German law requires **notarielle Beurkundung** — authentication by a notary — for the transactions where the state has decided that advice and formality must be forced on the parties:

- **Land and property transactions.** Every sale of a house or flat. No exceptions
- **Formation and share transfers of a GmbH**, and most company-law resolutions
- **Marriage contracts, and agreements on the consequences of divorce**
- **Erbverträge** and many inheritance instruments
- **Acknowledgements of paternity, adoption consents**
- **Grundbuch and Handelsregister applications**, which pass through the notary

The notary is required to be **impartial** — advising both sides, not representing either — and to explain the legal consequences before anyone signs. That is the justification for the monopoly: the formality exists to protect the weaker party, and the office-holder is paid to be neutral.

**No other profession in this repo holds a reserved activity this wide**, and none of it can be done by a lawyer who is not also a notary.

## Two systems, by Bundesland {#two-systems-by-bundesland}

This is the first thing to establish, because the career is completely different in each:

| | **Nurnotariat** | **Anwaltsnotariat** |
|---|---|---|
| Who | A full-time notary and nothing else | A practising **Rechtsanwalt** who is also appointed as a notary |
| Where | Most of Germany, including Bayern, Baden-Württemberg (since its reform), Rheinland-Pfalz, Saarland, the eastern states, Hamburg | Parts of the north and west, including Niedersachsen, Bremen, Schleswig-Holstein, Hessen, Berlin and parts of NRW |
| Route | **Notarassessor** — a salaried preparatory service of several years after the state exams | Practise as a lawyer for a qualifying period, then apply |
| Selection | A **ranked list**, on exam grades plus a notarial examination | Professional experience plus a notarial examination |

Which system applies is fixed by the Bundesland, so — as with [teaching](/jobs-in-germany/education/lehrer-in/), [Besoldung](/jobs-in-germany/reference/beamte-vs-angestellte/) and [the Architektenkammern](/jobs-in-germany/engineering/architekt-in/) — **the state decides the career**, not the federation. See [choosing a Bundesland](/jobs-in-germany/reference/bundeslaender/).

## The numerus clausus {#the-numerus-clausus}

Appointments are made only where a post exists. The state assesses the **need** for notarial services in a district and creates or withholds posts accordingly, so a vacancy generally arises only when someone retires or dies.

The consequences:

- **Entry is by competition for a specific location**, not by qualifying in the abstract
- **You go where the post is.** Geographic flexibility is a condition of the career
- **Exam grades matter for a very long time.** The ranking that decides Notarassessor places rests heavily on the two state examinations, taken years earlier

It is the same rationing logic as the [Kassensitz](/jobs-in-germany/healthcare/psychotherapeut-in/) in psychotherapy, with one crucial difference: **a notarial post is not bought and sold.** It is a public office, allocated by the state. Whatever else can be said about the cap, it is not tradeable.

## Fees — the exact opposite of the HOAI story {#fees--the-exact-opposite-of-the-hoai-story}

Notarial fees are fixed by the **Gerichts- und Notarkostengesetz (GNotKG)**, scaled to the value of the transaction. A notary **may not negotiate them, discount them, or waive them.**

Put that beside [Architekt/in](/jobs-in-germany/engineering/architekt-in/), whose binding fee scale the European Court of Justice struck down in 2019 for restricting competition. The notarial tariff survives because a notary is not exercising a freely provided service — they hold a public office, and the fees are part of its regulation. Two professions, two fee schedules, two opposite legal outcomes in the same decade.

For the reader, the practical effect: **every notary costs the same**, so choose on competence and availability, and there is no point shopping around on price.

## The route {#the-route}

1. **Jura**, then the **Erstes Staatsexamen**
2. **Referendariat** — the practical legal training — then the **Zweites Staatsexamen**. You are now a *Volljurist*
3. **In Nurnotariat states:** apply for the **Notarassessor** service — salaried, several years, ranked entry — and sit the **notarielle Fachprüfung**
4. **In Anwaltsnotariat states:** practise as a lawyer for the qualifying period, pass the notarial examination, apply when a post is advertised
5. **Appointment** by the state justice administration, to a place

Above and alongside: **Notarkammer** membership is compulsory, and the pension runs through the [Rechtsanwalts- or Notarversorgung](/jobs-in-germany/reference/versorgungswerke/), not the statutory scheme.

## Pay {#pay}

**Market estimate**, and unusually hard to generalise, because a notary is an office-holder running an office: the income is fee revenue minus the cost of premises and staff.

| Stage | Gross/month |
|---|---|
| **Notarassessor** (salaried preparatory service) | €4,500 – €6,000 |
| Newly appointed notary, smaller district | Highly variable; the office's costs come first |
| Established notary | **High** — commonly well into six figures annually, before tax and office costs |
| Anwaltsnotar | Law practice income plus notarial fees |

Two honest qualifications. Fee income tracks **property transaction volumes and company formations**, so it fell materially in the 2022–2024 property downturn. And the **Notarfachangestellte/r** staff who run the office — an Ausbildung closely related to [Rechtsanwaltsfachangestellte/r](/jobs-in-germany/commercial/rechtsanwaltsfachangestellte-r/) — are a real and scarce cost: notarial offices compete for them.

## Demand and outlook {#demand-and-outlook}

**Capped by design, and therefore stable.** The number of notaries does not respond to demand, which insulates the profession from competition and also from growth.

- **Succession is the opportunity.** A substantial share of notaries reach retirement this decade, and posts in rural and smaller districts are the ones that go unfilled — the same pattern as [rural dentistry](/jobs-in-germany/healthcare/zahnarzt-zahnaerztin/).
- **Digitalisation has arrived**: online GmbH formation and electronic authentication procedures now exist, with notarial involvement retained rather than removed. The office changed; the monopoly did not.
- **Transaction volumes** are the cyclical variable.

## Foreign candidates {#foreign-candidates}

**Be clear-eyed: this is among the least accessible professions here, and the obstacle is the German legal qualification, not nationality.**

1. **You need both German state law examinations.** There is no equivalence route for a foreign law degree into the notarial office — German substantive law *is* the qualification.
2. **Nationality is no longer the barrier it was.** Following a 2011 European Court of Justice ruling that the nationality requirement breached freedom of establishment, Germany amended the BNotO, and **EU citizens may be appointed**. Third-country nationals cannot.
3. **Language: native-level German.** The office consists of drafting, reading aloud and explaining legal instruments, and of being understood by people who are not lawyers.
4. **A realistic alternative** for foreign-trained lawyers is the [Notarfachangestellte/r](/jobs-in-germany/commercial/rechtsanwaltsfachangestellte-r/) route into a notarial office, or qualifying as a **Rechtsanwalt** through the European lawyers' routes, which do exist where the notarial ones do not.
5. **Visa:** §18b for legal employment generally; the notarial appointment itself is a public office, not a visa route.

## Pitfalls {#pitfalls}

- **Decide the Bundesland before the career.** Nurnotariat and Anwaltsnotariat are different professional lives.
- **Exam grades from your twenties follow you for a decade** in the Nurnotariat ranking.
- **You will move to where a post is**, possibly to a small town.
- **The office is a business with fixed prices.** Costs are the only variable you control.
- **Impartiality is the job.** A notary who thinks of the paying party as a client has misunderstood the office, and the professional supervision takes that seriously.
