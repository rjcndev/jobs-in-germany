## The permits, and who needs one {#the-permits-and-who-needs-one}

| Paragraph | Covers | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **§34d** | Insurance intermediation and advice | The main one |
| **§34f** | Investment intermediation — funds, closed-end products | Separate Sachkundeprüfung |
| **§34i** | Residential mortgage intermediation | Separate again |
| **§34h** | **Honorar** (fee-based) insurance advice | Mutually exclusive with commission-based §34d |

Each requires: **reliability**, **orderly financial circumstances**, **professional indemnity
insurance**, and an **IHK Sachkundeprüfung** — the one for insurance being the
*Versicherungsfachmann/-frau (IHK)* examination. Holders are entered in a **public
intermediaries register**, which anyone, including a customer, can search.

Then **15 hours of continuing training every year**, required by the EU Insurance
Distribution Directive and enforced through the register.

### The distinction that decides your career {#the-distinction-that-decides-your-career}

| | **Gebundener Vertreter** (tied agent) | **Makler** (broker) |
|---|---|---|
| Acts for | **One insurer** (or a few) | **The customer** |
| Permit | **Exempt** — the insurer registers you and is liable for you | **Own §34d permit**, own PI insurance |
| Product range | That insurer's | The market's |
| Liability | The insurer's | **Yours** |
| Independence | Limited | Real, and it is the profession's selling point |

Most people start as employees or tied agents and never need their own permit. Becoming a
broker is the step that turns this into a regulated business of your own — with the duty to
advise in the customer's interest, documented, and the liability that follows from getting it
wrong.

## What the job involves {#what-the-job-involves}

**Fachrichtung Versicherung** — advising on and placing cover; risk assessment; claims
handling, which is where an insurer's reputation is actually made or lost; commercial lines
for business customers; and increasingly compliance and documentation, because every piece of
advice must be recorded with its reasoning.

**Fachrichtung Finanzberatung** — investment and provision products, occupational pensions
(**betriebliche Altersversorgung**), and the retirement-planning advice that interacts with
everything in [health insurance](/jobs-in-germany/reference/health-insurance/),
[tax](/jobs-in-germany/reference/taxes-and-net-pay/) and the statutory pension.

Employers: insurers' own offices and head offices, tied agencies, independent brokerages,
banks' insurance arms, and industrial insurance brokers — the last being the best-paid and
least-known corner of the sector.

## Pay {#pay}

**Market estimate.** Insurers' own staff are frequently covered by the insurance industry's
collective agreement, which is comparatively good; agency and sales roles are commission-led.

| Role | Gross/month |
|---|---|
| Entry, insurer's office | €3,000 – €3,600 |
| Experienced, specialist lines or claims | €3,700 – €4,700 |
| **Industrial / commercial broking**, experienced | €4,500 – €6,500 |
| Agency sales | Base plus commission — **highly variable**, and the base is often low |
| Self-employed broker | A business result |
| Head-office roles (underwriting, actuarial support, product) | €4,500 – €6,500 |

**Look hard at the commission structure** in any sales role: how much is base, when
commission vests, and whether it is clawed back if a policy lapses. Clawback (*Stornohaftung*)
runs for years on life and provision products and is the single most common unpleasant
surprise in this occupation.

## Demand and outlook {#demand-and-outlook}

**Stable, consolidating, and demographically interesting.**

- **The intermediary population is ageing sharply**, and a large number of agencies and
  brokerages will change hands this decade with no obvious successor. For someone qualified
  and willing to take on a book of business, that is a genuine opportunity — and it is
  discussed openly in the industry.
- **Consolidation** continues: fewer, larger brokers; insurers pruning tied networks.
- **Digitalisation** removes simple products — motor and travel cover sold through
  comparison sites — and leaves the advice-intensive ones: commercial risk, occupational
  disability, provision planning, and claims. Those are the parts to be good at.
- **Occupational pensions** are a growth area, driven by statutory employer obligations.

Honest counterweight: the sales end of this industry has a reputation problem it partly
earned, and a career built on commission-driven product sales is both less secure and less
respected than one built on commercial or industrial broking.

## Foreign candidates {#foreign-candidates}

1. **To work: no recognition required**, and as an employee or tied agent, **no permit**.
2. **For the visa: yes** — §18a, assessed by **IHK FOSA**.
3. **To advise independently:** your own **§34d** (and §34f / §34i as applicable) permit,
   with the **IHK Sachkundeprüfung in German**, PI insurance and register entry.
4. **Language: C1.** Advice is documented and legally consequential, the exam is German, and
   the products are German legal constructs with no clean translation.
5. **Visa:** §18a, or **§16a** to train here. **Blue Card** only at senior head-office level.
6. **Foreign insurance qualifications transfer poorly** — the substance is German contract,
   tax and social-insurance law.

## Pitfalls {#pitfalls}

- **Know whether you are tied or independent** before signing. It determines your permit,
  your liability and whose interest you serve.
- **Stornohaftung is real.** Ask how long, and on which products.
- **The CPD obligation is annual and evidenced**, not a formality.
- **§34d does not cover investments.** Selling funds needs §34f; mortgages need §34i.
- **The fee-based route (§34h) is exclusive.** You cannot take commission and fees for the
  same activity.
