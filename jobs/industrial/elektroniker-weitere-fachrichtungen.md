# Elektroniker — the remaining Fachrichtungen, and the tier below

> The repo already has the two electrical anchors:
> [Elektroniker für Betriebstechnik](elektroniker-in-betriebstechnik.md) on the industrial

> on the Handwerk side. This file covers what is left: the **automation** specialisation,

## First, the rule that governs all of them

Electrical work in Germany is gated by competence, not by title. Under **DGUV V3** and
VDE 1000-10, only an **Elektrofachkraft (EFK)** may work independently on electrical
installations. Everyone else is an **elektrotechnisch unterwiesene Person (EuP)** and may
only carry out defined tasks under instruction.

Completing any of the qualifications below makes you an EFK for the scope you were trained
in. It is the most consequential of the
[occupational certificates](../../reference/occupational-certificates.md) in this repo,
because it decides who may legally touch the work at all — and it does **not** come with a
foreign electrical qualification automatically.

## Elektroniker/in für Automatisierungstechnik

**The control systems, not the power.** Where
[Betriebstechnik](elektroniker-in-betriebstechnik.md) keeps a plant's electrical
infrastructure running, automation builds and programmes what runs on it.

- **SPS/PLC programming** — Siemens TIA Portal above all, plus Beckhoff, Rockwell
- **Sensors, actuators, drives** and their parameterisation
- **Bus systems** — PROFINET, PROFIBUS, EtherCAT, IO-Link
- **Commissioning (Inbetriebnahme)**, often at the customer's site and frequently abroad
- **Fault-finding on running production**, where the cost of downtime sets the tempo
- **Functional safety** — safety controllers, emergency-stop architecture, and the
  performance-level assessments that go with them

**It is the most portable and best-paid of the electrical trades**, and the one with the
clearest route into engineering-adjacent work: Techniker, then automation engineering,
without necessarily going through a degree. It is also the one most likely to send you
abroad on commissioning trips, which suits some people and not others — and which carries
tax-free **Spesen** that the [pay table](../../reference/pay.md) does not show.

The Handwerk has its own near-equivalent, **Elektroniker Fachrichtung Automatisierungs- und
Systemtechnik**, with the same trap the repo records elsewhere: the industrial qualification
pays more and confers **no right to open an electrical contracting business**.

## Elektroniker/in für Gebäudesystemintegration

**New in 2021**, and the only genuinely new electrical trade in a generation. It exists
because buildings stopped being electrically simple.

The work is the integration layer of a modern building: **KNX**, **BACnet**, **Modbus** and
DALI; heating, ventilation, shading, lighting and access control brought under one system;
energy management tying together heat pumps, PV, storage and EV charging; commissioning,
parameterising and handing over the whole thing to a client who will need it to keep working
for twenty years.

Two things make it worth knowing:

1. **It is a Handwerk trade** (3.5 years, HWK), so it sits in the
   [Anlage A electrical world](../skilled-trades/elektroniker-in-energie-und-gebaeudetechnik.md)
   — employment free, self-employment needing the Meister.
2. **It is where the Energiewende meets the building**, alongside
   [SHK](../skilled-trades/anlagenmechaniker-in-shk.md) and
   [Dachdecker](../skilled-trades/dachdecker-in.md). Heat pumps, PV and charging
   infrastructure all need someone who can make the systems talk to each other, and there
   are very few such people.

Being new, it is also poorly recognised by employers and by foreign-qualification
assessments. Expect to explain it.

## Industrieelektriker/in — the two-year tier

A **two-year** IHK Ausbildung in two Fachrichtungen, **Betriebstechnik** and **Geräte und
Systeme**. It is a full, recognised qualification — not a partial one — and it makes you an
Elektrofachkraft **for a defined, narrower scope**.

**This mirrors [Fachlagerist vs Fachkraft für
Lagerlogistik](../logistics/fachkraft-fuer-lagerlogistik.md) exactly**, and the same
advice applies:

| | Industrieelektriker (2 yrs) | Elektroniker (3.5 yrs) |
|---|---|---|
| Scope as EFK | Narrower, defined | Broad |
| Pay | Lower — a grade or two below | Higher |
| Progression | **Credited toward the 3.5-year qualification** | — |
| Visa (§18a) | **Qualifies** — it meets the two-year minimum | Qualifies |

**Do not stop there if you can avoid it.** The two-year route exists to bring people into
the trade, and employers use it both to widen recruitment and — less benignly — to staff
work at a lower grade. Continuing to the full Elektroniker qualification is normal, credited,
and worth several hundred euro a month for the rest of a career.

Its one clear advantage: at two years it **does** meet the §18a threshold, which one-year
qualifications elsewhere in this repo do not.

## Pay

**Market estimate.** These are IG Metall / IG BCE occupations where they are tariff-bound,
and as the [sector README](README.md) records, those agreements are regionally fragmented
with employer-specific ERA grading — there is no single scale to verify against.

| Qualification | Entry | Experienced | Ceiling |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Industrieelektriker** | €2,900 – €3,400 | €3,300 – €3,900 | €4,200 – €5,000 |
| **Elektroniker Automatisierungstechnik** | €3,400 – €4,000 | €4,100 – €4,900 | €5,600 – €7,000 (Techniker, Inbetriebnehmer, Projektleitung) |
| **Elektroniker Gebäudesystemintegration** | €3,000 – €3,600 | €3,600 – €4,400 | €4,300 – €5,400 (Meister) |

Automation is the highest-paying non-degree electrical route here, and commissioning work
adds per-diems on top.

## Demand and outlook

**Strong across all three, for different reasons.**

- **Automation** — reshoring, labour shortage and the push to automate production keep
  demand high, and the skill is transferable across industries and borders.
- **Gebäudesystemintegration** — demand outstrips the tiny number of qualified people by a
  wide margin, and every heat pump, PV array and charging point adds to it.
- **Industrieelektriker** — used heavily as a recruitment channel; secure, but it is a floor
  rather than a career.

The honest risk, as in
[Betriebstechnik](elektroniker-in-betriebstechnik.md): German industrial employment is under
pressure from energy costs and the automotive transition, and plant closures do happen.
Automation skills travel between sectors better than most, which is the best hedge available
in this family.

## Foreign candidates

1. **To work: no recognition required** — except that you must be an **Elektrofachkraft**,
   and a foreign electrical qualification does not make you one automatically. Employers
   assess it, and may require documented German instruction or a bridging qualification.
   This is the real barrier, not the paperwork.
2. **For the visa: yes.** **IHK FOSA** for the industrial qualifications, **Handwerkskammer**
   for Gebäudesystemintegration.
3. **Self-employment**: only the Handwerk route leads there. The industrial qualifications
   confer no right to open an electrical business.
4. **Language: B1–B2.** VDE standards, safety instruction and shift handover are German;
   commissioning teams abroad often work in English.
5. **Visa:** §18a, or **§16a** to train here. **Blue Card no.**
6. **Hochvolt and other short tickets do not transfer** — see
   [occupational certificates](../../reference/occupational-certificates.md).

## Pitfalls

- **Check which chamber and which system** a vacancy sits in. The words overlap; the rights
  do not.
- **The two-year qualification is a starting point.** Get the continuation agreed in writing
  if an employer promises it.
- **EFK status is scope-bound.** Being an Elektrofachkraft for control cabinets does not make
  you one for building installations.
- **Gebäudesystemintegration is new enough to be misunderstood** by recruiters and by
  equivalence assessors. Bring the Ausbildungsordnung with you.
- **Commissioning travel is a lifestyle**, not an occasional trip. Establish how many weeks a
  year before accepting.
