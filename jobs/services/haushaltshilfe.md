# Haushaltshilfe (Domestic Cleaner / Household Help)

> The narrowest of the three cleaning tiers and the one with a genuine legal story:
> **the overwhelming majority of domestic work in Germany is undeclared.** It is included
> here precisely because the default is Schwarzarbeit — with no accident cover, no pension
> credit and no sick pay for the worker — and because the legal alternative is much cheaper
> than almost anyone believes.

## The default is illegal, and everyone knows it

Estimates of the share of domestic cleaning in Germany that is undeclared run to the large
majority — it is one of the biggest Schwarzarbeit markets in the country. Households pay
cash, nobody registers anything, and both sides assume the alternative is complicated and
expensive.

**It is neither.** That is the core of this file.

## The three legal ways to do it

| Arrangement | How it works | Who carries what |
|---|---|---|
| **Haushaltsscheck Minijob** | The household registers with the **Minijob-Zentrale**, which calculates contributions and collects them by direct debit twice a year. No payroll to run | The household is the **employer**, with the duties that implies |
| **Employment above the Minijob ceiling** | Ordinary employment with full social insurance | More administration; full cover for the worker |
| **Engaging a firm or a registered self-employed cleaner** | You buy a service and receive an **invoice** | No employer duties; the cleaner's own business carries them |

For the first route, the employer-side flat rate is roughly **half** the commercial Minijob
rate — in the order of 15% of gross, **including accident insurance**.

**And §35a EStG gives much of it back.** The household deducts **20% of the cost directly
from its tax bill**: up to €510 a year for a Haushaltsscheck Minijob, and up to €4,000 a year
for an invoiced service or employment above the Minijob limit — the larger figures requiring
an invoice and **payment by bank transfer**. Cash kills the deduction, which is the design.

The arithmetic is set out in
[Minijob and geringfügige Beschäftigung](../../reference/minijob-und-geringfuegige-beschaeftigung.md).
The short version: for a household Minijob at the ceiling, the levies and the tax credit
substantially cancel each other out.

## What being undeclared costs the worker

This is the part that rarely gets said plainly, and it falls almost entirely on one side of
the arrangement:

| | Undeclared | Haushaltsscheck |
|---|---|---|
| **Accident insurance** | **None.** Fall off a ladder in someone's home and there is no cover, no rehabilitation, no wage replacement | **Full**, included in the flat rate, from the first hour |
| **Pension credit** | None | Yes — small, but it counts toward the qualifying periods |
| **Sick pay, paid holiday** | None in practice | **Legal entitlement** — this is employment |
| **Proof of income** | None — which blocks tenancy applications, credit and some residence procedures | A payslip and an annual statement |
| **Unemployment cover** | None | None either — Minijobs build none. See the reference |
| **Legal exposure** | Both parties; for a non-EU worker it can also **endanger residence status** | None |

The household's exposure is a fine. The worker's exposure is their body, their pension and,
potentially, their permission to stay. The asymmetry is the argument.

## Live-in care — the part of this market nobody labels correctly

The largest and most legally fraught segment of private-household work in Germany is not
cleaning. It is **round-the-clock care of older people at home**, usually by women from
central and eastern Europe, working and living in the household for weeks at a time before
rotating out.

It is typically arranged through agencies as a **posting** by a foreign employer, which
places it under the posted-worker rules described in
[The Bauhauptgewerbe](../../reference/bauhauptgewerbe.md) — a construction reference, but the
same AEntG and A1 machinery.

**The decisive legal point:** German labour courts have held that **on-call time in live-in
care counts as working time and must be paid**, at least at the minimum wage. Arrangements
advertising "24-hour care" for a flat monthly fee cannot be reconciled with that, and the
sector has been in slow legal adjustment ever since.

If you are considering this work: establish **who your employer is**, which country's law
and social insurance apply, whether there is an **A1 certificate**, and how the hours are
recorded. If nobody can answer, the answer is bad.

## Pay

**Market estimate.** There is no sector agreement for private households, so the statutory
**Mindestlohn** is the floor — a perishable figure, not quoted here.

| | Reality |
|---|---|
| Undeclared cash rate | Commonly quoted per hour, above the minimum wage — which is how the arrangement is sold to the worker, and it is a bad trade against everything in the table above |
| Haushaltsscheck Minijob | The Minijob ceiling caps monthly earnings per household; several households is the normal pattern |
| Registered self-employed cleaner | Sets their own rate, invoices, and carries their own insurance and pension |

**Working for several households is how this work reaches a living income**, and it is also
how people end up unintentionally over the Minijob ceiling — the ceiling is tested per
worker, not per household, so multiple Minijobs add together.

## For people who have just arrived

1. **This work supports no visa.** There is no qualification and therefore no §18a route.
2. **Check your residence permit before working**, and be aware that undeclared work can
   directly jeopardise your status — a risk the household does not share.
3. **Ask to be registered via the Haushaltsscheck.** It costs the household very little after
   §35a, and the request is entirely reasonable. Bring the numbers.
4. **Language: A2 is enough for the work**, more for arranging it yourself.
5. **If you want a career in cleaning**, the route runs through the
   [Gebäudereiniger](gebaeudereiniger-in.md) qualification, not through more households.

## Pitfalls

- **"It is easier for both of us this way" is not easier for you.** The risks are not
  symmetrical.
- **Accident insurance is the single strongest argument.** It is included, it is cheap, and
  it is the thing you cannot buy back after an accident.
- **Multiple Minijobs add up** against one ceiling.
- **Live-in care is not a Minijob** and should never be treated as one. Establish the
  employer, the hours and the A1.
- **Keep your own record of hours and payments**, in every arrangement.
