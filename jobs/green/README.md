# Grüne Berufe — agriculture, forestry and horticulture

Fourteen recognised occupations covering farming, forestry, horticulture, viticulture,
livestock and fisheries. They sit outside both systems the rest of this repo describes.

## A third chamber

The [skilled trades](../skilled-trades/) answer to the **Handwerkskammer** and the
[industrial](../industrial/) occupations to the **IHK**. The Grüne Berufe answer to the
**Landwirtschaftskammer** — training supervision, examinations, and equivalence assessment
of foreign qualifications.

**And only in some Bundesländer.** Where no Landwirtschaftskammer exists, a state ministry
or Regierungspräsidium is the competent body instead. So this sector combines a third
chamber system *with* the sixteen-state fragmentation seen in
[Erzieher](../education/erzieher-in.md) and [Lehrer](../education/lehrer-in.md).

Identify the competent body for your target Bundesland before anything else.

## What else is distinctive

- **Qualification gates.** Applying pesticides commercially requires the
  **Pflanzenschutz-Sachkunde** certificate; chainsaw work requires documented training.
  Neither is implied by the job title, and neither transfers automatically from abroad.
- **Seasonal migrant labour.** Harvest work runs largely on the **70-day short-term
  employment rule**, social-insurance-free and overwhelmingly staffed from Romania and
  Poland. It is a structural feature of German agriculture with a documented enforcement
  record, and it is not the same thing as the qualified occupations profiled here.
- **Weather and physical risk** are everyday working conditions, not footnotes.
- **Public-sector employment is common** in forestry, which makes pay tariff-verifiable —
  unusually for a manual trade.
