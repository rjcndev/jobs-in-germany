# Fliesen-, Platten- und Mosaikleger/in (Tiler)

> The trade at the centre of Germany's deregulation experiment. Freed from the Meisterpflicht
> in 2004, it saw its business numbers multiply and its apprentice numbers collapse, and in
> 2020 it was re-regulated — the single best-documented example of Germany reversing a
> deregulation. [Raumausstatter](raumausstatter-in.md) carries the full account of that
> reversal; this file is the trade.

## What deregulation did here

After 2004 this trade became **Anlage B1** — anyone could register a business. What followed
is the clearest evidence in the whole debate:

- **Business numbers multiplied several times over** within a few years, overwhelmingly
  **solo operators** with no employees
- **A large share were registrations by nationals of the 2004 accession states**, for whom a
  German trade registration was also a route to working here legally. Much of that work was
  genuine; a documented part of it was **Scheinselbstständigkeit** — nominal self-employment
  masking what was in substance employment, with the social-insurance contributions missing
- **Apprentice numbers fell sharply**, because a business without a Meister cannot train
- Complaints about defective work rose, and tiling is a trade where a defect —
  a failed waterproofing layer behind a shower — surfaces years later and costs a
  bathroom

That combination is why Fliesenleger was near the top of the list when the Meisterpflicht was
restored in **February 2020**. Existing businesses were **grandfathered**, so the trade still
contains owners operating lawfully without a Meisterbrief alongside newer ones who needed it.

**Employment was never affected.** You can work as a tiler for someone else with no
qualification requirement at all, before 2004, between 2004 and 2020, and today.

## What the job involves

More technical than it looks from outside, and the technical part is where the money and the
liability both sit:

- **Untergrundvorbereitung** — assessing and preparing screed and substrate. Most tiling
  failures begin here
- **Verbundabdichtung** — the bonded waterproofing layer beneath tiles in wet areas, governed
  by **DIN 18534**. This is the safety-critical element of the trade and the usual subject of
  defect claims
- **Großformate** — large-format panels, thin porcelain slabs, and the handling and levelling
  systems they require. The biggest change in the trade in two decades
- **Naturstein** and mosaic, with different cutting, fixing and sealing rules
- **Bewegungsfugen**, tile layout and setting out, cutting and drilling
- **Renovation** — removal, substrate repair, and working in occupied homes
- Increasingly, **heated floors** and the build-up constraints they impose

Most work is bathroom and kitchen renovation, commercial interiors, and new-build fit-out.

## Tariff world

The Fliesenlegerhandwerk sits inside the construction **Sozialkassen** system: **SOKA-BAU**
holiday that travels between employers, the sector's supplementary pension, the funded
winter, and the AEntG minimum wage above the statutory one. Read
[The Bauhauptgewerbe](../../reference/bauhauptgewerbe.md) — most of it applies here even
though tiling is finishing work rather than structural.

## Knees — a recognised occupational disease

Tiling is kneeling work, and Germany recognises the consequence formally: **BK 2112,
Gonarthrose**, knee osteoarthritis caused by long-term kneeling, is a listed occupational
disease with a documented exposure threshold in the order of **13,000 hours** over a working
life.

That is not a warning in the abstract. It means:

- the disease is **compensable** through **BG BAU** if you can evidence the exposure — so
  **keep your employment record**
- knee protection, kneeling boards and rotation are employer obligations, not personal
  preferences
- and a tiler's career has a foreseeable physical end date unless it changes shape

Alongside it: **quartz dust** from cutting, which requires extraction or wet cutting; manual
handling of large-format panels, which is a two-person operation; and the solvents and
adhesives.

## Pay

**Market estimate.**

| Stage | Gross/month |
|---|---|
| Geselle, entry | €2,800 – €3,400 |
| Experienced | €3,300 – €4,000 |
| Specialist — large format, natural stone, high-end bathrooms | €3,800 – €4,600 |
| **Meister**, employed | €4,000 – €5,000 |
| Meister with own business | Not comparable |

Good tilers are genuinely scarce and charge accordingly; this is one of the trades where
piece-rate and bonus arrangements are common, and where a skilled self-employed operator's
day rate has risen well above the employed equivalent. Ask how any bonus is calculated, and
whether it survives a rain-delayed or badly prepared site.

## Demand and outlook

**Strong, and insulated from the new-build contraction** because the work is mostly
renovation. Bathroom renovation in an ageing housing stock, accessible and
barrier-free conversions for an ageing population, and commercial fit-out all continue
regardless of housing starts.

The shortage of qualified tilers is severe, and the 2020 re-regulation was partly aimed at
rebuilding the training pipeline that deregulation had emptied. Apprentice numbers have
recovered somewhat; the gap left by fifteen years of under-training has not closed.

## Foreign candidates

1. **To work: no recognition required.**
2. **To open a business: the Meisterbrief applies to you now.** If you are coming from a
   country where you operated independently as a tiler, this is the change that most directly
   affects you, and grandfathering does not extend to new registrations. See
   [the Meister file](../../reference/meister.md) for **§7b** and the other routes.
3. **Be careful with "self-employment" offers.** If a firm proposes that you register a trade
   and invoice them for work they direct, that is the Scheinselbstständigkeit pattern this
   trade is known for. The consequences — back-dated social insurance, and no employment
   rights meanwhile — fall on both parties but hurt you more.
4. **For the visa: yes** — **Handwerkskammer** equivalence for §18a.
5. **Language: B1–B2.** Site coordination, and domestic customers whose bathroom you are in.
6. **Visa:** §18a, **§16a**, or **§26(2) BeschV**.

## Pitfalls

- **Waterproofing is where liability lives.** Know DIN 18534 and document what you installed.
- **Pre-2020 guidance about opening a business is wrong.**
- **Record your kneeling exposure** across employers. BK 2112 claims turn on evidence you
  will not be able to reconstruct in twenty years.
- **Large-format panels are a two-person lift** and a different skill set; firms that treat
  them as ordinary tiling break a lot of material.
- **Check the contract's agreement and your SOKA-BAU number** — tiling is inside the
  construction social funds, and small firms sometimes do not explain that.
