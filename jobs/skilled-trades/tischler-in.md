## Tischler or Schreiner — the same trade, geographically

Uniquely in this repo, the official title **depends on where in Germany you are**:

- **Tischler/in** — north and east: Lower Saxony, Schleswig-Holstein, Hamburg, Berlin,
  Brandenburg, Saxony, Mecklenburg-Vorpommern, NRW's northern parts
- **Schreiner/in** — south and west: Bavaria, Baden-Württemberg, Hesse, Rhineland-Palatinate,
  Saarland

Both are the same Anlage A trade with the same Ausbildung and the same Meisterbrief. The
Handwerkskammer in each region uses its own term, as do job adverts. **Search both words**,
and do not read a regional variant on a certificate as a different qualification.

This is a geographic split, not a historical one like
[Schlosser](metallbauer-in.md) or [Lehrling](../../reference/glossary.md#lehrling) — both words are current.

## Not Zimmerer, and not Holzmechaniker

English flattens three distinct German occupations into "carpenter":

| Occupation | System | What it is |
|---|---|---|
| **Tischler/Schreiner** | Handwerk, Anlage A | Furniture, interiors, windows, doors — *joiner/cabinetmaker* |
| **[Zimmerer/Zimmerin](zimmerer-zimmerin.md)** | Handwerk, Anlage A | **Structural** timber: roofs, timber frame, Holzbau — *carpenter* |
| **Holzmechaniker/in** | **Industrie, IHK** | Series production in the furniture and wood-products industry |

Holzmechaniker is the industrial twin, making this the **fourth Handwerk/Industrie pairing**
in the repo after the electrical, metal and Kfz cases — same pattern: factory employment,
tariff pay, no right to open a workshop.

## What the job involves

Bespoke furniture and fitted interiors, kitchens, interior fit-out (**Innenausbau**), windows
and doors, staircases, shopfitting (**Ladenbau**) and exhibition stands (**Messebau**) — then
installing the result at the customer's premises.

The workshop is more industrial than the word "joinery" suggests: **panel saws, edge banders
and CNC nesting routers are standard**, and design work runs through CAD/CAM. A modern
Tischler who cannot work from a CAD model and run a CNC is limited to the small end of the
trade.

**Occupational health is a genuine issue here.** Hardwood dust — beech and oak especially —
is a recognised carcinogen with strict exposure limits under TRGS 553, and the trade has a
significant record of serious machine injuries on saws and spindle moulders. Extraction,
guarding and training are not paperwork.

## Qualification route

- **3 years dual**, Betrieb plus Berufsschule, **Handwerkskammer** exam
- **Entry:** formally open; Hauptschul- or mittlerer Schulabschluss typical
- **Paid throughout:** roughly €900–1,250/month gross, with wide regional variation
- **Ends with** the **Gesellenprüfung**, including a **Gesellenstück** — a piece you design
  and build yourself, still a living tradition in this trade
- **Then:** the **Tischlermeister/Schreinermeister** for business ownership, or
  **Restaurator im Handwerk** for historic-building and furniture restoration, a recognised
  and well-regarded specialisation

## Pay

Handwerk levels, at the lower end of the trades in this repo — **market estimates**, not
tariff-verified:

| Stage | Gross/month |
|---|---|
| Geselle, newly qualified | €2,600 – €3,100 |
| Geselle, experienced | €3,100 – €3,800 |
| CNC / Arbeitsvorbereitung, Ladenbau | €3,400 – €4,200 |
| Meister (employed) | €3,900 – €4,900 |
| Meister with own workshop | Highly variable; bespoke interiors are the profitable end |

As elsewhere in the Handwerk, **ownership is the earnings lever** rather than seniority.

## Demand — with the sector caveat stated plainly

Traditional furniture-making as a livelihood was largely destroyed by industrial production
decades ago. Nobody makes a living building wardrobes to compete with a factory.

What survives, and is genuinely in demand, is work industry cannot do cheaply: **bespoke
fitted interiors, kitchens, shopfitting, exhibition build, windows and doors, restoration,
and renovation work** on buildings whose dimensions are never standard. Renovation demand and
window replacement driven by energy retrofits both help.

The practical reading matches [Werkzeugbau](../industrial/werkzeugmechaniker-in.md): the
durable work is the work that has to be measured on site and made once. Choose employers who
do that, not those competing with factories.

## Foreign candidates

1. **To be employed:** no recognition legally required.
2. **To be self-employed:** Handwerksrolle entry, normally the **Meisterbrief**, or an
   equivalence decision, an **Ausnahmebewilligung (§8 HwO)**, or an employed Betriebsleiter.
   EU/EEA nationals use §9 HwO.
3. **Equivalence** sits with the **Handwerkskammer** under the BQFG.
4. **Language: B2.** Like [Metallbauer](metallbauer-in.md), you are measuring and discussing
   changes in customers' homes, which needs more language than workshop work alone.
5. **Visa:** skilled-worker route with recognition, or **§16a** to train here.

## Pitfalls

- **Search both Tischler and Schreiner**, or you will miss half the market.
- **Do not apply for Zimmerer roles expecting this trade**, or vice versa — different
  occupations, different Ausbildung.
- **CNC and CAD skills separate the career paths** within the trade.
- **Wood dust and machine safety** are long-term health questions worth taking seriously
  early.
- **Employment needs no recognition; self-employment does.** The standard Anlage A split.
