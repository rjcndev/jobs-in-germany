# Hospitality (Gastgewerbe)

No hospitality occupation is regulated or title-protected. Anyone may cook or work a
restaurant floor. Qualifications matter for two things only: **pay grading** and **visas**.

## The 2022 restructuring

The Gastgewerbe training occupations were overhauled on **1 August 2022**. Old titles
remain on people's certificates and in job ads:

| Since 2022 | Replaced | Length |
|---|---|---|
| Fachkraft für Gastronomie | Fachkraft im Gastgewerbe | 2 years |
| Fachmann/-frau für Restaurants und Veranstaltungsgastronomie | Restaurantfachmann/-frau | 3 years |
| Fachmann/-frau für Systemgastronomie | (renamed) | 3 years |
| Hotelfachmann/-frau | (revised) | 3 years |
| Hotelkaufmann/-frau | (revised) | 3 years |
| Koch/Köchin | (revised) | 3 years |

The 2-year Fachkraft qualification can be topped up to a 3-year one.

## Sector-wide facts

- **Pay floor:** the statutory **Mindestlohn** binds much of the sector. It rose to
  **€13.90/hour on 1 January 2026**, with a further increase scheduled for 2027. The
  Mindestlohnkommission revises it on a fixed cycle — check the current figure.
- **Collective agreements** are regional **DEHOGA** tariffs, and coverage is patchy.
- **Tips (Trinkgeld)** paid to employees are **tax-free without limit** under §3 Nr. 51
  EStG. Service charges routed through the employer are not the same thing.
- **Working time:** the Arbeitszeitgesetz applies in full — 8 hours standard, 10 maximum
  with compensation, 11 hours' rest between shifts. Breaches are common and unlawful;
  **Teildienst** (split shifts) is legal and widespread.
- **Turnover** is the highest of any German sector, and staffing never recovered from the
  pandemic exodus.
