# Hotelfachmann / Hotelfachfrau (Hotel Specialist)

> The generalist hotel qualification: reception, housekeeping, F&B service and
> administration. Broader than it sounds, and the usual stepping stone into hotel
> management.

| | |
|---|---|
| **Official title** | Hotelfachmann / Hotelfachfrau |
| **Protected title** | **No** |
| **Category** | hospitality |
| **Typical qualification** | Duale Ausbildung, 3 years, IHK exam |
| **Regulated** | No |
| **Last reviewed** | 2026-09 |

## What the job involves

Rotation through the hotel's departments: **Rezeption** (check-in, reservations, property
management systems), **Housekeeping** (room supervision, standards), **Service** (restaurant
and bar), **Bankett** (events), and back-office work in reservations or revenue.

Distinguish it from its neighbours: **Hotelkaufmann/-frau** is the commercial-administrative
variant (accounting, purchasing, marketing), while **Fachmann/-frau für Restaurants und
Veranstaltungsgastronomie** is the service-and-events specialist. Hotelfachleute are the
generalists, which is exactly why they move into management.

## Qualification route

- **3 years dual**, revised in the **2022 Gastgewerbe restructuring**
- **Entry:** formally open; mittlerer Schulabschluss typical
- **Paid throughout:** roughly €1,000–1,200/month gross by year
- **Ends with** the IHK Abschlussprüfung
- **Afterwards:** **Hotelmeister/in**, **Fachwirt/in im Gastgewerbe**, or a hospitality
  management degree — the latter is the common route into General Manager roles,
  especially in international chains

## Pay

| Stage | Gross/month |
|---|---|
| Newly qualified | €2,300 – €2,900 |
| Experienced, e.g. shift lead at reception | €2,900 – €3,500 |
| Department head (Rezeptionsleitung, Housekeeping-Leitung) | €3,400 – €4,300 |
| Hotel management | €4,500 – €8,000+, heavily dependent on property |

Chains (Marriott, Accor, Hilton franchises) pay more predictably and offer structured
progression and international transfers; independent and family-run hotels pay less but
give broader responsibility earlier. City business hotels pay above resort and leisure
properties.

Much of the sector sits near the **Mindestlohn** at entry level — €13.90/hour from January
2026 — which compresses junior pay regardless of qualification.

## Demand

High, particularly at reception and in housekeeping supervision. The staffing shortfall
that followed the pandemic has not closed, and hotels compete for the same small pool of
trained people. Multilingual candidates are actively sought.

## Foreign candidates

1. **To work:** nothing required — the occupation is unregulated.
2. **For the skilled-worker visa:** you need a **Gleichwertigkeitsfeststellung** of your
   foreign hospitality qualification through **IHK FOSA**. As with cooking, experience
   alone does not satisfy the immigration authorities.
3. **No EU Blue Card** at ordinary pay levels; hotel management salaries at larger
   properties can clear the threshold.
4. **Westbalkanregelung (§26(2) BeschV)** applies here too and is widely used in the sector.
5. **Ausbildung visa (§16a)** to train in Germany, needing B1–B2 for Berufsschule.
6. **Language:** **B1–B2**. Uniquely in this repo, **English is a genuine asset rather than
   a substitute** — international city hotels value it highly, but German remains necessary
   for German guests, suppliers, colleagues and paperwork.

## Pitfalls

- **Housekeeping is frequently outsourced** to service contractors at minimum wage and
  piece rates. A "hotel job" via an agency is a materially different job from direct
  employment; check who your contract is actually with.
- **Split shifts and weekend work** are structural, not occasional.
- **Seasonal contracts** dominate in resort regions. Check the befristet terms and any
  deduction for staff accommodation.
- **Chain vs. independent** is the biggest fork in the career. Decide deliberately rather
  than drifting.
- **Title overlap:** Hotelfachmann, Hotelkaufmann and the restaurant qualifications are
  distinct certificates. Ads conflate them; recognition procedures do not.
