# Wirtschaftsprüfer/in (Statutory Auditor)

> A **reserved activity** under the WPO — only a Wirtschaftsprüfer may sign a statutory audit
> opinion — behind an examination with a worse pass rate than the
> [Steuerberater](steuerfachangestellte-r.md) one. It sits at the top of the same ladder this
> repo already describes from the bottom, and it is the highest-paying non-medical
> qualification here.

| **Category** | commercial |

## The reserved activity

German companies above defined size thresholds must have their annual accounts audited, and
the **Bestätigungsvermerk** — the audit opinion — may only be issued by a Wirtschaftsprüfer or
a registered audit firm.

This is a genuine legal monopoly of the same kind as the
[architect's Bauvorlageberechtigung](../engineering/architekt-in.md) and the
**notary's authentication**, and it is what underwrites the profession's
economics. A WP may also do everything a **Steuerberater** may do — tax advice and
representation — which makes the qualification strictly broader than the one below it.

## The route, and the examination

1. **A degree** — economics, business, law, or increasingly any subject with the required
   content. Many candidates take an integrated Master's designed to give exemptions.
2. **Practical experience** — a period of audit practice, generally three years, shortened in
   defined cases.
3. **The WP-Examen.** Written papers in auditing, business administration and economics,
   business law and tax, plus an oral examination.

**The pass rate is the profession's defining fact.** Roughly half of candidates fail at each
attempt, and the proportion who pass everything first time is far lower. Candidates typically
prepare for a year or more alongside full-time work, and repeat attempts are normal rather
than shameful. Many people take the **Steuerberater** examination first — itself notoriously
hard — and enter through the partial-exemption route.

The honest framing: **this is a multi-year commitment with a real probability of not
succeeding**, undertaken during the most demanding years of an audit career. Firms fund and
support it because they need the qualification; the cost to you is time and attempts.

## What changed after Wirecard

The collapse of Wirecard in 2020 produced the most significant tightening of audit regulation
in decades, through the **Finanzmarktintegritätsstärkungsgesetz (FISG)** and related reform:

- **Mandatory audit firm rotation** for public-interest entities, on a shorter cycle
- **Separation of audit and consulting** for those clients — an audit firm may no longer sell
  most advisory services to a company it audits
- **Increased liability** for auditors, with the statutory caps substantially raised
- **Stronger public oversight** through the audit oversight body, with expanded inspection
  and enforcement powers
- **Enhanced internal controls and documentation** obligations

For someone entering the profession, the practical effect is more documentation, more
supervision, more rotation of client portfolios, and a heightened personal-liability
environment. It has also made the qualification more valuable, since the supply of qualified
auditors did not grow.

## Where the work is

| Employer | Character |
|---|---|
| **The large international firms** | Listed and multinational audits, structured progression, well-defined exam support, long hours |
| **Mid-tier and national firms** | Mittelstand audits, broader client contact earlier, more varied work |
| **Small WP/StB practices** | Audit alongside tax advice; closest to the [Steuerberater](steuerfachangestellte-r.md) world |
| **Industry** | Internal audit, group accounting, controlling — a very common exit, and a good one |
| **Public sector** | Rechnungshöfe and public audit bodies |

## Pay

**Market estimate**, but well documented by industry surveys.

| Stage | Gross/month |
|---|---|
| Audit assistant, pre-exam | €4,000 – €5,000 |
| Senior, pre-exam | €5,000 – €6,000 |
| **Newly qualified WP** | **€6,500 – €8,500** |
| Manager / Senior Manager | €8,000 – €11,000 |
| Partner | Not comparable — a profit share |
| Industry exit (group accounting, internal audit) | €6,000 – €9,000, with better hours |

**The hours are the trade.** Busy season — roughly January to April for calendar-year clients
— involves sustained overtime that is a well-known feature of the profession. Much of the
compensation is a payment for that, and the industry exit route exists precisely because many
people take the qualification and then leave for a life.

Also relevant: **WPK membership** is compulsory, and pension arrangements often run through
the [Steuerberater Versorgungswerk](../../reference/versorgungswerke.md) rather than a
dedicated one — this is the partial case the Versorgungswerke file flags. Confirm with the
chamber in your state.

## Demand and outlook

**Strong, and constrained by the exam.** Audit volume is set by law, rotation increases
switching, and the number of qualified auditors has not kept pace — the profession openly
discusses a succession problem as older WPs retire and fewer candidates sit the examination.

That has pushed firms to compete harder on pay, exam support and — belatedly — working
conditions. It has also increased the value of the qualification to anyone who obtains it.

Direction of travel: **sustainability assurance**. Auditing non-financial and sustainability
reporting is a growing statutory field, and it is the clearest new specialism for people
entering now.

## Foreign candidates

1. **Recognition is mandatory** to use the title or sign audits.
2. **EU/EEA auditors** have a defined route: an **aptitude test** covering German law, rather
   than the full examination. This is a substantial advantage and is the reason to establish
   your status early.
3. **Third-country auditors** face the full requirements; third-country audit firms auditing
   German-listed companies register separately with the oversight body.
4. **Foreign professional qualifications — ACCA, CPA, Chartered Accountant — do not confer
   the German title**, whatever their standing elsewhere. They are respected by employers and
   irrelevant to the reserved activity.
5. **Language: C1, without qualification.** The examination is in German, the audit opinion is
   a German legal document, and client discussions are legally consequential.
6. **Visa:** **§18b**, with the **Blue Card** comfortably reachable — this is one of the
   easier professions in this repo for an immigration route, and one of the harder ones for
   the qualification.

## Pitfalls

- **Establish the EU aptitude-test route if you qualify for it.** The difference between it
  and the full WP-Examen is years.
- **Budget for failure.** Plan the attempts, the study leave and the funding.
- **Rotation moves your clients**, which is good for independence and disruptive for
  specialisation.
- **Liability is personal and has been raised.** Understand what the firm's insurance covers.
- **The industry exit is not a defeat.** It is the majority outcome and it pays well.
