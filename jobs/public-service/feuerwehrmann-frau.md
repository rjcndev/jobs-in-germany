# Feuerwehrmann / Feuerwehrfrau (Firefighter)

> Mostly a rescue service, mostly staffed by volunteers, and professionally entered only
> **after** you already hold a trade qualification.

| | |
|---|---|
| **Official title** | Ranks — Brandmeister/in (mittlerer Dienst), Brandoberinspektor/in (gehobener) |
| **Protected title** | Yes in the professional service — Beamtenrecht |
| **Category** | public-service |
| **Typical qualification** | **A completed Ausbildung or degree first**, then a paid Vorbereitungsdienst |
| **Status** | **Beamter/Beamtin** in the Berufsfeuerwehr — see [Beamte vs. Angestellte](../../reference/beamte-vs-angestellte.md) |
| **Last reviewed** | 2026-09 |

## Germany's fire cover is a volunteer system

This surprises almost everyone from outside Germany. Roughly **a million volunteers** staff
the **Freiwillige Feuerwehr** against some tens of thousands of professionals. Most
municipalities have no professional brigade at all.

| Type | Who | Status |
|---|---|---|
| **Freiwillige Feuerwehr** | The large majority of German fire cover | Volunteers; expense allowances, statutory release from work, accident cover |
| **Berufsfeuerwehr** | Required only in larger cities — the threshold is set by each state's Brandschutzgesetz, commonly around 100,000 inhabitants | **Beamte** |
| **Werkfeuerwehr** | Chemical plants, refineries, airports, large industrial sites | **Employees**, often under industrial tariffs — frequently paid well |

**For a foreign resident, the Freiwillige Feuerwehr is genuinely open.** It needs no
citizenship, no qualification and no German-born network, it trains you, and it is one of
the more effective routes into a German community. It is also, for some, a first step toward
the professional service.

## You need a trade before you need the fire service

The defining entry rule of the **mittlerer feuerwehrtechnischer Dienst**: you must already
hold a **completed Ausbildung**, normally in a craft or technical trade — electrical,
plumbing and heating, metalwork, vehicle mechatronics, carpentry and similar. Only then are
you appointed a **Brandmeisteranwärter/in** for roughly **18 months** of paid training.

This makes the fire service a second career built on a first one, and it connects directly
to the [skilled trades](../skilled-trades/README.md) and [industrial](../industrial/README.md)
files: a brigade needs people who can cut, pump, climb, weld and fix things under pressure.

- **gehobener Dienst:** a **Bachelor**, usually engineering, plus a two-year
  Vorbereitungsdienst → Brandoberinspektor/in
- **höherer Dienst:** a Master or Diplom in engineering plus a Referendariat →
  Branddirektor/in

## Most of the work is medical

Typically **the large majority of Berufsfeuerwehr call-outs are rescue service, not fires.**
Many city brigades run the local Rettungsdienst, and most require or strongly prefer the
**[Notfallsanitäter](../healthcare/notfallsanitaeter-in.md)** qualification — or at minimum
Rettungssanitäter — alongside the fire training.

That closes a loop the paramedic file already opens: **the Berufsfeuerwehr is the route by
which a Notfallsanitäter becomes a Beamter**, with the pay and pension that brings. If you
are weighing the two careers, they overlap far more than the job titles suggest.

## The selection gates

- **G26.3 Atemschutztauglichkeit** — the occupational medical for breathing-apparatus work.
  Strict, repeated throughout your career, and a common disqualifier. Losing it can end
  operational service.
- **Sporttest**, including **swimming** — non-swimmers are excluded outright
- **Confined spaces and heights** are tested, not asked about
- **Driving licence** — C/CE is usually required or funded during training
- **Age limits** at appointment, set per Bundesland
- **Citizenship** — §7 BeamtStG. Fire services are generally more open to EU nationals than
  [police](polizist-in.md), but it varies by state and city. Check before applying.

## Conditions

**24-hour shifts** are standard, followed by multiple days off — a pattern people either
suit or do not. Nights, weekends and holidays are ordinary working time.

Two long-term risks deserve naming. **Occupational cancer risk from combustion products** is
now well recognised in fire services internationally, and decontamination discipline after
fires is a live and improving practice rather than a solved problem. And the
**psychological load** of repeated trauma exposure is the same issue the
[paramedic file](../healthcare/notfallsanitaeter-in.md) raises — ask what supervision a
brigade actually provides.

As a Beamter you have **no right to strike**.

## Pay

**Besoldung — state law, NRW figures, valid 01.04.2026 – 28.02.2027**, verified 2026-09.
They do **not** transfer between Bundesländer:

| Stage | Grade | Gross/month |
|---|---|---|
| Brandmeisteranwärter/in (training) | AW A 7 | **€1,560** |
| Brandmeister/in | A 7 – A 8 | €3,216 – €4,100 |
| Oberbrandmeister/in | A 9 | €3,438 – €4,295 |
| Gehobener Dienst, Brandoberinspektor/in | A 10 – A 11 | €3,694 – €5,257 |
| Senior posts | A 12 – A 13 | €4,505 – €6,381 |

Plus **Feuerwehrzulage** and supplements for night, weekend and holiday duty, which are
substantial on a 24-hour shift pattern. Remember Beamte gross is **not comparable** to an
employee's — see [Beamte vs. Angestellte](../../reference/beamte-vs-angestellte.md).

**Werkfeuerwehr** pays on industrial tariffs instead, with no Beamtenstatus but often
strong terms, especially in chemicals and aviation.

## Demand

Steady and rising as cities expand professional cover and rescue-service volume grows. The
harder problem is the **volunteer** system: recruitment has fallen with commuting, shift work
and rural depopulation, and it is a genuine civil-protection concern rather than a staffing
inconvenience.

## For non-German candidates

1. **The volunteer service is open now** — no citizenship, no qualification.
2. **The professional service needs a German-recognised trade or degree first.** Your
   existing trade may be recognisable through the
   [Handwerkskammer or IHK FOSA](../../reference/visa-routes.md) — that is the step to take
   before anything fire-related.
3. **No foreign firefighting qualification is recognised.** Fire training happens inside the
   German Vorbereitungsdienst, whatever your experience.
4. **Citizenship** — see above; more open than policing, but check.
5. **Language: C1.** Radio traffic, incident command and casualty handover are time-critical
   and unforgiving, and the written incident documentation is evidential.

## Pitfalls

- **Applying without a trade.** The mittlerer Dienst will not take you.
- **The G26.3 medical**, at entry and for the rest of your career.
- **Expecting fires.** The job is mostly medical calls; if that is not what you want, it is
  the wrong career.
- **24-hour shifts** suit some lives and wreck others — be honest with yourself early.
- **Werkfeuerwehr vs Berufsfeuerwehr** are different employment worlds; compare properly.
